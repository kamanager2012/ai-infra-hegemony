## 1. Executive judgment: S2 remains provisional, with mixed physical-layer signals

The November evidence supports a **simultaneous** set of observations:

1. **Hyperscaler capital intensity remains elevated**, but reported CapEx, cash property additions, cash flow, guidance, and finance-lease principal are distinct accounting identities. Their published values cannot be added to manufacture a common “four-cloud AI hardware CapEx” number.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
2. **NVIDIA supplier and capacity commitments increased** from US$279B to **US$390B** as of October 25, largely from multi-year memory procurement. This indicates reservation intensity, not deployed capacity or a supplier-level payment allocation. A separate November 17 announcement describes substrate and packaging expansion agreements with differentiated commissioning dates.[^7][^8]
3. **Power permitting and interconnection remain relevant**, but the AWS/Talen Susquehanna denial occurred on **November 1, 2024**, not in November 2026. Talen subsequently changed the PPA structure and voluntarily dismissed its appeal in **January 2026**. It is an illustrative historical case with a subsequent adaptation, **not a new Q4 2026 regulatory veto**.[^5][^6]
4. **HBM4 contract-based reservation and weakness in certain conventional memory channels coexist.** TrendForce on November 18 forecasts NAND oversupply during 2027 and reports weak NAND spot trade; this contradicts an undifferentiated “all memory prices have surged” account.[^10][^11][^12]
5. **Remote compute access has become a regulatory control dimension.** A BIS interim final rule announced November 16 addresses certain overseas data centers and remote advanced-compute service for specified restricted counterparties. Scope, exemptions, legal effect and implementation must be read from the actual rule: the announcement is **not evidence of a universal ban on cross-border cloud computing**.[^9]

**Scenario read:** **S2 — Baseline Concentration** remains the best *provisional qualitative fit*. Some scarce layers may become more tightly contracted while others gain headroom. An S3 claim requires durable, verified multi-layer allocation concentration; an S4 claim requires independent deterioration in workload economics and capital clearing across actors. Neither follows from one commitment number or a memory-price forecast.

## 4. NVIDIA: multi-year supply reservation and actual capacity must be separated

NVIDIA's fiscal Q3 ended **2026-10-25**; its 2026-11-17 result release reported revenue of **US$115.8B**, Data Center revenue **US$107.6B** and Vera Rubin NVL72 shipments.[^16] These operations precede Huang's November 11 leadership transition, despite the later publication date. Succession-related causal interpretations belong in the dated [Special Chapter 33](en/publication/part-IV-ch33-founder-succession.md).

The contemporaneous CFO commentary discloses **US$390B aggregate supply/capacity commitments**, up from **US$279B** in the prior quarterly disclosure, mainly relating to multi-year memory supply procurement.[^7] It also discusses **US$102B** of cloud-service agreements and data-center lease commitments combined. **The categories may overlap in financial planning and must not be treated as current cash expense, current energized compute, or an official AI infrastructure demand total.**

A separate first-party 2026-11-17 announcement describes:
- Kinsus plans for dedicated ABF substrate capacity **>3.5×** current production at one site, with additional output targeted for **2027 H2 / 2028 Q3**;
- multi-year Amkor and SPIL advanced packaging expansions and related prepayments;
- broader TSMC and manufacturing-partner support for Vera Rubin and later products.[^8]

These are **named counterparty plans/commitments with future ramp dates**, not proof that the stated capacity is already qualified, delivered and fully utilized. Their public disclosure strengthens the case that supplier orchestration extends beyond GPU die pricing; it does **not** demonstrate the distribution of the US$390B by supplier or production layer.

**Next test for H3/H15:** Track (i) contractual quantity and cancellability; (ii) customer- and product-qualified throughput; (iii) shipment/acceptance milestones; (iv) alternative-buyer access and observed lead times; (v) utilization and cash realization. Without these, S3 remains a hypothesis rather than a measured allocation outcome.

## 5. Power and interconnection: correct chronology, retain the mechanism

The official **2024-11-01** FERC action concerning Susquehanna's proposed nonconforming interconnection-service-agreement changes was a rejection **without prejudice** on the record then presented.[^5] The source did not establish a universal prohibition of all behind-the-meter colocations or all nuclear PPAs.

Talen's subsequent SEC description reports: the AWS PPA was amended in **June 2025**, moving toward a **front-of-the-meter** arrangement; interconnection rights were addressed during 2025; the original ISA appeal was **voluntarily dismissed in January 2026** and the matter closed.[^6]

**What this supports:** Contracted or pre-reserved capacity, legally permitted interconnection, energized site power and utilized compute are separate states:

\[
\text{Signed / reserved MW} \ne \text{Approved MW} \ne \text{Energized MW} \ne \text{Utilized AI load}
\]

**What it does not support:** A fresh 2026 FERC “denial,” a claim that any PPA guarantees power, or a static assumption that rejection leaves a project permanently dead. This case also demonstrates **contract redesign as a response to regulatory friction**.

A valid H6 time-to-power indicator must specify location, utility/ISO jurisdiction, grid queue, permitting phase, construction, firm-power delivery date and qualified load factor. Supplier announcements of future **IT-GW design capacity** are not observed firm energized utility MW.

## 6. Reasoning, inference and the limits of stack-displacement inference

OpenAI o1's 2024 launch proves the age of the reasoning-model category, **not a new September 2026 hardware event**.[^13] It remains reasonable to study inference growth, runtime optimization, KV-cache handling and the value of HBM bandwidth, yet physical requirements differ sharply across:
- **Prefill** vs **decode** at particular sequence lengths and batch sizes;
- **Single-accelerator serving** vs tensor-/expert-parallel multi-accelerator serving;
- **MoE / routing / all-to-all** traffic and scale-out replication;
- **Training** vs inference and quality-adjusted long reasoning.

A proposed inference-stack substitution must show a **matched production workload** and include p95/p99 latency, tokens per second, effective quality, batch mix, memory requirements, reliability, developer migration time, joules per unit, fully loaded system TCO and the **cost per verified useful workflow**. Benchmark peak throughput alone is inadequate.

**Scenario link:** Cloud ASIC and open runtime substitution can shift value capture toward S1 if *full-stack deployed economics* improve. Memory intensity alone does not prove NVLink/InfiniBand becomes irrelevant or that NVIDIA’s software switching cost collapses.

## 7. HBM4, NAND and downstream economics: divergence is the finding

TrendForce's 2026-11-04 DRAM bulletin describes selected HBM4 2027 volumes as already negotiated or sold out and an increased role for **long-term agreements**.[^10] This supports tighter **contract-level reservation** for particular suppliers/products, not a universal forecast that all DRAM remains tight forever.

TrendForce's **2026-11-18** NAND bulletin expects consumer/spec demand downgrades and Chinese CSP order cuts, with a transition toward **NAND oversupply in 2027** and some contract prices declining sooner than previously forecast.[^11] Its Nov 18 spot report describes weak DRAM buying, relatively steady branded DDR5 spot prices and a >20% NAND spot/contract gap.[^12]

Together these observations are more informative than an indiscriminate "AI caused all chips to double in price" statement:

- **HBM4:** contracting intensity and qualification can constrain product-specific supply;
- **Server DRAM/RDIMM:** contract and spot channels may diverge by capacity;
- **Consumer DDR:** inventory and affordability can suppress short-term demand even when manufacturers prioritize HBM;
- **Enterprise SSD / NAND:** inventory cycles, Chinese and North American CSP procurement and QLC ramp can flip the sign of a shortage;
- **Phone/PC:** BOM pass-through, reductions in memory/storage configuration and unit volumes must be measured **by model, region and calendar quarter**, not claimed from out-of-period 2024 devices.

**Consequent hypothesis correction:** The original "Non-AI Residual Capacity" concept still matters, but one layer's HBM reservation cannot determine the price or quantity sign of another layer's NAND market. Supply response and demand elasticity can create *shortage in one layer and excess in another*.

## 8. New access dimension: overseas remote-compute regulation

BIS announced an interim final rule on **2026-11-16** addressing certain overseas data centers supplying advanced computing remotely to specified restricted counterparts and ownership/control categories.[^9] It extends the analytical problem from *physical shipment* to *who can lawfully obtain remote use of that computing capacity*. Do not turn an IFR notice into a generalized ban: jurisdiction, thresholds, end users, exceptions, compliance timing and downstream guidance must be checked against the operative rule.

Add a **Regulatory-Access Gate (RAG)** to the existing physical capacity and system-stack analysis; it is **a monitoring dimension, not a fifth scenario**:

\[
\text{Usable Compute Access} = \min\{\text{Qualified Physical Delivery},\ \text{Energized / Operable Capacity},\ \text{Lawful Customer Access}\}
\]

The \(\min\) formulation is a **conceptual bottleneck relation**, not an arithmetic equality across dissimilar units. For an actual model, convert all three to the **same customer-workload-equivalent units and time window**.

Operationally track provider, facility jurisdiction, beneficial owner/customer category, applicable authorization and compliance state; do not infer that the entire Chinese cloud sector or every third-country data center is blocked. The interaction maps to **H11 (regulatory environment), H13/H19 (parallel stack/closure), H3 (effective scarcity access), and H7 (viability of alternative cloud stacks)**.

| **H14/H18 ROI and seller-assisted financing** | Quarterly FCF divergence and commitments increase monitoring need | No verified broad impairment, utilization collapse or credit clearing |
| **H16 downstream demand** | Conventional NAND and consumer storage demand can soften despite HBM reservation | Spot ≠ contract ≠ retail SKU; cross-layer generalization forbidden |

**Variables not independently rescored in this cycle:** H2, H4, H12, H17 and any H not named above. The 19-variable taxonomy remains unchanged. A separate historical study would be required to convert the evidence here into calibrated S1–S4 likelihoods.

**Table A.4 — Scenario signals and falsification**

| Scenario | Strengthening signal | Evidence that would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 Competitive Expansion** | Verified qualified supply ramp, lower lead times, multi-stack workload substitution, improving buyer options | Multi-layer access stays constrained despite announced expansions |
| **S2 Baseline Concentration** | High growth, dominant system coordination and real but partial alternatives coexist | Persistent broad shift in multi-layer access (S1/S3) or financial clearing (S4) |
| **S3 Resource Hegemony** | Multiple independent scarce layers become persistently pre-reserved, buyer alternatives weaken and monetization stays healthy | Supply elasticity, qualification and portable workloads restore access |
| **S4 Capital Hard Landing** | Falling utilization/clearing prices plus independent financing deterioration and commitment cancellations across actors | Sustained cash generation, renewal quality, utilization and credible financed demand |

**Decision as of 2026-11-19:** **S2 remains provisional**. HBM4 LTA intensity may pull toward S3 in selected layers, while forecast 2027 NAND oversupply is a counter-signal against universal scarcity. The framework must retain this non-uniformity instead of forcing all observations into a single heroic narrative.

## 10. Research-quality gates for the next edition

Before assigning a new scenario state:
1. Obtain first-party *matched-period* utilization and workload monetization, rather than assuming GPU shipped equals GPU productively used.
2. Compare company-wide CapEx with company-specific cash flows and lease accounting; do not identify AI-hardware-only spend without a denominator.
3. Follow supplier **signed / funded / qualified / energized / delivered** states independently.
4. Cross-check institutional memory forecasts against observed spot/contract prices, inventory and 2027 ramp outcomes.
5. Evaluate remote-access compliance against the operative BIS rule and later changes, not an unqualified headline.
6. Treat [Special Chapter 33](en/publication/part-IV-ch33-founder-succession.md) as post-departure governance research; 2026 Q3 operations ended **before** the November resignation.
7. Preserve the English canonical text, synchronize Chinese, and update the public reader **only after** evidence and numerical checks.
