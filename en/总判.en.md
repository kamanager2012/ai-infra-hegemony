# Judgment: The Age of AI Infrastructure Hegemony

The numbers are current. What follows from them is a hypothesis from the present, not a claim that it already happened.
Profit and cash: [Volume 1](vol-01.en.md). TSMC and ASICs: [Volume 4](vol-04.md). Memory residual: [Volume 5](vol-05.md). Four-cloud scope: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). Antitrust: [Volume 11](vol-11.md). Chinese originals remain the source of truth where an English file is not yet up.

## 1. Four corners

NVIDIA is athlete, referee, and coach at once, and it also trains models.

- Athlete: GPUs, cabinets, interconnect. FY26 revenue $215,938M; data center $193,737M; gross margin 71.1% (H20 inventory charge; Q4 back to 75.0%); operating income $130,387M; net income $120,067M (back-calculated net margin 55.6%); free cash $96,575M; R&D $18,497M. Q1 FY27 operating income $53,536M. Net income $58,321M includes $15.9B of equity marks; bid for capacity against operating income.
- Referee: CUDA, NVLink, cabinet standard.
- Coach: Hopper to Blackwell to Rubin; HBM / CoWoS / NVLink mix.
- Model player: Nemotron / NeMo / NIM. The revenue bulk is still on cabinets.

The sales unit moved from the chip to the cabinet.

## 2. A margin monopoly, not a share monopoly

Intel in 1999: revenue $29,389M, net income $7,314M, gross margin 59.7%. That was CPU share.

Today is one year of free cash about $96.6B and one quarter of operating income about $53.5B. Sellers are already rich: TSMC 2025 gross margin 59.9%; Hynix operating margin 49%. No buyer, selling cabinets, can outbid 71%–75%. Apple, AMD, Qualcomm, Huawei are buyers. Comparing sticker prices is meaningless.

10-K language: prepaid manufacturing and capacity agreements. Supply commitments $95.2B at FY26 year-end, $119.0B at Q1 FY27 end, up $23.8B in three months, most payable through FY2027. Official text locks inventory and capacity. It does not split TSMC or CoWoS. Some agreements can be rescheduled.

Microsoft FY25 net income $101,832M; PPE additions $64,551M. The clouds are not poor. That hundred billion builds halls. NVIDIA's hundred billion is earned selling the scarce slice itself. Prepayments are reproduction.

Hypothesis from here: while the margin holds, commitments keep rising. Anyone else who wants the same material pays more, or does not get it. TSMC has no financial reason to offend the first customer for the second.

## 3. The increment is HPC. Booked 19% is not a safe distance

TSMC 2025: shipments 15,022 thousand 12-inch equivalents (annual report writes 15.0 million); capacity above 17 million. 2026 outlook 16–17 million is an outlook.

Wafer **revenue** (not units): 3nm 24%, 5nm 36%, 7nm 14%, 7nm and below 74%. Platforms: HPC 58% (+48%), smartphone 29%. First customer 19% (up from 12%), second 17% (down from 22%). Jensen has said they are already the largest customer. The 20-F does not name names.

Packaging and test revenue about 8% in 2024, a bit over 10% in 2025. C.C. Wei, 4Q24: CoWoS tight / cannot meet demand. 2Q26: limits customers' growth. "Sold out through 2026" at the shareholders' meeting has no official transcript we could open.

TrendForce 2026 Q1 foundry revenue: TSMC 72%, Samsung 6.5%, SMIC 5.1%. Public high-end AI logic volume is still mostly TSMC, with Samsung as a parallel line.

Hypothesis from here: what is being opened is HPC and packaging. Apple locks phone logic. Booked 19% is not a safe distance. Occupy the increment and next year's slots belong to the first name.

## 4. Residual bits and wafer math

Official cabinet: 72 GPUs, 36 Grace, 13.4 TB HBM, 17 TB LPDDR. Two B200s about 800 mm² at reticle limit (Electronic Design / IEEE Spectrum). H100 814 mm² (NVIDIA blog).

10,000 cabinets: 134 PB HBM, 170 PB LPDDR.
30,000 cabinets: 402 PB HBM, 510 PB LPDDR.
50,000 cabinets: 670 PB HBM, 850 PB LPDDR.

TrendForce official note: 51% of 2026 DRAM bits are HBM+RDIMM. Memory is 47% of major CSP capex in 2026 and 68% in 2027. Micron 10-K: the same bits of HBM take more wafers. Hot Chips paraphrase: about 3× DDR5. Hynix: HBM revenue more than doubled in a year. Channel talk: 16G DDR5 from 450 to 1800. Channel talk is not a filing.

5.39 is a CoWoS wafers-per-cabinet estimate: 72 packages ÷ about 13.4 per wafer. Front-end logic is another line: 144 dies at 70% yield ≈ 2.9 wafers per cabinet. 835,000 cabinets = 4.5 million wafers ÷ 5.39 uses 30% of the whole-factory pool. Wrong pool. The binding line is CoWoS. Sell-side puts NVIDIA at about 60% / 595–630k wafers (Morgan Stanley / Mizuho). TSMC publishes no split. At 5.39, about 100,000 cabinets take more than half that packaging line. You do not need 800,000 cabinets to lock 3nm.

Hypothesis from here: makers shift wafers to HBM; consumer bits shrink. Server DDR5 is already residual, not a second river.

## 5. Who builds the cloud ASICs

Public next-gen parts are not 5nm plus commodity DDR5.

- Maia 100: Microsoft writes TSMC 5nm, CoWoS-S, HBM2E 64GB. Already not DDR5.
- Maia 200: Microsoft writes TSMC N3, HBM3e 216GB.
- TPU v5p / v6e / Ironwood: Google specs are HBM; Ironwood official 192GB HBM3E. Pages do not name TSMC or Broadcom.
- Inferentia1: DDR4 8GB. Inferentia2: HBM 32GB. The company itself moved from DDR to HBM.
- Trainium3: AWS writes first 3nm AI chip, HBM3e 144GB, does not name TSMC.
- Early MTIA: Meta writes TSMC 7/5nm + LPDDR5. From the 300 generation: Broadcom + HBM stacks, no 3nm named.

No firm says it is going back to 5nm plus commodity DDR5 as the inference mainstay. The direction is the opposite.

Samsung's $16.54B long contract (announced 22.76 trillion won, through 2033) is Tesla. Musk named the Taylor fab for AI6. 16k / 24k / 40k wafers per month are not in the announcement. SMIC at 5.1% cannot take Western leading-edge die.

Hypothesis from here: to beat today's cabinet you return to 3nm, HBM, and CoWoS, and you bid against 75% gross margin. Only a real retreat to 5nm with less packaging dodges the scarce slice. Public next-gen parts have not stopped.

## 6. China's stack is three layers, not one

The China market cannot save global cabinets, HBM, CoWoS, or CUDA. At home it can at most do phones. PCs and AI do not resolve. The layer is wrong.

CXMT's prospectus: 0 mentions of HBM. 350k wafers per month is not an announcement. YMTC writes 200+ layers and does not publish monthly wafer counts. Once the international price holds, makers sell first to the high bidder. Huawei's high price is a rent unpaid to NVIDIA inside the iron curtain. Allocation stops domestic bits from leaking out. It does not mean CXMT has been nursed to HBM.

Near gate: administrative lock. Far gate: everything that is not silicon. If domestic advanced packaging and HBM-class bits can already hold up a training cabinet, the administrative lock is enough to keep a closed loop. If not, the lock only keeps the phone layer.

## 7. Bundling and the five interrupts

Acting alone means handing over each year's new lines and packaging. To change allocation, bind three checks: prepayments large enough that the foundry cannot recognize only one buyer; software bound into one stack (UXL / oneAPI are embryos); memory longs that leave a line for commodity bits. Miss one check and you are still picked off one by one.

Five interrupts that would break the line: the West actually bundles; cloud ASICs take training, not just inference; power stops the buildout; the East stands up a second bid; a structural split or a forced-open CUDA.

None of the five is done. What is done: supply commitments to $119.0B; inquiries exist, complaints do not; France is near the end and has not issued a statement of objections; the EU sent questionnaires, cleared Run:ai unconditionally, and has no 101/102 case. 10% of annual turnover is a statutory ceiling. FY26 × 10% = $21.6B. That is not a fine. White House NSM-25, BIS, and DoD write advanced chips as national security. There is no order that says "therefore do not split." Hypothesis from here: a fine is a cost. A split is hard under national security.

## 8. The compute rent

FY26 data center $193.7B is the rent the world paid. Four-cloud capex maps to each firm's own statements: Microsoft FY25 PPE $64,551M; Amazon CY25 net $128,320M; Google CY25 $91,447M; Meta CY25 equipment $69,691M (company figure $72.22B). Mixed-year add: about $211.9–$213.9B. That is not "AI hardware $215B."

The application layer is squeezed. The back end collects rent. Phones and PCs that never buy a GPU still pay a memory spillover tax. The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down, and one firm writes the rules.

If those five interrupts are not done, the line walks past half of the scarce slice. The window is the few years before the next first-to-market node is locked by commitments.
