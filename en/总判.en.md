# Judgment: The Age of AI Infrastructure Hegemony

> **How to read this page:** this report does not write the future as fact. Already-observed and verifiable claims are **F (facts)**; causal transmission derived from industrial structure is **M (mechanisms)**; 2026–2036 resource-allocation and end-state claims are **H/S (hypotheses/scenarios)**. The common method is **F → H → M → S**: facts must be checked; hypotheses can be bold but must state their conditions; scenarios must be derivable from the stated variables.

The numbers are current. What follows from them is analysis.

## 1. Four corners

NVIDIA is simultaneously an athlete, referee, coach, and model vendor.

- **Athlete:** GPUs, full racks, and interconnect. FY26 revenue $215,938M; Data Center $193,737M; GAAP gross margin 71.1% after the H20 charge; operating income $130,387M; net income $120,067M; free cash flow $96,575M; R&D $18,497M. Q1 FY27 operating income was $53,536M. **Q2 FY27:** revenue $96,221M, Data Center $89,000M, GAAP gross margin 75.0%, operating income $63,734M; Vera Rubin had entered full production.
- **Referee:** CUDA, NVLink, and rack/system standards.
- **Coach:** Hopper → Blackwell → Rubin, and the HBM / CoWoS / NVLink mix.
- **Model player:** Nemotron / NeMo / NIM. The revenue bulk is still infrastructure.

The sales unit has moved upward from the chip to the full system.

## 2. Profit-pool control: excess value capture at the scarce layer, not a simple share monopoly

Intel in 1999 had revenue of $29,389M, net income of $7,314M, and gross margin of 59.7%. That was CPU share power.

Today NVIDIA has roughly $96.6B of annual free cash flow and its latest quarterly operating income has reached **$63.7B**. TSMC's 2025 gross margin was 59.9%; SK hynix's operating margin was 49%; NVIDIA has maintained a 71%–75% gross-margin band at the platform/system layer. These different business models cannot be collapsed into one "monopoly score." The report's real object is **value-capture power**: who controls the hardest-to-substitute scarce layers and can reserve the next generation of capacity early.

NVIDIA's filings describe prepaid manufacturing and capacity agreements. Supply commitments rose from $95.2B at FY26 year-end to $119.0B at Q1 FY27, +$23.8B in three months, with most payable through FY2027. The filing says inventory and capacity are being secured; it does not disclose a TSMC or CoWoS split, and some commitments can be rescheduled.

Microsoft FY25 net income was $101,832M and additions to property and equipment were $64,551M. Cloud buyers are not capital-starved. But buyer civil works and seller prepayments are different accounts.

**H1 — Strong-buyer hypothesis:** if NVIDIA's cash flow, margin, and order certainty remain materially ahead, its prepayment and supply-commitment capacity keeps strengthening.

**M1 — Profit-seeking allocation mechanism:** advanced capacity is not allocated by "fair share." Price, long-duration orders, prepayments, customer credit, utilization, and next-generation roadmap certainty all matter. If the leading buyer stays ahead on those variables, new scarce capacity has an economic incentive to tilt toward it. Suppliers still care about customer diversity, geopolitics, and long-run bargaining power; this is not one-customer determinism.

**S3 — Resource-hegemony scenario:** if that tilt persists across two or three product generations, NVIDIA's capture of some **incremental** AI-critical resources could enter a 60%–80% stress-test range. This is not a disclosed current share and does not mean NVIDIA owns 80% of all TSMC N2 capacity.

## 3. Watch incremental advanced-capacity capture, not the historical 19% revenue share

TSMC 2025 shipped about 15.0M 12-inch-equivalent wafers and had annual capacity above 17M; 2026 guidance is 16–17M. Wafer **revenue** mix, not wafer count: 3nm 24%, 5nm 36%, 7nm 14%, with 7nm-and-below at 74%. Platforms: HPC 58% (+48%), smartphones 29%. The first customer was 19% of annual revenue, the second 17%; the 20-F does not name them.

Packaging and testing rose from about 8% of revenue in 2024 to a little above 10% in 2025. TSMC has repeatedly described CoWoS as tight and growth-limiting. TrendForce put TSMC at 72% of foundry revenue in 2026 Q1, Samsung at 6.5%, SMIC at 5.1%. Public volume production for frontier AI logic remains heavily concentrated at TSMC, with Samsung providing a limited parallel source.

**F:** HPC and advanced-packaging importance are rising while customer concentration is also rising. The 19% figure is annual revenue share, not wafer share and not an advanced-node share.

**M:** the forward variable is the **Advanced HPC Incremental Capacity Capture Ratio**. If one customer repeatedly captures most of newly opened scarce capacity, installed base, ecosystem scale, cash flow, and prepayments reinforce the next allocation round.

**H/S:** 50% / 60% / 70% / 80% are stress-test levels. If a single platform captures 70%–80% of selected **incremental** N2/N2P/A16-class AI logic, CoWoS subclasses, or first-wave HBM supply across generations, competitors can face nonlinear deterioration in delivery, cost, product cadence, and ecosystem feedback.

## 4. Residual capacity and wafer math

Official rack reference: 72 GPUs, 36 Grace CPUs, 13.4 TB HBM, 17 TB LPDDR.

- 10,000 racks: 134 PB HBM, 170 PB LPDDR.
- 30,000 racks: 402 PB HBM, 510 PB LPDDR.
- 50,000 racks: 670 PB HBM, 850 PB LPDDR.

TrendForce states that HBM + RDIMM account for 51% of 2026 DRAM bits, and memory reaches 47% of major CSP CapEx in 2026 and 68% in 2027. Micron provides first-party evidence that, at the same node and bit capacity, HBM3E requires about **3× the wafer supply** of DDR5; HBM4's trade ratio is expected to be higher. Consumer and general-purpose markets are therefore exposed to a **Non-AI Residual Capacity** problem after higher-value AI memory takes priority.

The report's 5.39 CoWoS wafers-per-rack coefficient is a geometry-based pressure-test estimate: 72 packages ÷ roughly 13.4 packages per wafer. It is **D-grade**, not a fab disclosure. TrendForce's >60% NVIDIA CoWoS-demand share is institutional **B-grade** evidence; TSMC does not publish the customer split. Specific 595k–630k wafer counts come from another sell-side/supply-chain layer. The only valid use of 5.39 here is sensitivity analysis: around 100k racks can create very high packaging pressure under the assumed geometry. Do not use the entire fab wafer pool as the denominator.

As makers shift wafers toward HBM, commodity/server DDR5 becomes residual capacity rather than an independent unconstrained river.

## 5. Cloud competition is now system stack vs system stack

The 2026 reality is no longer "GPU vs one ASIC die."

- **Google Ironwood:** the TPU pod is designed as a complete supercomputer; 192 GB HBM3E per chip and proprietary high-bandwidth interconnect.
- **AWS Trainium3:** 3nm, 144 GB HBM3e, up to 144 chips in a Trn3 UltraServer, organized upward by NeuronLink and the Neuron software stack.
- **Microsoft Maia:** Maia 100 at 5nm + HBM2E evolved to Maia 200 at N3 + HBM3e.
- **Meta MTIA:** moved from LPDDR-based recommendation accelerators toward 300/400/450/500 generations and a full-stack path that includes PyTorch, vLLM, Triton, and OCP rack standards.

Clouds are therefore attacking interconnect, compilers, runtimes, and rack standards—not merely the accelerator die. But this system-level counterattack still depends heavily on advanced logic, HBM, packaging, and power. **Platform profit-pool diversion** and **upstream resource competition** must be analyzed separately.

As of FY27 Q2, NVIDIA Data Center revenue was $89.0B for the quarter, up 117% year on year, with GAAP gross margin at 75.0%. That does not prove cloud system stacks have failed; it shows their diversion has not yet appeared as a structural collapse in NVIDIA's revenue or margin.

## 5A. Model companies are becoming anchor infrastructure buyers

OpenAI's Stargate program and Anthropic's long-duration AWS commitments show that frontier model companies are no longer only passive cloud tenants. They are becoming **anchor compute buyers** whose multi-year demand can influence data-center financing, power planning, and chip deployment.

**Anchor Compute Buyer Effect:**

```
long-duration model demand
→ higher financing certainty for infrastructure
→ earlier cloud / data-center / power buildout
→ priority capacity for the model company
→ larger training and inference
→ larger long-duration commitments
```

Infrastructure cycles must therefore be read through both cloud CapEx / semiconductor supply commitments and model-company capacity commitments. Expansion, rescheduling, under-utilization, or cancellation of the latter can become a leading signal between the growth and hard-landing scenarios.

## 6. China's stack: parallel supply exists at some layers, not an equivalent frontier training stack

China can build parallel supply in smartphones and parts of consumer computing, but it does not yet offer an equivalent substitute for the full frontier-training combination of advanced logic + HBM + advanced packaging + high-speed interconnect + mature software ecosystem. The relevant questions are **which layer, what scale, what performance, what software migration cost, and whether the non-silicon constraints close at the same time**.

CXMT's prospectus contains no HBM line. Publicly repeated monthly-capacity numbers are not official disclosures. YMTC publicly discusses 200+ layer NAND but not the monthly wafer figure often repeated elsewhere.

The near gate is administrative access; the far gate is non-silicon capability. If domestic advanced packaging and HBM-class memory can support training systems at scale, administrative closure can sustain a local loop. If not, controls alone do not create an equivalent frontier-training stack.

## 7. Coordination and the five interrupts

Acting separately can leave each new line and packaging increment to the strongest bidder. To change allocation, three elements matter together: capacity prepayments large enough to create a credible second bid; a portable software base rather than one proprietary lock; and memory contracts that preserve commodity/general-purpose bit capacity. Missing one leaves the counterweight incomplete.

Five interrupts can materially rewrite the line:

1. Western buyers coordinate into a credible second bid.
2. Cloud system stacks move a material share of training **and** inference over the long run—not merely deploy an in-house chip.
3. Power/interconnection becomes the binding brake on expansion.
4. An eastern/alternative stack creates a second large-scale bid for advanced non-silicon resources.
5. Structural antitrust relief or forced-open CUDA materially changes switching economics.

None is complete enough today to rewrite the global allocation structure. Progress is uneven: cloud system-stack competition is already real; open migration routes exist but are not yet frictionless at frontier scale; long-duration power contracts are growing; alternative stacks remain constrained by advanced non-silicon layers; structural antitrust relief has not landed. Investigations exist, complaints/splits do not. A 10% revenue figure is a statutory ceiling in the relevant European framework, not a fine already issued.

## 8. The compute rent

FY26 NVIDIA Data Center revenue of $193.7B is one of the largest single seller-side proxies for AI-infrastructure monetary flow and value capture. It is **not** equal to "all global compute rent."

Cloud CapEx must stay on its own accounting scope. The 2025 mixed-year figure is about $354.0B–$356.5B. By August 2026, Microsoft had guided to roughly $190B of calendar-year CapEx, Alphabet to $180B–$190B, Meta to $125B–$145B, and Amazon's trailing-twelve-month net PPE was around $169B. The time windows and line items still differ, so they cannot be mechanically added into one "AI hardware CapEx" number.

The application layer can fall in price while the backend keeps capturing value. Phones and PCs may buy no GPU and still pay the memory residual tax. The system does not need to "explode" for the hierarchy to matter; upgrades can stall one node below the frontier while the platform layer keeps setting the rules.

If the interrupt conditions fail to close while AI returns, prepayments, and anchor-buyer demand reinforce one another, the S3 resource-hegemony scenario gains weight. If system-stack substitution, new supply, and lower migration cost arrive together, S1/S2 gain weight. The decisive window is the set of years before next-generation first-wave capacity and long-duration commitments are fully locked.
