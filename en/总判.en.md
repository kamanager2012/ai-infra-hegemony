## Current judgment

As of **August 28, 2026**, the report's central conclusion is:

> **AI infrastructure competition has moved from individual chips toward system stacks, scarce physical capacity, capital formation, and workload monetization.**

The current evidence mix is closest to **S2 — Baseline Concentration**:

- leading system platforms retain strong economics;
- HBM, advanced packaging, LPS/power, and migration cost remain important constraints;
- hyperscaler custom systems provide real second-stack substitution;
- model companies increasingly act as Anchor Compute Buyers;
- financing is expanding beyond corporate CapEx into third-party infrastructure capital and selective supplier support;
- China's production-grade parallel stack is real, while autonomous frontier closure remains a separate and higher standard;
- an industry-wide S4 Capital Hard Landing is not established without broad deterioration in accounting, utilization/revenue, market clearing, forward commitments, and demand-financing quality.

## The six questions that matter most

1. Does the competitive unit remain a full system stack rather than a single accelerator?
2. Does scarce-layer supply become more elastic, or does the bottleneck keep rotating?
3. Can alternative system stacks carry large production workloads at acceptable migration cost?
4. Can Anchor Buyer commitments convert into energized, utilized infrastructure?
5. Can workload monetization and independent financing support the expanding capital base?
6. Does China progress from production-grade substitution toward autonomous frontier closure?

## Evidence discipline

The report does not convert:

- revenue share into wafer share;
- demand share into allocation;
- contracted / secured capacity into energized load;
- IT-GW into generation GW;
- institutional forecasts into company actuals;
- model-training compute into full corporate R&D cost;
- production-grade substitution into autonomous frontier closure.
