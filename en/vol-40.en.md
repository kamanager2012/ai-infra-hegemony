## 3. Mechanism (physical / commercial / geopolitical)

### 3.1 What the closing chapter can lock is a base, not a wish

Profit and cash: [Volume 1](vol-01.md). TSMC's books: [Volume 4](vol-04.md). Residual: [Volume 5](vol-05.md). Cloud ASICs: [Volume 4](vol-04.md). The China stack: [Volume 12](vol-12.md). The closing chapter only synthesizes: a margin monopoly is already standing, prepayments are adding, the sales unit has moved up to the cabinet, the revenue bulk is still on cabinets. The model player is an extension of power, not a second profit pool. No split. Do not invent token revenue.

### 3.2 Individuals: stand on the side that cannot be tokenized

Personal survival rules, mechanically, are not as empty as "join the AI industry." Bargaining comes from the layer that cannot be tokenized.

Intermediate text that can be compressed and concurrency-amplified will get cheaper. People who can keep a high-density cabinet running stably, who can match a twenty-year power contract to four-year depreciation, who can schedule between CUDA and CANN, who can make an actuator take instructions on site, will not depreciate linearly with token unit price. Analysis added: personal strategy is therefore choosing a constraint, not a trend. Constraints are power, heat, packaging, responsibility, the floor. Edge devices will be expensive, but if a person can run a workflow locally, lock-in to a cloud API falls. That is the same logic as the firm strategic sentence.

Intelligence rent has three collection points: model weights, compute quota, workflow data. The matching sentence for individuals: do not write irreplaceable judgment as middleware an API can fully recap. Steps that can be published, checked, and rolled back will price as tokens first. Steps that must be signed, must be on site, must answer for yield and for bodies still price as wages and insurance.

This volume has no labor statistics. It does not invent unemployment rates. What can be written is only the mechanism: jobs often stay; the task mix is hollowed out. Medicine, law, structural sign-off, nuclear operations will all require a human in the loop. Headcount inside the loop will fall. Unit price need not fall. Shop-floor labor likewise: a robot puts compute in only one slice of the BOM. The rest is motors, reducers, sensors. Assembly, calibration, repair, and care still need people. If policy only subsidizes the "AI job" title, it will miss the technicians who are truly scarce.

Two referees cut the same job's toolchain by border. Analysis added: a multinational's "globally unified grades, unified tools" will split. Export controls do not lay people off directly. They decide who can use a cabinet cluster, and who can only work on Ethernet or a domestic interconnect. A productivity gap becomes a wage gap. If a person can complete a workflow on only one stack, bargaining is taken by the owner of that stack.

An individual's ability to trade packaging consumables, liquid cooling, or nuclear indicators is limited. The more realistic move: stand occupationally near these constraints, rather than chasing seller gross margin in the secondary market. Once a year, check which side of depreciation you stand on — collecting a software premium, or carrying four-year hardware and a twenty-year power contract. Standing on the wrong side without knowing it is the most expensive strategic error in this cycle.

### 3.3 Firms: moats queue by difficulty. The model name comes last

Firm moats, mechanically, are four, hard to easy.

The first is physical quota: advanced packaging, HBM, grid interconnect, nuclear PPAs. Without these four, a model name is only a lease. TSMC has no financial reason to offend the first name for the second. Anyone else who wants the same material pays more, or does not get it. This is not an administrative takeover. It is prepayments buying the queue. A neutrality pledge has no consideration in front of a check.

The second is the system: interconnect that treats many cards as one memory, plus liquid cooling and power. Only then does gross margin approach the seller's band. What an Ethernet scheme must prove is effective tokens, not peak TFLOPS. No official sample was found proving "95% of the world's frontier operators are bound to CUDA" or "AMD actual inference lags 30%–50%." Those two sentences do not enter the fact layer. The mechanism is still there: if next-generation hardware features keep walking CUDA kernel scheduling, switching stacks pays a migration tax.

The third is workflow: connecting Agents into steps that actually produce cash, not demos. The moat is not "we have a large model." It is "our depreciation can be covered by incremental workflow revenue."

The fourth is the brand model. Analysis added: the 2025–2027 depreciation test will kill in this order — model firms without cash-flow workflows first, hardware firms without quota next, clouds and foundries with power and packaging last. That is an order judgment, not a shipment forecast.

ASICs (TPU / Trainium / Maia / MTIA) are a second moat the clouds dig for themselves, to reduce single-point dependence on a 71%–75% gross-margin seller. The products exist. This pass has no official training-share split. They have not won. Do not write "the counterattack is about to succeed," and do not write "they can only be wiped out." A cloud ASIC hits the athlete's die. It does not hit the referee. To beat today's cabinet, public next generations return to the same scarce slice. Only a real retreat to 5nm and less packaging dodges it. Public next generations have not stopped.

OEM contract manufacturing must be split. On the AI-cabinet slice, OEMs shipping DGX-class / certified systems is already happening. Consumer notebooks becoming "assembled to Nvidia drawings" has not finished, and has no shipment proof. Dell, Lenovo, ASUS, MSI, HP becoming system OEMs is an extension of this already-happening cabinet logic, not a settled fact about consumer notebooks. Chip foundry is still TSMC, not a PC brand becoming a wafer fab. Sony thinning into a software-and-games brand is mechanically coherent: console chips can walk a Tegra / Switch model; content and channel are its moat. Microsoft Intelligent Cloud FY25 $106,265M, Azure FY25 above $75B, is a cloud that buys cabinets, not a game studio. Xbox can thin. Azure will not. Writing both names as the same sentence "become a software brand" is denied by this hard figure. All marked scenario. Do not name the year a rival exits.

The overweight list on the investment side keeps the draft's original sentence: packaging consumables, liquid-cooling CDUs, nuclear and grid interconnect, not overweight narrative stocks. In an oligopoly scenario they are rent-collecting assets. In a hard-landing scenario they are the long liabilities hardest to write down. Before overweighting you must first pick the branch you believe, and accept the cost of picking wrong.

### 3.4 Nations: a single point cannot be fully copied inside ten years

National strategy, mechanically, is admitting a single point cannot be fully copied inside ten years. High-NA, Japanese materials qualification, CoWoS, HBM yield are not physical times a funding slogan can shorten. Analysis added: if a country piles resources into a parameter race, it becomes a price-taker in front of global capex that has already happened and may still add.

A feasible national combination is a three-piece set. First, keep or rebuild power and licenses, or chips wait on power. Second, treat algorithms and architecture as a lever: cheaper training and more memory-sparing structures break the shakedown that "you must own a hundred thousand cards"; DeepSeek training about $5.576 million, KV compressed 93.3% is a draft anchor, not re-checked against the original technical report this pass, not a new audit fact — only the mechanism stays, "algorithms can cut cabinets; they cannot cut packaging and memory." Third, under a dual-track scenario, train CANN or an equivalent stack until migration cost is acceptable. The draft said open-source model rewrite volume can fall below 5%. This pass has no public check. Do not promote it.

CHIPS-Act-style domestic fabs are a political necessity, but if packaging, materials, and the grid are not built at the same time, the closing chapter must mark them a high-cost copy, not a second TSMC. The copyability ladder still holds: assembly can change location; packaging, foundry, lithography, and materials get harder to copy by statute the further up you go. Politically plural, technically still a single point, is the mechanism, not a completed fact.

On the China stack, three gates: [Volume 12](vol-12.md). The third gate cannot be passed now, so the China stack cannot save Apple / AMD's seat at TSMC. US antitrust cannot save Huawei. National-security documents: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). The documents only prove "it was written as national security." They do not prove "a decision not to break up has already been made."

### 3.5 Antitrust is not a shortcut. A fine does not change the bid

The case file: [Volume 11](vol-11.md) and [Volume 31](vol-31.md). What can change the rules overnight is not the second name. It is a breakup, forced interoperability, a ban on bundled supply. A fine does not change the bid. The investigation is there. A complaint is not.

### 3.6 Compute rent: the application layer grinds. The backend collects

Rent and four-cloud scope: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). The closing chapter only leaves the direction: a slice of cloud capex becomes cabinets, then becomes seller revenue. The application layer grinds. The backend collects rent. Phones and PCs that buy no accelerator still pay a memory-spill tax. The world is not blowing open. It is an upgrade parked on the next-lower node, with rules set by one firm.

The five interrupts: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). If they are not done, the line walks the scarce slice past halfway. The window is these few years before next-generation first silicon is locked by commitments. The engine has not changed: demand pushes profit, profit pushes prepayments, prepayments lock capacity. ### 3.8 Endgames carry branches, not faith, and not forged probabilities

A geopolitical endgame requires strategy to carry branches, not faith. Draft 40% / 30% / 15% / 15% is only discussion weight. None were checked this pass. Keep them marked unverified. Do not treat them as a forecast. What the closing chapter rewrites is the content of the endgame, not another tree with percentages.

Oligopoly / hardware dominance: hang the strongest form, mark it scenario. Rivals are all still here today. Do not write who exits. Individuals join the workflow layer. Firms that distribute still control data. Nations talk access and power. Testable: whether the next-generation console APU is still AMD; whether consumer leading-edge process can still volume a non-Nvidia scheme; whether OEM AI servers are left with only the Nvidia certified list. No notice, leave it blank.

Algorithm breakthrough and edge: edge models strong enough that a device can run everyday intelligence; individuals regain a small piece of on-device means of production; firm moats retreat to scene and brand; national regulation moves from export control to safety and responsibility. The counter is oligopoly: compute locked by a few clouds and Nvidia, workers generally becoming distribution channels.

Capital hard landing: cash is king, overweight becomes write-down, whoever has the hardest power contracts and prepayments hurts most. Most of the $119.0B supply commitments are to be paid in FY27. Some agreements can be rescheduled. Rescheduling is not free. The 10-K wrote Changes to these agreements may result in additional costs.

East–West dual track: talent, standards, and spare parts must be prepared as two sets. The referee corner splits before the wafer fab. Ascend / CANN versus CUDA is two deployment stacks. It does not need High-NA to be copied first. That the product lines exist can be written; 910C shipments, SMIC yield, domestic HBM capacity were not publicly checked this pass. Do not promote them. Dual track means two referees, two cabinets, two grids. Global demand does not double because of it. Algorithm leverage can reduce the necessity of the athlete's cabinet. It does not unlock packaging and memory at the coach corner.

Foundry is two layers. Chip foundry is still TSMC. What the Dells can become is system OEM. Writing "PC giants become foundries" as wafer fabs is the wrong layer. Consoles: they do not now make a first-party console brand the way Sony / Microsoft do. They already make console chips (Switch uses Tegra). FY25 Gaming $11,350M, FY26 Gaming $16,042M, mainly GeForce, not console share. Do not invent a Switch royalty. "They will certainly make a console brand in the future" is too full. Testable: whether an Nvidia-brand console appears, or whether a console SoC moves from AMD exclusive to an Nvidia scheme.

A country or firm optimized only for the oligopoly scenario will run naked on the other branches. Analysis added: the right order is first see whether you hold power, packaging, workflow, and dual-stack capability, then see which side of which endgame you land on. A firm with grid interconnect and a nuclear contract has a tailwind in oligopoly and a long liability in a hard landing. A firm with only a model name has algorithm breakthrough as its only living path; the other branches are dependence. A country with only subsidies and no licensing reform will buy a high-cost copy in every scenario.

## 4. Analysis added (scenarios, counters, testable indicators)

Analysis added. Fold the three subjects' moves into a checkable list. Do not assign new quantitative targets. Do not invent a ten-year forecast.

### 4.1 Individual checklist

Whether you have at least one "power / heat / packaging / floor / responsibility" skill; whether you can complete the same workflow on two software stacks; whether income depends on a single proprietary API. Checking these is closer to the draft than checking "whether you can use the latest chat model." Whether occupational standing is near the constraint layer, not near a tokenizable middle layer. Whether once a year you check which side of depreciation you stand on.

Counters: writing the closing chapter as a pep list ("embrace change," "invest more") with no depreciation and no queue; treating "can use a model" as bargaining power; chasing seller gross margin in the secondary market with no ability to hold packaging, liquid cooling, or grid-interconnect assets.

### 4.2 Firm checklist

Whether capex and depreciation can live on usage in the matrix cell where token unit price falls. The token matrix is still scenario arithmetic. This pass has no high-frequency figures for lead time, prepayments, or second-hand cabinets. Whether you have both multi-chip scheduling and a closeable workflow. Whether there is a visible contract on CoWoS / HBM / power, not only a press release. Whether inference cost has been broken out into cost of sales. Whether a cloud ASIC has truly pulled training off the cabinet, or only shunted internal inference.

Investment side: pick a branch before overweighting. In an oligopoly scenario, packaging consumables, liquid-cooling CDUs, nuclear and grid interconnect are rent-collecting assets. In a hard-landing scenario they are the long liabilities hardest to write down. A seller whose prepayments are as large as $119.0B also hurts in a hard landing. It just has one year of $96,575M free cash as a pad. Buyers do not have that pad.

Counters: treating 2026 $750B as destiny; treating the one of four endgames you like as a plan; writing "regulation is about to open the market" as a timetable; writing "we have a large model" as a moat.

### 4.3 National checklist

Whether power licenses and main-transformer queues are written in the same industrial policy as chip subsidies. Whether algorithm research is treated as a lever, not publicity. Whether dual-track spare parts and textbooks exist. Whether the near gate truly blocks domestic bits, tools, and substrates from flowing out at an international premium. Whether the far gate has non-silicon volume that can hold a training cabinet, or only a slogan.

Test indicators use what is already public: whether queue years are still the draft's 5~7 (grid-queue originals not re-checked this pass, not a new anchor); whether 3nm / 2nm still concentrates at the same foundry; whether the domestic stack's model-migration cost is truly low; whether SMIC / domestic packaging has a public N3 / N2-class export customer; whether CXMT has public HBM export and pricing; whether justice.gov shows *United States v. NVIDIA*; whether France has issued a statement of objections; whether the EU has a 101/102 opening text.

Counters: treating a parameter race as industrial policy; writing a CHIPS-Act fab as a second TSMC; reading a national-security memo as "regulation will not touch the athlete" or "regulation will certainly protect the athlete"; writing the China stack as already able to take a Western photoresist.

### 4.4 Decision tree: pick the constraint first, then the story

Individuals, firms, and nations often make the same error: pick the endgame story they like, then go back for numbers. The right order is the reverse. First see whether you hold power, packaging, workflow, and dual-stack capability, then see which side of which endgame you land on.

The decade's survival rule is therefore short. Individuals: stand on the constraint side tokenization cannot swallow. Firms: let depreciation be covered by workflow cash; let quota and power be contracts you can show. Nations: treat algorithms as a lever, power and process as the base, keep one set of spare parts for dual track. Visions beyond these three sentences have no evidence in the draft. The closing chapter does not add them.

When the full case ends it still has to return to one unpleasant sentence: most readers cannot change a CoWoS quota or a nuclear license. They can only change which layer of rent they stand on. Standing on a tokenizable middle layer, the decade will look bad. Standing on the constraint layer, the decade will be tiring, but bargaining is still there. The use of a strategy report is to make that layer visible, not to make people feel inspired.

The closing chapter also does not pick an endgame for the reader. 40%, 30%, 15%, 15% is only the draft's discussion weight. Which one you pick is a judgment, not a fact. An investigation can drag many years. A fine is a cost. The paths that can change the rules overnight have not been done. The window is these few years before next-generation first silicon is locked by commitments. After that, even a second-name coalition is asking for leftovers.

### 4.5 How the four corners land on the three subjects

The closing chapter does not open a separate empire catalog. Forty volumes' titles are different surfaces of the same power. The four corners decide, better than a "four-layer industry catalog," which side individuals, firms, and nations each stand on.

The athlete decides who can pay for a cabinet. Individuals almost cannot buy a whole cabinet. What they can do is stop turning themselves into an intermediate step billed by the cabinet. A firm without a quota contract is renting the athlete's capacity. A country with only subsidies and no power or packaging is a price-taker for the athlete's customers.

The referee decides the stack-switch tax. If individuals and firms can deliver on only one stack, the tax is paid yearly. If a country's textbooks teach only one referee, a dual track will be short of people the moment it opens. A forced opening of CUDA can change the rules overnight. On the public record it has not been done.

The coach decides who gets next year's newly opened line. Individuals cannot touch the coach corner. What a firm can show is only a long contract and a prepayment. A national-statute fab that does not also build packaging and materials is a high-cost copy. Packaging tightness: [Volume 24](vol-24.md).

The model player is an extension of power, not a second profit pool. An individual who locks a workflow into a proprietary API is turning themselves into this corner's distribution. A firm that treats "we have a large model" as a moat is treating the fourth moat as the first.

The first siphon wave: [Volume 22](vol-22.md), [Volume 33](vol-33.md). Individuals replacing devices more slowly is how rent reaches the terminal. It is not a statistic that "AI jobs disappeared."

How to read the window: do not invent new figures. The gates are each volume's testable items. If the gates have not moved, the closing chapter does not change the three strategic sentences.
