### 3.2 Individuals: stand on the side that cannot be tokenized

Personal survival rules, mechanically, are not as empty as "join the AI industry." Bargaining comes from the layer that cannot be tokenized.

Intermediate text that can be compressed and concurrency-amplified will get cheaper. People who can keep a high-density cabinet running stably, who can match a twenty-year power contract to four-year depreciation, who can schedule between CUDA and CANN, who can make an actuator take instructions on site, will not depreciate linearly with token unit price. personal strategy is therefore choosing a constraint, not a trend. Constraints are power, heat, packaging, responsibility, the floor. Edge devices will be expensive, but if a person can run a workflow locally, lock-in to a cloud API falls. That is the same logic as the firm strategic sentence.

AI-related value capture can occur at several layers, including model capability, compute access, proprietary data, workflow integration, and system interfaces. The matching sentence for individuals: do not write irreplaceable judgment as middleware an API can fully recap. Steps that can be published, checked, and rolled back will price as tokens first. Steps that must be signed, must be on site, must answer for yield and for bodies still price as wages and insurance.

This volume has no labor statistics. It does not invent unemployment rates. What can be written is only the mechanism: jobs often stay; the task mix is hollowed out. Medicine, law, structural sign-off, nuclear operations will all require a human in the loop. Headcount inside the loop will fall. Unit price need not fall. Shop-floor labor likewise: a robot puts compute in only one slice of the BOM. The rest is motors, reducers, sensors. Assembly, calibration, repair, and care still need people. If policy only subsidizes the "AI job" title, it will miss the technicians who are truly scarce.

Multiple software/runtime ecosystems can split the same occupational toolchain across geopolitical boundaries. a multinational's "globally unified grades, unified tools" will split. Export controls do not lay people off directly. They decide who can use a cabinet cluster, and who can only work on Ethernet or a domestic interconnect. A productivity gap becomes a wage gap. If a person can complete a workflow on only one stack, bargaining is taken by the owner of that stack.

An individual's ability to trade packaging consumables, liquid cooling, or nuclear indicators is limited. The more realistic move: stand occupationally near these constraints, rather than chasing seller gross margin in the secondary market. Once a year, check which side of depreciation you stand on — collecting a software premium, or carrying four-year hardware and a twenty-year power contract. Standing on the wrong side without knowing it is the most expensive strategic error in this cycle.

### 3.3 Firms: moats queue by difficulty. The model name comes last

Firm moats, mechanically, are four, hard to easy.

The first moat is physical access: advanced packaging, HBM, executable grid interconnection, and long-duration power. These do not make one buyer automatically entitled to every increment. Allocation depends on price, order scale, prepayment, customer credit, utilization, roadmap certainty, supplier diversification, and geopolitics. Strong free cash flow and long-duration commitments can tilt scarce **incremental** capacity toward the strongest buyers, while suppliers still have reasons to preserve customer diversity and bargaining power.

The second moat is the **system stack**: accelerator, scale-up/scale-out interconnect, compiler/runtime, rack/pod, cooling/power design, and the workload layer that turns the hardware into usable compute. Migration must be measured in effective workload performance, reliability, cost, and software rewrite—not peak FLOPS alone. Public evidence does not support claims such as "95% of frontier operators are bound to CUDA" or a universal 30%–50% AMD inference gap. The mechanism that remains is switching cost across a tightly integrated stack.

The third is workflow: connecting Agents into steps that actually produce cash, not demos. The moat is not "we have a large model." It is "our depreciation can be covered by incremental workflow revenue."

The fourth is the brand model. in a capital-clearing scenario, pressure is likely to appear first among model firms without cash-flow workflows first, hardware firms without quota next, clouds and foundries with power and packaging last. That is an order judgment, not a shipment forecast.

TPU, Trainium, Maia, and MTIA are no longer best described as "cloud ASICs that only hit the die." They are developing into **cloud system stacks** that combine chips, HBM, interconnect, compiler/runtime, rack/pod design, and internal workloads. They create real pressure on NVIDIA's platform profit pool, but public data do not provide a comparable training/inference share across the four clouds. The correct test is whether these stacks can migrate material workloads over time, reduce switching cost, and reduce dependence on NVIDIA systems—while also observing whether they continue to compete with NVIDIA for advanced logic, HBM, packaging, and power.

OEM contract manufacturing must be split. On the AI-cabinet slice, OEMs shipping DGX-class / certified systems is already happening. Consumer notebooks becoming "assembled to Nvidia drawings" has not finished, and has no shipment proof. Dell, Lenovo, ASUS, MSI, HP becoming system OEMs is an extension of this already-happening cabinet logic, not a settled fact about consumer notebooks. Chip foundry is still TSMC, not a PC brand becoming a wafer fab. Sony thinning into a software-and-games brand is mechanically coherent: console chips can walk a Tegra / Switch model; content and channel are its moat. Microsoft Intelligent Cloud FY25 $106,265M, Azure FY25 above $75B, is a cloud that buys cabinets, not a game studio. Xbox can thin. Azure will not. Writing both names as the same sentence "become a software brand" is denied by this hard figure. All marked scenario. Do not name the year a rival exits.

For capital allocation, the same physical asset can change sign across scenarios. Packaging, cooling, power, and interconnection can capture scarcity value in S2/S3, yet become long-duration utilization risk in S4. Scenario choice therefore matters more than a generic "AI infrastructure" label.

### 3.4 Nations: a single point cannot be fully copied inside ten years

National strategy begins by recognizing that a frontier system stack cannot be replicated by funding one component in isolation. High-NA, Japanese materials qualification, CoWoS, HBM yield are not physical times a funding slogan can shorten. if a country piles resources into a parameter race, it becomes a price-taker in front of global capex that has already happened and may still add.

A feasible national strategy requires at least three coordinated layers. First, keep or rebuild power and licenses, or chips wait on power. Second, treat algorithms and architecture as a lever: cheaper training and more memory-sparing structures break the shakedown that "you must own a hundred thousand cards"; DeepSeek-V3's **2.788M H800 GPU-hours** and report-side **$5.576M** formal-training conversion show that algorithmic efficiency can materially alter compute economics, while DeepSeek-V2's 93.3% KV-cache reduction is a separate technical comparison. Neither removes packaging, HBM, interconnect, or power constraints. Third, under a dual-track scenario, train CANN or an equivalent stack until migration cost is acceptable. The older “under 5% rewrite” migration claim remains outside the fact layer unless independently reproduced.

CHIPS-Act-style domestic fabs are a political necessity, but if packaging, materials, and the grid are not built at the same time, the closing chapter must mark them a high-cost copy, not a second TSMC. The copyability ladder still holds: assembly can change location; packaging, foundry, lithography, and materials get harder to copy by statute the further up you go. Politically plural, technically still a single point, is the mechanism, not a completed fact.

On the China stack, use the layered framework in [Volume 12](vol-12.en.md). China has stronger parallel supply in smartphones, consumer computing, electromechanical manufacturing, and parts of the domestic accelerator/software stack than it has in a fully equivalent frontier training stack. The unresolved frontier layers are advanced logic, HBM-class memory, advanced packaging, high-speed interconnect, and software migration at scale. U.S. antitrust does not itself open restricted manufacturing capacity, and national-security documents do not prove that regulators have already decided either to protect or to break up a particular platform.

### 3.8 Endgames carry branches, not faith, and not forged probabilities

A geopolitical endgame requires strategy to carry branches, not faith. The former 40% / 30% / 15% / 15% discussion weights are not part of the formal scenario model and should not be treated as a forecast. What the closing chapter rewrites is the content of the endgame, not another tree with percentages.

Oligopoly / hardware dominance: hang the strongest form, mark it scenario. Rivals are all still here today. The report does not treat who exits. Individuals join the workflow layer. Firms that distribute still control data. Nations talk access and power. Testable: whether the next-generation console APU is still AMD; whether consumer leading-edge process can still volume a non-Nvidia scheme; whether OEM AI servers are left with only the Nvidia certified list. No notice, leave it blank.

Algorithm breakthrough and edge: edge models strong enough that a device can run everyday intelligence; individuals regain a small piece of on-device means of production; firm moats retreat to scene and brand; national regulation moves from export control to safety and responsibility. The counter is oligopoly: compute locked by a few clouds and Nvidia, workers generally becoming distribution channels.

Capital hard landing: cash is king, overweight becomes write-down, whoever has the hardest power contracts and prepayments hurts most. Most of the $119.0B supply commitments are to be paid in FY27. Some agreements can be rescheduled. Rescheduling is not free. The 10-K wrote Changes to these agreements may result in additional costs.

East–West dual track: talent, standards, and spare parts must be prepared as two sets. software/runtime ecosystems can diverge before manufacturing stacks become fully independent. Ascend / CANN versus CUDA is two deployment stacks. It does not need High-NA to be copied first. That the product lines exist can be written; 910C shipments, SMIC yield, domestic HBM capacity remain outside the verified fact layer. A dual-track scenario requires separate software, systems, and infrastructure capacity. Global demand does not double because of it. Algorithm leverage can reduce the necessity of the system platform's cabinet. It does not unlock packaging and memory at the supply-coordination layer corner.

Foundry is two layers. Chip foundry is still TSMC. What the Dells can become is system OEM. Writing "PC giants become foundries" as wafer fabs is the wrong layer. Consoles: they do not now make a first-party console brand the way Sony / Microsoft do. They already make console chips (Switch uses Tegra). FY25 Gaming $11,350M, FY26 Gaming $16,042M, mainly GeForce, not console share. The report does not infer a Switch royalty. "They will certainly make a console brand in the future" is too full. Testable: whether an Nvidia-brand console appears, or whether a console SoC moves from AMD exclusive to an Nvidia scheme.

A country or firm optimized for only one scenario can be badly exposed in the others. the right order is first see whether you hold power, packaging, workflow, and dual-stack capability, then see which side of which endgame you land on. A firm with grid interconnect and a nuclear contract has a tailwind in oligopoly and a long liability in a hard landing. A firm with only a model name has algorithm breakthrough as its only living path; the other branches are dependence. A country with only subsidies and no licensing reform will buy a high-cost copy in every scenario.

## 4. Scenario-dependent strategy and testable indicators

Fold the three subjects' moves into a checkable list. Do not assign new quantitative targets. The report does not infer a ten-year forecast.

### 4.1 Individual checklist

Whether you have at least one "power / heat / packaging / floor / responsibility" skill; whether you can complete the same workflow on two software stacks; whether income depends on a single proprietary API. Checking these is closer to historical stress-test material than checking "whether you can use the latest chat model." Whether occupational standing is near the constraint layer, not near a tokenizable middle layer. Whether once a year you check which side of depreciation you stand on.

Counters: writing the closing chapter as a pep list ("embrace change," "invest more") with no depreciation and no queue; treating "can use a model" as bargaining power; chasing seller gross margin in the secondary market with no ability to hold packaging, liquid cooling, or grid-interconnect assets.

### 4.2 Firm checklist

Whether capex and depreciation can live on usage in the matrix cell where token unit price falls. The token matrix is still scenario arithmetic. The current evidence review has no high-frequency figures for lead time, prepayments, or second-hand cabinets. Whether you have both multi-chip scheduling and a closeable workflow. Whether there is a visible contract on CoWoS / HBM / power, not only a press release. Whether inference cost has been broken out into cost of sales. Whether a cloud ASIC has truly pulled training off the cabinet, or only shunted internal inference.

Investment side: pick a branch before overweighting. In an oligopoly scenario, packaging consumables, liquid-cooling CDUs, nuclear and grid interconnect are scarce-infrastructure assets. In a hard-landing scenario they are the long liabilities hardest to write down. A seller whose prepayments are as large as $119.0B also hurts in a hard landing. It just has one year of $96,575M free cash as a pad. Buyers do not have that pad.

Counters: treating 2026 $750B as destiny; treating the one of four endgames you like as a plan; writing "regulation is about to open the market" as a timetable; writing "we have a large model" as a moat.

### 4.3 National checklist

Whether power licenses and main-transformer queues are written in the same industrial policy as chip subsidies. Whether algorithm research is treated as a lever, not publicity. Whether dual-track spare parts and textbooks exist. Whether the near gate truly blocks domestic bits, tools, and substrates from flowing out at an international premium. Whether the far gate has non-silicon volume that can hold a training cabinet, or only a slogan.

Test indicators use what is already public: whether interconnection lead times remain materially longer than rack deployment cycles; whether 3nm / 2nm still concentrates at the same foundry; whether the domestic stack's model-migration cost is truly low; whether SMIC / domestic packaging has a public N3 / N2-class export customer; whether CXMT has public HBM export and pricing; whether justice.gov shows *United States v. NVIDIA*; whether France has issued a statement of objections; whether the EU has a 101/102 opening text.

Counters: treating a parameter race as industrial policy; writing a CHIPS-Act fab as a second TSMC; reading a national-security memo as "regulation will not touch the system platform" or "regulation will certainly protect the system platform"; writing the China stack as already able to take a Western photoresist.

### 4.4 Decision tree: pick the constraint first, then the story

Individuals, firms, and nations often make the same error: pick the endgame story they like, then go back for numbers. The right order is the reverse. First see whether you hold power, packaging, workflow, and dual-stack capability, then see which side of which endgame you land on.

The strategic summary is concise. Individuals should build capability near judgment, responsibility, and physical/system constraints. Firms should make workflow cash flow cover infrastructure commitments and preserve portability. Capital should distinguish scarce-layer value from duration risk. States should coordinate algorithms, manufacturing, packaging, memory, software migration, and executable power rather than optimize a single component.

When the full case ends it still has to return to one unpleasant sentence: most readers cannot change a CoWoS quota or a nuclear license. They can only change which layer of value capture they stand on. Standing on a tokenizable middle layer, the decade will look bad. Standing on the constraint layer, the decade will be tiring, but bargaining is still there. The use of a strategy report is to make that layer visible, not to make people feel inspired.

The closing chapter also does not pick an endgame for the reader. 40%, 30%, 15%, 15% is only historical stress-test material's discussion weight. Which one you pick is a judgment, not a fact. An investigation can drag many years. A fine is a cost. The paths that can change the rules overnight have not been done. The window is these few years before next-generation first silicon is locked by commitments. After that, even a second-name coalition is asking for leftovers.

### 4.5 How the platform-control layers land on the three subjects

The forty volumes describe different surfaces of the same system: resource allocation, platform control, physical bottlenecks, capital duration, and institutional response. The closing chapter therefore returns to system-level positioning rather than a company-by-company prediction list.

the system platform decides who can pay for a cabinet. Individuals almost cannot buy a whole cabinet. What they can do is stop turning themselves into an intermediate step billed by the cabinet. A firm without a quota contract is renting the system platform's capacity. A country with only subsidies and no power or packaging is a price-taker for the system platform's customers.

the software/runtime layer decides the stack-switch cost pressure. If individuals and firms can deliver on only one stack, the cost pressure is paid yearly. If a country's training system teaches only one software/runtime ecosystem, a dual-track scenario can create immediate migration and talent bottlenecks. A forced opening of CUDA can change the rules overnight. On the public record it has not been done.

the supply-coordination layer decides who gets next year's newly opened line. Individuals cannot touch the supply-coordination layer corner. What a firm can show is only a long contract and a prepayment. A national-statute fab that does not also build packaging and materials is a high-cost copy. Packaging tightness: [Volume 24](vol-24.en.md).

The model player is an extension of power, not a second profit pool. An individual who locks a workflow into a proprietary API is turning themselves into this corner's distribution. A firm that treats "we have a large model" as a moat is treating the fourth moat as the first.

The first siphon wave: [Volume 22](vol-22.en.md), [Volume 33](vol-33.en.md). Individuals replacing devices more slowly is how value capture reaches the terminal. It is not a statistic that "AI jobs disappeared."

How to read the window: do not invent new figures. The gates are each volume's testable items. If the gates have not moved, the closing chapter does not change the three strategic sentences.
