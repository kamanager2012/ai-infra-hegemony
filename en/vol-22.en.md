# Volume 22: A quantitative shock model for consumer electronics (Snapdragon 8 Elite $180–$190 and phone/PC BOM inflation)

Chinese original: [vol-22.md](vol-22.md)

## 1. The question this volume answers

The title promised a quantitative model that could compute "how many points of whole-device BOM inflation." What this pass can stand on is not that table. What this pass can stand on is the sentence already written in Judgment section 8: phones and PCs that never buy a GPU still pay a memory spillover tax. The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down. One firm writes the rules.

What this volume answers is therefore not "by how many percent next year's flagship will rise again." That sentence exceeds the draft, and it exceeds this task's number discipline. What this volume answers is three harder layers.

Layer one: is the shock a one-time price rise, or does it become structure for as long as the HBM penalty rate lasts. One-time rise means: after the cloud side cuts orders, leading-edge logic and conventional bits flow back, and SoC and memory prices stop or give back. Structure means: makers allocate wafers to stacks and server sticks; the consumer seat becomes residual after the same river is drawn down; the rise does not have to wait for the next node switch. Judgment section 4 already nailed the direction: makers convert to HBM, consumer bits fall further; server DDR5 is already residual, not another river.

Layer two: is the China market a second gravity. The deepening note wrote this volume's place as a residual receiver, not a second bid. More domestic phone and PC shipments buy residual bits, not HBM, and not advanced packaging. The more they buy, the more buyers sit inside the residual. The residual itself is first cut by that half of 2026 DRAM bits that is HBM plus RDIMM. The premium Chinese consumers pay lifts domestic retail and brand margin. It does not lift TSMC HPC's plus 48% year on year, and it does not stop the firm that sells cabinets from adding another tranche of prepayments.

Layer three: what can be quantified, and what must stay empty. What can be quantified: half the bits, 3× silicon, three makers' books standing on the cabinet side, scenario petabytes multiplied by cabinet, and channel talk on consumer sticks. What cannot be quantified: a complete phone or PC bill of materials, a public purchase contract from a brand or Qualcomm, a consecutive-decline table of non-AI server shipments, or a "whole-device inflation model" backed out of analyst SoC prices. The $180 to $190 in the title, the 30% BOM share, and the 40-month replacement cycle all stay as "the draft once wrote this." They are not promoted to inputs for this pass.

The first wave of siphoning is set by the main line as PCs, commercial PCs, general-purpose servers, and phones. Servers must be split: AI cabinets are expanding; general-purpose dual-socket boxes and brands that cannot get accelerators are squeezed. Extinction lists are marked as scenarios. No names. This volume does not write "brand X has already exited." It only writes structure: the cabinet can pay; the device cannot; makers shift production, they do not shift the right to set the sticker; what is expensive is not the price tag the cabinet-seller writes on a consumer stick, but the bits and wafers that were drawn away.

### 3.2 TrendForce: half the bits already went to cabinets

It is not an "memory purchases" row in Amazon, Google, Microsoft, or Meta statements.

How this holds the volume: 51% is bit supply, not revenue. HBM unit price is high, so the revenue share will be higher. The remaining less-than-half of bits is phones, PCs, consumer, and other non-RDIMM. If the cloud shifts inference onto DDR5, it is fighting for the residual already inside that 51%, or the slice already rising with it. For phones and PCs, that is the denominator a quantitative model can actually use: not one flagship's SoC purchase price, but that half the world's bits have already been cut away.

## 4. Mechanism (how a physical price rise becomes whole-device inflation)

The shock has three conveyor belts that reinforce one another, not one. Analysis add. None of the three has to pass through an analyst purchase price on Snapdragon 8 Elite.

### 4.1 First belt: the right to bid sits on the cabinet, not the device

The firm that sells cabinets takes 71% to 75% gross margin. Profit, cash, and supply commitments are [Volume 1](vol-01.md).

Among buyers, none, on the act of selling cabinets, can outbid 71% to 75%. Apple, AMD, Qualcomm, Huawei are all buyers. Comparing sticker prices is meaningless. TSMC 2025 gross margin 59.9%, Hynix operating margin 49%, are already seller riches, and still do not cover the cabinet-selling tranche. Hypothesis from here: while the margin holds, commitments keep rising. Anyone else who wants the same material pays more, or does not get it. TSMC has no financial reason to offend the first name for the second. Maker boards answer to shareholders. When the profit the first name pays exceeds the increment from everyone else, a neutrality pledge has no consideration in front of a check.

A training box is a million-dollar-class system. Memory, however expensive, does not break the cabinet sale. Phones, PCs, and consumer sticks have a high memory share; the same rise eats the whole-device BOM first. This is structure, not a percentage newly measured in this pass. There is no official "memory as x% of cabinet BOM" line item. Do not invent one. The cabinet is not afraid of expensive. The device dies first. The first sentence a quantitative model can use is that sentence, not "the application processor broke 30% of BOM."

Another official sentence opened in the project audit is used here only as contrast. This volume does not claim this task newly read the 10-K: FY25 Gaming $11.350B against data center $115.186B; Q4 Gaming $2.544B, down 11% year on year and 22% quarter on quarter, original text written as limited supply for Blackwell and Ada. It is not "a published cut of how many tens of thousands of game cards." It is an official admission that consumer cards and the data center fight for the same supply. Consumer discrete GPUs are already being squeezed. That is harder than an analyst quote on one flagship phone. Official 60-class list prices that can be used: RTX 4060 RMB 2,399, RTX 5060 RMB 2,499, about +4%, suggested retail, not a Huaqiangbei print. The contrast: official game-card stickers walk almost sideways, and a single-quarter jump in memory contract prices is not the same order of magnitude. What is expensive is not the price tag the cabinet-seller writes on a 60-class card. It is the bits and wafers that were drawn away.

### 4.2 Second belt: half the bits were cut away; silicon burn is harsher than bits

51% is bit supply. HBM unit price is high, so the revenue share will be higher. Hynix has already written the revenue-side tilt into a press release as "more than doubled in a year." Micron CMBU jumps from $3.79B to $13.52B; MCBU walks from $11.67B to $11.86B, almost still for a year. Samsung Memory walks from KRW 84.5T to KRW 104.1T, and what it writes as priority is HBM, server DDR5, and enterprise SSD. Three books stand on the same side.

Silicon drawn away is harsher than bits. The 10-K only writes "more wafers." Hot Chips paraphrases about 3×. The draft's 3:1 is a sketch formula: area loss times a yield product. The formula is not a measured constant inside a manufacturing execution system. 4:1 and 5:1 earn the right to move from forecast to observation only after 16-Hi is in volume and makers still say "one wafer counts as four." This volume does not write a sketch formula as a law of nature. What can enter the fact layer is the direction of "more wafers" and "about 3×." Move a line, and residual falls by more. Mechanical hard drives are not inside the cabinet. They sit next to cloud storage, and they are drawn by the hall budget the same way. TrendForce also writes NAND into the 47% to 68% of cloud capex. Consumer drives are not bystanders.

Multiply-by-cabinet writes the turn as arithmetic. 10,000 cabinets take 134 PB of HBM and 170 PB of LPDDR. 30,000 cabinets: 402 and 510. 50,000 cabinets: 670 and 850. NVIDIA does not report cabinet counts. Makers do not report full-year absolute bits. So these are scenarios, not shipments. The use of the scenario is not to report that some percent of the world has already been bought. It is to say: once shipments walk by cabinet, demand multiplies by cabinet; if supply cannot keep up with the product, the leftover goes to phones, PCs, and consumer drives, split among a few dozen brands. Memory share inside the cabinet is low, so the cabinet is not afraid of expensive. Phone and PC share is high, so they die first. Channel 16G 450 to 1,800 is the tip, not pricing power.

If the cloud shifts inference onto DDR5, that is not opening a new river. HBM has already drawn wafers at a higher silicon burn; the DDR5 bits that remain are still being split between RDIMM and phones and PCs. Stacking more DDR5 is a tax on the slice already rising. Public next-generation specs are [Volume 4](vol-04.md). No firm was found saying it would retreat to 5nm plus commodity DDR5 as the inference mainstay. So "shift inference onto conventional sticks and the phone will get room" has no anchor in public next generations.

### 4.3 Third belt: the increment in leading-edge logic is not in the phone

TSMC 2025 HPC 58%, up 48% year on year; smartphone 29%, up 11% year on year. What is being opened is HPC and packaging. Apple locks phone logic. It cannot lock the increment. Booked 19% is not a safe distance. Occupy the increment and next year's slots belong to the first name. Phone absolute dollars are still up 11%; the share fell from a higher seat to 29%. Memory makers turning to HBM are following this increment, not the phone LPDDR pricing meeting.

The draft writes N3E at $20,000 to $22,000 per wafer as the upstream of flagship SoC price rises. This pass's evidence did not recheck that wafer quote. It does not enter the fact layer. The mechanism is still isomorphic: a flagship SoC, for an NPU and a larger cache, often also wants a larger die; "each wafer more expensive times each chip occupying more area" stacked together is how analysts write $180 to $190. This volume admits that belt holds in physics. It does not admit those two dollar figures are already a contract. The PC side has no matching CPU or discrete-GPU purchase price, but whoever fights training chips for N3 or N2 pays the premium first. Phone application processors mainly take standard packages. What is actually gated is leading-edge process quota and memory wafers, not an advanced-packaging quota table. Writing NVIDIA 60% and Apple 8% into the phone shock makes readers think the first evidence of siphoning is a packaging monopoly. It should be rewritten: the official packaging anchor is "tight, plus a rising revenue share"; the official consumer-side anchor is limited game-card supply, plus TrendForce writing PC OEMs and phones as the side short of quota.

On geopolitics, the consumer-electronics supply chain is highly concentrated in East Asian assembly and Korean memory. Export controls mainly hit leading-edge GPUs. They do not directly give phones a holiday. But if HBM and leading-edge-logic capex keep filling the makers, a relaxation of controls will not automatically knock DDR5 and LPDDR prices back to the old platform. Automotive electronics are slower. A 2-to-3-year automotive qualification means that even if they will pay a premium, they cannot take a seat in this node switch. Once automotive material is locked on a high-price node, exit is also slower than the phone.

### 4.4 The China market is a residual pool, not a second bid

The China market cannot save the globe. That is hardest in this volume. Analysis add. The Judgment is not denied.

More China phone and PC shipments buy residual bits, not HBM, and not advanced packaging. The more they buy, the more buyers sit inside the residual. The residual itself is first cut by the 51% that is HBM plus RDIMM. The premium Chinese consumers pay lifts domestic retail and brand margin. It does not lift TSMC HPC's plus 48%, and it does not stop the 75% firm from adding another tranche of prepayments. So "the China market is large, consumer electronics will refill itself" is old-cycle speech. This is an AI game: the scarce good sits on the cabinet, not in Honor's or Xiaomi's BOM.

The layer must be right. The phone layer eats LPDDR and consumer bits. The PC layer eats DDR sticks. The AI layer eats HBM and server sticks. Matching domestic phone shipments to HBM on global training cabinets is the wrong layer. CXMT prospectus: 0 mentions of HBM in the full text. 350k wafers per month is not an announcement. Once the international price holds, makers sell first to the high bidder. Huawei's high price is a rent unpaid to NVIDIA inside the iron curtain. It is not CXMT already nursed to readiness. Rationing blocks domestic bits from flowing out. It is not domestic makers already raised into training-cabinet supply. The near gate is an administrative lock. The far gate is non-silicon. If domestic advanced packaging and HBM-class bits can still be volume-produced far enough to hold a training cabinet, an administrative lock is enough to keep a closed loop. If they cannot, an administrative lock leaves only the phone layer.

To get AMD, Intel, Apple, Qualcomm, and MediaTek off TSMC's queue, a China fab must at the same time: reach the top node, have capacity that can take the load, and have the United States and those firms willing to hand over masks. The third item is export control and trust. It does not pass. Catch the first two, and Western flagship chips still cannot be taken. Those firms are still in TSMC's queue. In the queue they are still behind NVIDIA. More bundling by China phone makers binds memory residual. It does not change the advanced-packaging slot, and it does not change the increment lock behind 19%. What can actually change the cabinet is the bundle that makes accelerator cards and cloud ASICs, plus memory longs in real money. That check is not done. What is done is supply commitments added to $119.0B.

CXMT and YMTC are companies. The prospectus does not list HBM as a line. The main line is conventional DRAM. After the three giants draw good capacity to HBM, conventional prices are high, and CXMT makes more money doing conventional. Once the international price holds, they sell first to the high bidder. If Huawei wants high-end bits, it bids at that price. Those who can pay join the high-price peer. Those who cannot take residual. It is not that the maker is unpatriotic. It is that the maker wants profit. Cheapness alone does not fill the tax of the unified compute architecture. A high-price peer can join the high-price bid. Domestic clouds switch stacks only under cutoff and policy. Both sides are expensive. The China stack lives on people who cannot buy NVIDIA, not on cheapness punching through the unified compute architecture.

Cannot be broken means none of the five interrupts is done. See the Judgment and [Volume 8](vol-08.md). It is not a law of physics. Written as a hypothesis: if they are not done, the line walks past half of the scarce slice and recognizes only the first name. The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down. Phones and PCs pay a memory tax. One firm writes the rules.

### 4.5 Why the two old belts are not enough, and why they cannot be deleted

The old volume wrote the shock as wafer quotes plus a memory penalty. The wafer-quote belt: this pass did not recheck $20,000 to $22,000, so it must be demoted. The memory-penalty belt: this pass has 51%, 3× silicon, three books, multiply-by-cabinet, and 450 to 1,800, harder than the old volume. The two still reinforce each other: leading-edge-logic quota goes first to the buyer who can prepay; memory wafers go first to stacks and server sticks. Even if a flagship SoC can still get N3, it still faces LPDDR bits of which half have already been cut away. A sub-flagship that stays on an older node can dodge the wafer quote. It cannot dodge the residual tax. That is the boundary between "broad inflation" and "only the flagship hurts": if only the top SoC pays the leading-edge-logic tax, mid-range devices can live on mature process, and the industry becomes "the flagship hurts, volume still walks"; if the memory penalty lifts LPDDR across the board, the pain sinks to the thousand-yuan phone. The draft only supplied top-end evidence, so the old model must demote "broad inflation" to a hypothesis still to be tested. This pass's evidence pushed that hypothesis one step forward: TrendForce has already written PC OEMs and phones as the side short of quota; Huaqiangbei talk has already written the 16G stick as a 3× jump. The sink is happening. What is still missing is a brand official consecutive-decline shipment table. Without the table, do not write "mid-range is already dead."

The PC cannot stay empty. The draft gave no notebook CPU or discrete-GPU purchase price, but the belt is the same. If consumer discrete GPUs keep fighting the data center for the same generation of packaging and memory, retail will see stock-outs or price rises first, not "AI PC" software points cashed. If an OEM uses AI-PC marketing to cover SoC and memory inflation, it must be able to point at which workflow makes a user willing to pay early for the next replacement inside those 40 months. The draft has no penetration rate for that workflow, so a marketing narrative cannot fill the quantitative model's blank. Official limited supply on game cards is already the hardest sentence on the PC side. General-purpose servers have no public "shipments already extinguished" statistic. What can be written is structure: the CPU becomes a cabinet accessory; the firm that sells cabinets still has an automotive business and a Grace path, which shows they do not hand general-purpose compute to a rival. Do not write "commercial-server brands have already exited." Testable items can only stay empty: non-AI server shipments, which brands exit leading-edge SKUs. No statistic, leave it empty.

Looking back from the channel: if carrier plans and trade-in are still designed around a two-year replacement, they will misalign with a 40-month reality, and inventory and subsidy efficiency will worsen together. 40 months is the draft's paraphrase of an industry house, not a second house repeated in this pass. What can be watched is whether plan design has changed. No new percentage is needed. If channel 16G 450 to 1,800 continues, assembled PCs and brand PCs will first cut memory specs, then raise the whole device. That is an observable order, not a newly measured elasticity.

## 5. Analysis add (scenarios, the other side, testable marks)

Three shock scenarios. Analysis add. Pushed from the present. The Judgment is not denied.

Structural siphon. The HBM penalty rate lasts. Makers keep allocating wafers to stacks and server sticks. TrendForce pushes memory's share of 2027 cloud capex further to 68%; HBM contract prices in 2027 may still rise 70% to 140%. Flagship SoCs stay in the high-price band; the replacement cycle holds above the draft's 40 months. Brands lean on a few ultra-premium SKUs for margin; shipments do not return to the old scale. China demand keeps eating residual. It cannot eat the scarce slice back. The line walks past half of the scarce slice. Upgrades stop one node down.

Quota reflux. If the cloud side cuts orders in 2026–2028, advanced-packaging and DRAM wafers flow back, effective N3 supply to consumer electronics rises, and SoC and memory price rises stop or give back. This does not need the phone to suddenly become an "AI must-have." It only needs the upstream to stop monopolizing the wafer. Micron's own 10-K wrote that sentence: if HBM demand weakens and makers cut capacity back to conventional, conventional supply will rise materially. Watch items: whether the cabinet-seller's data-center revenue growth falls to single digits, whether Gaming becomes large again, whether four-cloud mixed-year capex turns from the Judgment's $211.9B-to-$213.9B order of magnitude. The draft $670B to $750B is an extrapolation, unverified, not to be treated as audited fact. Reflux is a fork, not the present. The present is supply commitments still rising.

Spec downgrade. Brands actively leave the sub-flagship on an older node, and use the draft's $155 versus $180-to-$190 gap for product layering. Shipments hold, but "flagship experience" and profit concentrate at the top. Memory specs are allowed to step down, to avoid fighting servers for the same wafer while the penalty rate lasts. PC OEMs should ask the symmetric question: which SKUs must follow N3 or N2, and which can keep a last-generation processor that is good enough. This is a hedge, not a reversal of the siphon. Phone-maker bundling at most changes residual allocation. It does not change the increment lock behind 19%.

The other side must be written fully, or the quantitative model will pretend it has already closed. Analysis add.

First other side: $180 to $190 is an analyst purchase price, not a public contract. Brands can break effective cost apart with bundling, rebates, and baseband integration; a 30% BOM share may overstate the "cash shock." This volume agrees. So the SoC price does not enter this pass's inputs.

Second other side: a longer replacement cycle has many factors. Screens and cameras already suffice, channel inventory, macroeconomics — it cannot be booked only to the AI siphon. 40 months was not repeated by a second industry house opened in this pass. This volume agrees. So replacement months stay in the draft, and are not the sole causal explanation.

Third other side: if an on-device NPU can actually run a usable assistant locally, the phone may regain a reason to replace, and siphon and pull will hedge each other on the same machine. The draft gave no on-device-model shipments or daily actives. "On-device AI saves the phone" cannot be written as already happened. This volume marks that item as still to be tested.

Fourth other side: TrendForce 51% and 47% to 68% are institutional, not maker segments. Contract-price +270% is an outlook, not a full-year ASP already landed. Some long-term agreements carry a price ceiling, which means the rise is also not unbounded. This volume agrees. Institutional notes enter the evidence layer. They are not promoted to a 10-K. A ceiling means the cloud side is also negotiating. It does not mean residual has already been protected.

Fifth other side: CXMT world fourth, YMTC world third — domestic supply will knock the residual tax down. This volume does not agree to write that as already happened. CXMT follows the international floor; the prospectus has no HBM project. YMTC fills NAND; it cannot fill that half of bits that is server DDR5. However large domestic shipments are, they do not become "Washington will hand over masks." If the Eastern bid stands, it is a parallel stack. If it does not stand, the West is left with one bidder. A parallel stack cannot save Apple's and AMD's seats at TSMC, and it cannot save Western PC brands from the residual tax.

Testable marks: write only what can be checked against a table. No new percentages. In the next TrendForce note, whether HBM plus RDIMM as a share of DRAM bits is still near 51%, and whether memory inside cloud capex still walks the 47% to 68% direction. Whether Hynix still discloses HBM revenue more than doubled in a year, and whether operating margin is still in the 49% band. Whether Micron CMBU is still expanding, and whether MCBU still walks almost sideways. Whether Samsung Memory still writes HBM, server DDR5, and enterprise SSD as priority. Whether TSMC earnings calls still have smartphone-platform revenue below HPC, and whether the first customer is still walking from 12% toward 19%. Whether the next CXMT prospectus or periodic report is still 0 mentions of HBM, and whether DDR plus LPDDR is still above 98% of main business. Whether Huaqiangbei 16G still sits near RMB 1,800, or gives back. Whether brand-report shipments and ASP walk "volume down, price up." Whether the limited-supply sentence on game cards is still in the CFO commentary. The PC needs its own BOM watch. A phone SoC price cannot stand in for it. An automotive introduction cycle of 2 to 3 years means it is not 2026's first validator. Non-AI server shipments, and which brands exit leading-edge SKUs: no statistic, leave them empty.

For brand firms, the output a quantitative model can use is not another "it will rise another x% next year." It is a layered decision. Analysis add. If the flagship still walks the leading-edge node to hold a technology image, it must admit it is fighting training chips for the same logic line. The sub-flagship actively stays on an older node, to make shipments. Memory specs are allowed to step down, to avoid fighting servers for the same wafer while the penalty rate lasts. SSD specs are likewise allowed to step down, because NAND is also written into the 47% to 68% of cloud capex. If channel and carrier subsidies are still designed around old-day volume, inventory will appear on a longer replacement cycle. None of these actions needs a new price number. They only need to admit the half of bits and the residual tax the Judgment already gave. China brands have one more layer: do not treat CXMT as an HBM spare, and do not treat domestic shipments as a second gravity. Domestic shipments are the number of buyers inside the residual pool, not a bidding function inside the scarce slice.

Actions an OEM can take that do not depend on new numbers: write SKU layering in hard, and avoid crowding the whole series onto the leading-edge node; change memory specs from "high-spec across the board" to steppable; rewrite differentiators unrelated to AI (battery, repair, channel) as margin sources again. These are hedges, not a reversal of the siphon. Reversal waits on the Judgment's forks being done. Until they are done, this volume's working hypothesis is: the line walks past half of the scarce slice, and consumer electronics keep paying the tax.

Cash in transit and retained profit must also be kept apart. Analysis add. Most of the tax consumer electronics pay does not become the brand's own retention. The tax first stops in the maker's operating margin: Hynix 49%, Samsung Memory KRW 104.1T, Micron DRAM $28.58B. Further up, the tax is locked by the cabinet-seller's prepayments into the next slot. What the brand sees is a dearer BOM, slower shipments, and misaligned plans. The profit pool is not in Honor's or Dell's phone or PC segment. It is in the cabinet, the stack, and leading-edge logic. Drawing four-cloud gross margins and brand whole-device gross margins on the same "consumer-electronics shock" map misleads: the denominators differ. The right comparison is "who can pay for the next tranche of material," not "who stickers high."

This volume's quantitative boundary stops here. What can be used: the half of bits already written, 3× silicon, three books, multiply-by-cabinet scenarios, channel talk, HPC 58% against smartphone 29%. What cannot be used: a whole-device tear-down not given, an SoC purchase price not rechecked, 40 months not repeated. Writing "by how much next year's phone ASP will rise" from here exceeds the draft, and it exceeds this task's number discipline. The layered and misaligned observations above are enough to stop this volume on the boundary "the shock mechanism can be explained; whole-device inflation cannot be precise to the next percentage point." The part of the title's quantitative model that does not match the name is "how many points of whole-device BOM inflation." The part that matches the name is: residual has already been cut into a shape, and the shape can be drawn with half the bits and multiply-by-cabinet.

## 6. Gaps and what cannot be treated as fact

1. There is no complete phone or PC BOM table, only the draft's SoC price, SoC share, and one sentence of 20% to 30%. A true whole-device cost-inflation model cannot be done. The quantification the title promised can, in this pass, only reach residual structure, not whole-device percentage points.
2. $180 to $190, $155, 25% area cost, and 40 months all come from the draft's paraphrase of analysts and TrendForce, not a contract or customs series visible to this task. They do not enter this pass's inputs.
3. PC processors, discrete GPUs, tablets, and IoT have no matching prices. The title wrote phones and PCs; the draft body is almost only phones. The official sentence this pass can add on the PC side is limited game-card supply, not a whole-device BOM.
4. "About 20% to 25% above the prior generation" does not give the prior-generation purchase price. The reader cannot back-calculate.
5. The 3:1 penalty rate is a capacity sketch of HBM versus DRAM, not a direct elasticity of phone-memory retail prices. The 10-K only writes more wafers. 3× is a conference paraphrase.
6. NVIDIA 60% of advanced packaging, Apple about 8%, is not a TSMC split. This volume does not put it in the fact layer. Phone SoCs mostly do not take advanced packaging in the first place.
7. Robot BOM percentages are unrelated to this volume's title. They cannot fill a blank in the phone model.
8. TrendForce 51%, 47% to 68%, and a contract-price outlook of +270% are institutional, not maker segments. Cloud capex +98% is a TrendForce tracked sample, not a four-cloud 10-K add.
9. Hynix and Samsung do not split DRAM and NAND amounts, and do not report full-year absolute bits. Do not convert won into dollars and then add them to Micron. The three fiscal years do not align.
10. CXMT 350k wafers per month is not in the prospectus. It is a semiconductor-analysis-house outlook. YMTC official layers are written to 200 and above; press 267 does not enter. Neither firm has an official monthly capacity in units of 10k wafers.
11. Huaqiangbei 450 to 1,800 is channel talk. It cannot be backed out into maker ASP.
12. There is no official consecutive-decline shipment table for phones, PCs, or non-AI servers. "General-purpose servers have already been extinguished" is not written.
13. Supply commitments of $119.0B are not split to Hynix or HBM. Multi-year cloud-service agreements are not memory prepayments.
14. Converting HPC 58%, 3nm 24%, and first customer 19% into tens of thousands of wafers. Revenue share is not unit share.

16. The draft $215B, $670B to $750B, and 4.1-year depreciation cannot be treated as audited fact on the Judgment and capex-scope-check reading.
