## 1. The question this essay answers

Has compute already moved from a tool to a cognitive means of production that can evolve itself? If so, on which checkable benchmarks should global compute power be measured, rather than on slogans? This essay does not forecast a ten-year endgame. It answers only three things: what the leap's mechanism is; which cells have already happened; which way the overall judgment tilts. The next seven essays split packaging, memory, cloud ASICs, siphon, energy, bubble, and endgame, all from the anchors here.

"Self-evolving" here is not a science-fiction subject. What the draft said is large-scale Transformers, MoE, and inference reinforcement learning, letting a model generate code, induce logic, and decide inside a workflow. The test is concrete: a stretch of work that used to have to be finished step by step by a mid-level employee — can a cluster now finish it continuously and accept it, under a given power budget and a given dollar of depreciation. If it cannot, compute is still a tool. If it can, rent starts moving from the wage account to the wafer, packaging, and power accounts.

Jensen Huang wrote the same thing as a factory in the FY26 full-year press release: "customers are racing to invest in AI compute — the factories powering the AI industrial revolution". That is official rhetoric, not an audit fact. It is useful because it matches cabinets, prepayments, and free cash in the filing: what the seller is already selling is not a single chip. It is a cabinet factory that can emit tokens continuously.

## 2. What the draft already argued (compressed; no key number dropped)

Three leaps. Steam and the internal-combustion engine turned fossil energy into mechanical kinetic energy. Capital substituted for muscle through coal, oil, and the factory. The 1970–2020 information revolution pressed the marginal cost of transmitting and retrieving information near zero, but PCs, phones, and general-purpose servers were still a passive extension of human brainpower, output bounded by physiology and cognitive bandwidth. 2026–2036 was written in the draft as: compute for the first time could generalize, generate code, induce logic, and decide autonomously. The diagram's endpoint is capital monopolizing wafers, packaging, HBM, and nuclear power, superclusters driving Agents, directly substituting junior and mid-level intelligence. The endpoint is a scenario, not a contract.

The four-dimension benchmark table is the draft's skeleton, and it includes forecasts. Physical process: 2026 3nm about $20,000; 2030 2nm/A16 breaking $32,000; 2036 1nm (CFET) above $45,000. The constraint was written as High-NA EUV depreciation at $380 million a tool. Packaging: 2026 CoWoS about 125,000 wafers/month; 2030 above 250,000 wafers/month; 2036 450,000 wafers/month (CoPoS). Constraint: silicon interposers breaking the reticle limit. Memory: 2026 1:3 penalty; 2030 1:4 (HBM4/4e); 2036 1:5+ (3D logic–memory bond). Capex: the draft wrote 2026 North-American four-cloud on the order of several hundred billion dollars, 2030 breaking a trillion, 2036 spend stabilizing and turning to depreciation operations. Energy: 2026 a single top campus 100~300 MW; 2030 500 MW~1.0 GW; 2036 2.0~5.0 GW. Constraint: PJM/ERCOT queues 5–7 years.

Historical quote alignment is also draft: 7nm $10,000, 5nm $16,000, 3nm $20,000~22,000, 2nm $30,000~35,000. SaaS gross margin was written as compressed from 85% to 55%. In these cells, the left side is close to already happened or cited by many parties; the right side is extrapolation. Official files opened this pass do not print 2030/2036 quotes, monthly capacity, or campus power. The right side cannot be written as "inevitable in ten years."

The qualitative conclusion the draft already wrote correctly: how compute supply is allocated will directly decide where industry profit flows and how national wealth is redistributed. What this essay must change is the measurement, not this judgment.

## 5. Mechanism (physical / commercial / geopolitical)

Physically, the condition for becoming a means of production is that a model can finish a stretch of acceptable work without step-by-step human instruction. That requires three walls to be engineered around by systems work at once, not erased by single-chip shrink. The memory wall is stacked hard with HBM and unified memory. The interconnect wall uses NVLink to fuse tens of cards into one logical chip. The power wall hard-connects liquid cooling and direct power. A computer in the tool era can wait, can slow down. A cluster in the means-of-production era must run continuously, because depreciation is taken straight-line over a few years. The opportunity cost of downtime moves from "a few fewer calculations" to "idle on the order of tens of billions of dollars a year." The draft wrote NVL72 as 13.8 TB, NVLink 1.8 TB/s, 120 kW DLC. This pass uses the official single-cabinet 13.4 TB HBM. Interconnect and cabinet power were not re-checked in this essay. Do not promote them.

Commercially, whoever controls wafers, packaging, HBM, and power collects intelligence rent. The seller's one year of free cash $96.6 billion, last two quarters' gross margin back near 75%, is rent already cashed on the seller side. Buyer mixed-year PPE lifted from about $212 billion to about $354 billion is the ticket. The necessary path for recovering the ticket is Agents substituting junior and mid-level intelligence: without enough incremental revenue, the ticket becomes a bad debt. That is a mechanism, not an already-realized labor statistic.

Geopolitically, once the means of production can continuously emit acceptable work, the object of export control moves from a product to "a machine that can produce cognition." Two ecosystems will each build a wafer–packaging–model stack on security logic. The four-dimension table's 2036 1nm, 450,000 wafers/month, trillion-class spend, multi-GW campuses all assume the world is still on one technical track. If dual track stands, the global totals in the table split into two smaller sets; the single-point premium inside each set may be higher. Dual track splits the referee corner first (CUDA versus CANN). It does not need High-NA to be copied first.

## 6. Analysis added (scenarios, counters, testable indicators)

Analysis added. The table's 2030/2036 cells are unverified. Do not treat them as audit fact.

Scenario A: the table's 2026 column roughly lands; the 2030 column lands in part. Process and packaging walk the direction capital can stack. Buyer spend does not reach the draft's right-hand bin, because firms must first prove usage. Energy walks to 500 MW-class campuses, but 1 GW belongs only to a few direct-power sites. This is "means-of-production happens; scale is more conservative than the table."

Scenario B: algorithm breakthrough. The table's right side fails as a whole. A class of work interrupts the pathological demand for HBM and interconnect. Edge devices carry everyday intelligence. Infrastructure decays into a thin-margin utility. Labor substitution still happens, but rent moves from chips to scene and data. The draft's right-hand bin of annual spend becomes overbuild.

Scenario C: dual track. The table becomes two more expensive, smaller tables. High-NA, Japanese materials, CoWoS single points are rebuilt as strategic inventory on both sides. The global sum rises. Unilateral usable volume falls.

Counters: using the word "self-evolving" to imply a model already has unconstrained subjectivity. What the draft wrote is a workflow, not a subject in the legal-person sense. Counters also include: writing the draft's 2026 four-cloud spend bin as already happened; calling 2024 mixed-year equipment purchases "AI hardware" and linearly extrapolating to 2036. Neither holds.

Testable indicators: whether public 3nm/2nm quotes walk the draft's $20,000 to above $30,000; whether CoWoS is still written by TSMC as unable to meet demand, and whether packaging-and-test share stays above 10%; whether HBM's wafer occupancy of DRAM is still named by both makers and buyers; whether four-cloud PPE parks near mixed-year 2025 about $354 billion, or steps up again after three-name guidance plus Microsoft; whether top-campus interconnect power leaves the 100~300 MW band; whether supply commitments park at $119.0 billion or keep adding. If two columns clearly fail inside five years, the right-hand endgame should be rewritten, not explained away with "it still holds in the long run."

The four-dimension table has one more use: it is a filter that refuses empty talk. Any "ten-year megatrend" speech that cannot give at least two checkable numbers from process quotes, packaging tightness, HBM occupancy, cloud PPE, and campus power should not be written into this monograph's judgment. Whoever writes an extrapolation as already happened is using the master outline to supply rhetoric for a bubble.

Becoming a means of production does not automatically equal a welfare improvement. When rent rises, countries without quota and firms without workflows become price-takers: they pay more wafer, packaging, and power for the same unit of intelligence. Essay 1 is therefore not a cheer for the revolution. It fixes the revolution's unit of measure, for the next seven essays to take apart item by item.
