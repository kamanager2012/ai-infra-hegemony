# Volume 35: The AI energy war, deep cut (Three Mile Island 835 MW PPA, Susquehanna direct connect, and SMR evidence)

Chinese original: [vol-35.md](vol-35.md)

Whether power can stop the buildout, and whether it has been done, is in [Volume 13](vol-13.md). This volume does not retell the five interrupts, and does not retell antitrust. It only nails the three contracts that can be matched in public, and why the queue can no longer treat "2,600 GW" as a sum of three markets.

## 1. The question this volume answers

The mechanism of the power interrupt is already in Volume 13. This volume writes only the evidence. Queues, direct-connect nuclear, and SMRs are constraints on energizing a site. They are not already a lock. Writing "power has already shut the hegemony" denies the present. Writing "power is a fork not yet done" is a push from the present.

This volume nails three things. First, after a single cabinet jumps from air cooling to whole-cabinet liquid cooling, is the constraint still the chip. Second, when Microsoft, Amazon, and Google buy nuclear power, do they buy watts, carbon attributes, or interconnection rights. Third, can regulation finish the power path for the buyer: the case file is [Volume 11](vol-11.md); a fine does not change main-transformer approval. Power and regulation are two clocks.

The sales unit has already moved from the chip to the cabinet. Official cabinet: 72 GPU, 36 Grace, HBM 13.4 TB, LPDDR 17 TB. The NVIDIA DGX GB cabinet user guide writes: whole-cabinet power about 120 kW; the power shelf uses six air-cooled 5.5 kW supplies, eight shelves in N+N, 33 kW input per shelf. That is the energy war inside the cabinet. Outside the campus is another war: whoever first gets executable baseload and interconnection can turn cabinets in a warehouse into running assets. The two wars are not the same thing, and they cannot substitute for each other.

## 2. What the draft already argued, what public sources can keep, and what must be demoted

### 2.1 The cabinet heat island: what official text can keep, and the draft arithmetic

What NVIDIA product pages and the user guide can match is the cabinet form, not campus megawatt-hours. 72 Blackwell, 36 Grace, liquid cooling, busbars, cold plates, whole cabinet about 120 kW. The power shelf turns AC into about 50–51 V DC, then the busbar splits it to compute trays and NVLink switch trays. Compute trays themselves are liquid-cooled; networking and storage still use fans. The draft wrote legacy air cooling as 5～10 kW, the liquid-cooling subsystem as 1.5%～2% of whole-cabinet BOM, and passive copper saving 20 kW per cabinet versus optics. This pass has no official tear-down. All of that is marked "draft numbers" and is not promoted. 500 cabinets, 60～80 MW, annual consumption over 600 million kWh, about equal to 60,000 US households, is draft arithmetic, not operating hours checked in this pass. Do not treat it as an audit fact.

Analysis added here. The meaning of 120 kW is not a slogan. Feeders and main transformers must move from "a small slice of the campus" to "a dedicated line plus a main transformer plus backup." A 5～10 kW air-cooled cabinet can live on room air-conditioning and ordinary low-voltage feeders. 120 kW means copper busbars, CDUs, cold plates, and quick-disconnects must enter the cabinet. Every kilowatt saved, if true, is not an environmental ad. It is more compute on the same feeder and the same CDU. Energy efficiency at cabinet scale converts directly into deployable GPU count. That is the same sentence as the judgment's "the sales unit moved up to the cabinet": what is sold is no longer one chip's thermal design power. It is the electricity, cooling, and interconnect one cabinet must satisfy at once.

### 2.2 Three nuclear contracts: what official releases can nail, and what the draft added

The three contracts are this volume's evidence. They must be written in the companies' own sentences, not promoted from second-hand compilations.

Microsoft and Constellation. 2024-09-20, Constellation official press release and 8-K: a 20-year power purchase agreement to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1, renamed Crane Clean Energy Center, adding about 835 MW of carbon-free power to the grid; Microsoft buys the plant's output to match load at its PJM data centers. Restart and delivery under the contract must pass Nuclear Regulatory Commission safety and environmental review, plus state and local permits. A separate license-renewal filing would extend operations at least to 2054. Expected ready to operate in 2028. Unit 1 is a separate facility from Unit 2 of the 1979 accident. Before retirement the plant was rated 837 MW, and in its last year capacity factor was 96.3%. Utility Dive / CNBC citing investor materials: Constellation plans to spend about $1.6 billion to restart, aiming at the federal 45Y clean-electricity tax credit. Utility Dive also citing a Constellation spokesperson: no plan to build a Microsoft data center at the plant. A Brattle Group economic-impact study commissioned by Pennsylvania building trades writes: 3,400 direct and indirect jobs, $16 billion added to state GDP, more than $3 billion in state and federal tax. That $16 billion is a state-GDP impact study, not the PPA consideration. Part II wrote the amount as "over $16 billion" and implied a contract price. The scope is wrong. This volume does not follow it.

Amazon and Talen / Susquehanna. 2024-03, Talen sold the Cumulus data-center campus to AWS for $650 million (close $350 million, plus $300 million in milestones). The campus sits against the Susquehanna nuclear plant. Public materials write the plant at about 2.5 GW gross capacity; World Nuclear News writes 2,475 MWe, with a Talen subsidiary holding 90%. Combined redundant substation capacity at the campus can reach 960 MW. AWS contract power commitments climb in 120 MW steps, starting in 2025; a one-time option caps the commitment at 480 MW; two further ten-year extensions hang on the two units' 2042 and 2044 license renewals. This is version one of "move the data center inside the plant fence." It is not a behind-the-meter lock that the two parties can finish by handshake.

FERC sent version one back a step. 2024-11-01, FERC denied 2-1 a revised interconnection service agreement PJM had filed. The revision would have expanded behind-the-meter colocation from the existing 300 MW to 480 MW. The majority wrote: such colocated-load arrangements implicate reliability and consumer cost, and PJM had not shown the need to depart from the standard agreement. 2025-04, FERC denied rehearing. 2025-02, FERC separately opened a show-cause on large colocated load inside PJM, finding the current tariff appeared not to write the price and terms of colocation clearly. 2025-06-11, Talen Exhibit 99.1: the parties signed a new PPA, full amount 1,920 MW, through 2042, extendable; the ramp reaches full no later than 2032. Existing colocated load is to become a "front-of-the-meter" arrangement after transmission reconfiguration in the 2026 spring refueling window: plant power goes onto the PJM grid first, Talen supplies at retail, PPL handles transmission and distribution. The same release also writes: the parties will explore new SMRs inside Pennsylvania's footprint and uprates, intending to add net power to PJM. In the same release Amazon claims a $20 billion Pennsylvania investment and 1,250 high-skill jobs. That is the company's own investment narrative, not a capex line item checked in this volume.

Google and Kairos Power. 2024-10-14, Google official blog and Kairos press release: a power-purchase arrangement for multiple small reactors, self-described as the first enterprise-side agreement "to buy power from multiple SMRs." First unit targeted for 2030 interconnection, then deployment through 2035, totaling at most 500 MW of around-the-clock carbon-free power. Kairos writes fluoride-salt cooling and ceramic pebble-bed fuel (TRISO), low-pressure operation. Official releases do not write "7 units." A same-day Reuters item wrote "six to seven units." That is a reporter conversion, not a unit count on either party's signature page. This volume nails only 500 MW, first unit 2030, and the 2035 window. It does not write 7 units as official fact. Kairos's non-power Hermes demonstration reactor in Tennessee has an NRC construction permit and has broken ground. That is a demonstration reactor, not a 500 MW commercial fleet already under construction.

### 2.3 The interconnection queue: "2,600 GW" can no longer be treated as a sum of three markets

The draft wrote PJM, ERCOT, and CAISO queues as more than 2,600 GW, substation and high-voltage dedicated-line approval and construction 5～7 years, data-center vertical construction 12～18 months. 2,600 GW cannot be matched in this pass to any official sum. It does not enter the fact layer. Vertical construction 12～18 months and approval 5～7 years remain draft clocks. This pass has no unified average cycle across the three RTOs. Mark them "draft numbers."

What can be kept is public queues by market, and it must be written that "in queue is not under construction." ERCOT around June 2026: large-load interconnection requests about 438,000 MW (press release) to about 474 GW (Senate materials), of which about 90% are data centers; Texas historical peak load 85,508 MW (2023-08-10). PUCT 2026-06-18 approved Batch Zero: large loads of 75 MW and above move to batch studies, no longer one-off reviews; about 205 GW qualify for the first batch from existing studies; a first-batch transmission-plan draft is expected autumn 2027. 2026-08-03 the Texas governor asked that new data-center interconnections be paused; ERCOT was asked to finish an audit by December 10. Large loads that have already had a stability study and plan to come online within the year: about 17, combined peak about 6.6 GW, taking about five years to ramp full. PJM's first generation queue after reform is reported at about 201.5～220 GW; PJM's own load-growth narrative writes at most about 70 GW by 2038, mainly data centers. Generation projects studied since 2020 exceed 300 GW; those holding interconnection agreements are only about 51 GW. Queue capacity and executable interconnection are not the same number.

Analysis added here. What matters for AI is not "how many GW sit in the queue." It is when a high-voltage dedicated line and a main transformer can actually energize a campus of 120 kW cabinets. GPU generations are 2～3 years. Financial depreciation is 4 years. Nuclear permits and main transformers often outlast both beats. Power's clock is slower than the chip's clock. That is the "energy war." Writing "already no power" exceeds the present.

## 3. Mechanism (physics / commerce / geopolitics)

### 3.1 Three clocks do not line up. Idle depreciation arrives before a blackout

The core of the energy war is three clocks that do not line up. Chip generations 2～3 years, financial depreciation 4 years, main transformers and nuclear permits often longer than both beats, and a power contract once signed is 20 years. Whoever decides on the wrong clock goes bankrupt on the other two. Buying NVL72 on the chip clock and queuing on the utility clock produces cabinets built, feeders not arrived, depreciation spinning empty. Locking 835 MW on a 20-year power contract and then failing to absorb baseload in a year when inference demand is cut produces power longer and heavier than the chips. Requiring each campus to pay back in the year under 4-year depreciation produces a refusal to touch nuclear, a permanent stop at the hundred-megawatt class, and a permanent place at the back of the queue.

That a data center can be built in 12～18 months is a draft clock. Even if true, it is not 12～18 months to power. The vertical-construction clock deceives capex decisions. Before energization, annual kWh is all zero, and depreciation still runs on 4 years. The energy war looks like generation. In accounting it looks more like preventing idle time. Whoever can compress the gap between "built" and "energized" to shorter than one GPU generation destroys less of the group-wide spend that has already happened. Analysis added here: this is the same mechanism as the judgment's "without power, this batch of assets will idle through the depreciation life." The only change is that $215 billion can no longer be used to push an annual-depreciation midpoint.

Direct-connecting existing piles (Three Mile Island restart, Susquehanna behind-the-fence) is a shortcut inside the third clock: use a restart of an existing pile, or load inside the fence, to compress a new pile's ten years into the length of a permit revision. SMRs admit the shortcut is not enough and buy 2030–2035. Neither is a "green-power story." Both privatize interconnection rights out of the public queue. The draft wrote nuclear operators into the 60%～75% gross-margin band for this reason: when the queue itself becomes a commodity, whoever holds an executable permit starts collecting land rent. This pass has no official gross-margin split for nuclear operators. 60%～75% is not promoted. Only the mechanism is kept.

### 3.2 835 MW, 960 MW, and 500 MW do not buy the same thing

Three Mile Island 835 MW is a restart of an existing pressurized-water reactor. Power goes onto PJM first. Microsoft uses a PPA to match its data-center load in that region. It buys baseload and carbon attributes, not a dedicated busbar at the plant gate. Constellation itself says there is no behind-the-fence plan. By arithmetic, 835 MW is about the power cap of ten 80 MW mid-size campuses. Capacity factor is unverified. Do not treat the multiplication as available output. Microsoft is not buying a REC game. It is buying baseload watts that skip part of the "new generation plus new dedicated line" queue. It still has to pass the NRC. It still may not deliver until 2028. Signed in 2024, possible delivery in 2028: a generation and a half of GPUs in between.

Susquehanna version one was closer to a physical direct connect: campus inside the fence, substation redundant capacity written to 960 MW, contract climbing in 120 MW steps, cap option at 480 MW. After FERC denied the revision from 300 MW to 480 MW, direct connect was no longer something a private contract could finish alone. The June 2025 new contract lifted the full amount to 1,920 MW and at the same time put the physical path back front-of-the-meter: power onto the grid first, then retail and the transmission company to the load. The number got larger. The legal form retreated from "bypass the public queue" to "use the public grid." Analysis added here: this is the most important correction in this volume. Direct-connect nuclear is not already-privatized interconnection rights. It is an attempt that regulation sent back and then rewrote as a front-of-the-meter long contract. 960 MW is campus planned capacity. 1,920 MW is the new contract's full amount. Neither can be written as "continuous output already delivered to the cabinet."

Google's 500 MW is a forward order book. First unit 2030, fleet written through 2035, technology path fluoride-salt reactor plus TRISO, almost no continuous commercial-operation sample in history. The commercial-operation window itself is a risk disclosure. Google going to SMRs equals admitting that the first half of this decade has not enough new piles to interconnect, so it can only sign forward. TVA later wrote the Hermes 2 demonstration as a PPA of at most 50 MW. That is demonstration scale, not 500 MW already on the ground.

The three contracts are therefore not a completed tense of "the cloud giants have already gone nuclear." One is a matching PPA on an existing pile that may restart only in 2028. One is a front-of-the-meter long contract FERC rerouted. One is a modular-reactor order book after 2030. For power to stop the buildout, watts have to fail to reach the cabinet. What fails to arrive now is permits, reconfiguration, and first concrete, not a global blackout.

### 3.3 Compute rent is cabinet rent. Power is a second layer of rent

Rent: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). Supply commitments: [Volume 1](vol-01.md). Analysis added here. Power is a second layer of rent, not a line item inside the first. The first layer is paid to the athlete and upstream makers. The second is paid to nuclear operators, transmission companies, and state permits. Writing the second layer as already having replaced the first is writing a fork not yet done as an allocation already rewritten. However large the power contract, it does not change the sentence "TSMC has no financial reason to offend the first name for the second." Power can only decide: the material the first name locked, is there a place to plug it in.

ERCOT's spot and PJM's capacity market are different structures. The same 20-year PPA hedges different risks in the two markets: one fears price spikes more, the other fears capacity shortfall. This volume does not write the two markets' spreads as numbers. Firms writing "we bought green power" as one story covers that difference until the bill clears at the nodal price.

### 3.4 Antitrust does not change a main transformer. National security has no order that says "therefore do not split power"

The case file: [Volume 11](vol-11.md). National-security files: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). A fine does not change the bid, and it does not change the NRC timetable of one 835 MW unit. Writing "regulation is about to open the market, and the power constraint will open with it" as a timetable exceeds the public record. The Pennsylvania governor, in the Talen new contract, said "more power is more national security." That is a state-government political sentence, not a White House-issued "do-not-split order" or "must-protect-direct-connect order."

When FERC denied the Susquehanna expansion agreement, the chair's dissent wrote: blocking colocation puts a roadblock in front of an industry national security needs. The majority cared more about reliability and consumer cost. On the same nuclear plant, the national-security narrative and the grid-fairness narrative are already fighting inside one order. Analysis added here. A large load bypassing the public queue privatizes the scarcest interconnection right inside queue capacity. If regulation later blesses it, more Cumulus-style acquisitions are encouraged. If regulation later denies it, planned capacity has to fall back into the public queue. Both outcomes enter the clouds' depreciation models, with opposite signs. The June 2025 rewrite to front-of-the-meter 1,920 MW is a middle state of that fight, not an end state.

### 3.5 Why the power fork is still not done

Judgment section 7: acting alone means handing over each year's new lines and packaging. To change the allocation, bind three checks: prepayments large enough that the foundry cannot recognize only one buyer; software bound into one stack (UXL / oneAPI is a seed); memory longs that leave a line for commodity bits. Miss one check and you are still picked off one by one. Power is not any of those three checks. Power is another line: even after the material is in hand, without energization the cabinet is still a liquid-cooled warehouse. Making "power stops the buildout" does not take three press releases. It takes enough campuses failing on permits and main transformers that even the first name's prepayments have nowhere to become running cabinets.

What is visible now is the opposite middle state. Buyers are adding group-wide capex: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). Seller supply commitments: [Volume 1](vol-01.md). Nuclear contracts are being signed, but 835 MW waits on 2028, 500 MW waits on 2030–2035, and 1,920 MW ramps through 2032. ERCOT has many applications and little delivery. That is a constraint, not yet a lock.

China grid line items are empty: [Volume 12](vol-12.md). "China nuclear installed capacity is large" cannot be used to fill "global cabinets have already lost power." Cloud ASICs: [Volume 4](vol-04.md). In-house chips do not save a 120 kW cabinet's need for a main transformer. Writing "we make our own chips" as "we do not lack power" welds two forks into one.

Restarting existing piles is also not an industrial process already rolled out. CNBC citing Constellation: if Three Mile Island returns in 2028, it will be the second commercial pile restarted in US history, with Michigan Palisades written as the first and targeted earlier. Both still have to pass the NRC. Constellation's press release writes that restart requires replacing the turbine, generator, main transformer, and cooling controls; the company separately filed to extend operations at least to 2054. The about $1.6 billion in investor materials is restart capex, not the total Microsoft pays Constellation under the PPA. The two sums cannot be swapped. Brattle's $16 billion is a state-GDP impact study, a third sum, still less a contract. Three numbers appearing in the same news chain is the place this volume must cut.

A matching PPA and a plant-side direct connect must also be cut apart. Microsoft buys carbon matching inside the PJM region; the spokesperson said clearly there is no behind-the-fence plan. Amazon buys a campus inside the fence plus a later-rerouted front-of-the-meter long contract. Google buys a modular-reactor order book not yet built. All three are "buying nuclear." The bundle of rights they bought is not the same. Welding three press releases into "the clouds have already gone nuclear" covers legal form with a verb. FERC has already shown: legal form can send a direct connect back on the last centimeter of an expansion.

### 3.6 The 20 kW inside the cabinet and the 835 MW outside the campus are two scales of the same war

The draft wrote passive copper saving 20 kW per cabinet versus optics. This pass has no official comparison experiment. 20 kW does not enter the fact layer. Only the mechanism is kept: every kilowatt saved inside the cabinet is more compute on an existing feeder; every megawatt bought outside the campus is to make the feeder itself appear. A firm that does only one of the two is exposed on the other. The NVIDIA user guide writes the whole cabinet at about 120 kW, the power shelves as eight shelves, 33 kW per shelf, N+N. That means redundant power itself is a heat load. Liquid cooling solves the compute tray. It does not solve the campus-side main transformer. Whoever only changes cold plates and does not change interconnection is doing a local optimization on a feeder already built.

Cabinets times spec: [Volume 16](vol-16.md). If the power constraint is truly done, the product stops at a smaller cabinet count. If it only stops in the application queue, the product keeps walking and residual bits keep being pulled. This volume does not write any cabinet-count tier as shipments that already happened.

Of the four firms, only three walk into this volume's nuclear evidence. Meta has no matching public restart or SMR-fleet release in this pass's search. "The four clouds are all buying nuclear" cannot be written as fact. Constellation calls itself the largest US nuclear company, total installed 55 GW; Talen calls itself about 10.7 GW of generation assets, of which nuclear about 2.2 GW. Both sentences are company self-introductions, used to show who still holds existing piles, not available output checked in this volume. The Talen new contract also writes that the long contract will reduce the company's reliance on the federal nuclear production tax credit; Constellation investor materials write the Three Mile Island restart as aimed at 45Y. The tax credit is part of the restart economics. It is not a power-price formula Microsoft or Amazon has already paid on someone else's behalf. This pass did not read a power-price formula. Leave it empty.

## 4. Added analysis (scenarios, objections, testable checks)

Analysis added here. Power-volume extrapolations are unverified. Do not treat them as audit facts. Scenarios are not probabilities. They are forks from the present.

Scenario A: direct connect and restarts become standard kit for a few clouds, and the public queue keeps worsening. Restarts of existing piles like Three Mile Island, and behind-the-fence sites rewritten as front-of-the-meter long contracts like Susquehanna, are copied onto a limited number of old sites. Winners are operators who already hold nuclear assets, and clouds that can sign a 20-year contract. Losers are mid-size colos that can only queue. 120 kW cabinets keep entering campuses, but campus count is capped by the power ceiling. Supply commitments still rise. The first name still locks material. Only some of the locked material has to wait for power in a warehouse. That is not the hegemony shutting. It is acceleration cut by a slice. The objection: if FERC writes front-of-the-meter arrangements as a copyable standard tariff, the scarce rent of direct connect is diluted, and nuclear operators cannot collect "interconnection-rights land rent," only ordinary long-term power sales.

Scenario B: SMRs slip, and 2026–2030 produces "chips waiting for power." If Kairos 500 MW slides out of 2030–2035, Google can only return to the queue, gas peaking, or buy another matching PPA on an existing pile. If NRC review of Three Mile Island slips past 2028, Microsoft's 20-year contract is a paper that has not yet delivered. If the Talen 1,920 MW ramp stops at the front-of-the-meter reconfiguration, the 960 MW campus plan has to walk at the public grid's pace. The objection: if FHR commercializes on schedule, it gives copyable modular baseload, and only then can 500 MW-class campuses in the second half of the decade stand. The draft wrote 2036 top campuses at 2.0～5.0 GW. Without nuclear or equivalent baseload, that end-state does not hold. 2.0～5.0 GW is a draft forecast, unverified.

Scenario C: algorithms and low-power architectures pull power density back from 120 kW. MLA compresses memory. Edge small models shunt inference. Cabinets return to air cooling or 40 kW-class liquid cooling. Then a 20-year nuclear PPA becomes an over-hedge. The objection: the judgment's training-cluster direction is larger unified memory and higher cabinet power, and public next-generation cloud ASICs have not retreated to 5 nm plus commodity DDR5. No firm says the next cabinet will return to air cooling. Writing "efficiency will save power" as already happened exceeds the present.

Scenario D: regulation welds power and a split together, and still cannot finish it. The case file: [Volume 11](vol-11.md). What is alleged is tying, not a main transformer. A fine does not change that 835 MW in 2028. FERC has already shown: a national-security sentence and consumer cost can fight on opposite faces of the same order. A true CUDA split is another line, [Volume 8](vol-08.md). This volume does not write it as already started.

One comparison often missed: the duration mismatch between prepayments and power contracts. Most supply commitments are payable through fiscal 2027. They lock the next wafer, packaging, and inventory. A nuclear PPA once signed is 20 years. It locks watts and carbon attributes. Prepayments paid in 2027 may buy cabinets that only have 835 MW to match in 2028, a first SMR to hope for in 2030, and a possible full 1,920 MW in 2032. In the years between, cabinets walk on 4-year depreciation, and power walks on permits. Whoever writes the two timetables as one "already-locked energy war" covers the progressive tense with the completed tense.

Testable checks, only those whose right or wrong can be seen on the public record:

- Whether Three Mile Island Unit 1 actually restarts around 2028, and whether Microsoft buys matching power near 835 MW under the 20-year contract; whether a behind-the-fence build appears contrary to the official statement.
- Whether the Susquehanna front-of-the-meter reconfiguration finishes in the 2026 spring refueling window; whether 1,920 MW climbs on the disclosed pace, rather than stopping at the denied 480 MW direct connect.
- Whether Kairos still claims first unit 2030, the 2035 window, and at most 500 MW in total; if official text writes a concrete unit count, then it enters the fact layer.
- Whether ERCOT Batch Zero produces a transmission plan in autumn 2027, and how much of the 474 GW of requests is marked allocatable load; whether the governor's pause becomes a formal ban.
- Whether capacity holding interconnection agreements in PJM's reformed generation queue still sits near the 51 GW order of magnitude.
- Whether equipment purchases in the four clouds' next earnings releases keep rising on a group-wide scope; whether anyone begins to break out "power-contract lives longer than 4-year hardware depreciation."
- Whether justice.gov shows *United States v. NVIDIA*; whether France issues a communication des griefs; whether the EU issues an Article 101/102 case press release. If not, it is still "inquiries exist, complaints do not."

## 5. Gaps and what cannot be written as fact

What this volume read is the Constellation 2024-09-20 press release and 8-K, Talen 2024-03 deal paraphrases and the 2025-06-11 Exhibit 99.1, the Google / Kairos 2024-10-14 official releases, the NVIDIA DGX GB user guide, ERCOT / PUCT 2026 public materials, Reuters and commission releases on FERC 2024-11 and later proceedings, and numbers already nailed in the judgment, [Volume 11](vol-11.md), and [Volume 8](vol-08.md). An "official press release" cannot be promoted to a checked contract.

What cannot be written as already happened:

- Power has already stopped the buildout, or has already shut the hegemony.
- The Microsoft Three Mile Island contract consideration is "over $16 billion" — that is Brattle's state-GDP impact, not the agreement price.
- Google officially ordered 7 SMR units — official text only writes at most 500 MW, multiple units, 2030–2035.
- PJM + ERCOT + CAISO queue 2,600 GW.
- Four-cloud 2024 AI-hardware capex = $215 billion; 2026 $670～750 billion.
- $193.7 billion all from the four clouds, or four-cloud capex and data-center revenue can be crossed into a rent rate.
- A Justice Department complaint, a settlement, a Mellanox split, a forced-open CUDA; a French statement of objections or a fine; an EU Article 101/102 case or a 10% fine.
- The White House or the Pentagon has already issued text forbidding a split, or already issued text guaranteeing nuclear direct connect.
- 835 MW, 960 MW, 1,920 MW, 500 MW are already verified annual operating hours.
- 500 cabinets, 60～80 MW, annual 600 million kWh, 60,000 households; liquid-cooling BOM $40,000～55,000; copper saving 20 kW. The above are draft numbers or arithmetic.
- China and Europe grid queues, nuclear permits, data-center load — this volume has no addable official split.
- Nuclear operators already enjoy 60%～75% gross margin.

Power changes whether the cabinet can turn. FERC explains: a direct connect can be signed, and it can be sent back. Power is a fork not yet done, not a gate already down. The five: [Volume 8](vol-08.md).
