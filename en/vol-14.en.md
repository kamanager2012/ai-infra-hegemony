## 1. The question this volume answers

Who in this round actually collects profit, and who is only larger in revenue while cash is sucked into halls and a residual tax? The draft of Volume 14 was boilerplate. The old expansion redrew the map as a four-layer industry catalog: semiconductors / cloud / SaaS / devices. Audit and the master judgment already changed the question. Winners are first the four corners themselves, and the hardware revenue, not a second profit pool in models. Next are the three memory makers and advanced packaging that the coach pulls along and that reprice to the floor. After that, power counterparties who can lock baseload into a long contract. The first wave of losers is phones, PCs, commercial PCs, and general-purpose servers that take the residual, plus CPUs whose profit center has been moved — a demotion, not an extinction. Traditional SaaS "gross margin compressed from 85% to 55%" has no company sample and no year. It does not enter this volume's fact layer. Extinction of consumer electronics as a class is marked as a scenario. It is not written as a loser list that already happened.

What this volume nails is not another copy of the income statement. The margin monopoly is in [Volume 1](vol-01.md). The increment in HPC and booked 19% are in [Volume 4](vol-04.md). The binding line at CoWoS is in [Volume 24](vol-24.md). The four clouds cannot be used as a profit-pool denominator; the scope is in [Volume 8](vol-08.md). This volume only draws who collects rent and who pays the residual tax.

### 3.5 Winners: four-corner hardware, plus the three who follow the price, plus packaging

Analysis addendum. Draw the map on the four corners, not on an industry catalog.

The athlete wins on the cabinet. FY26 data center $193.7B is the rent the world paid. Free cash $96.6B says most of that rent stayed. Asset-light: capex is tiny versus revenue; residual value is mostly not on its own balance sheet. The referee wins on the switching tax: a unified compute architecture is a ten-year developer stack. UXL and oneAPI exist. They are digging. They have not dug through. Hardware can be cheap. The switching tax is still there. A 75% pad means it can also cut price. The coach wins on the mix: Hopper to Blackwell to Rubin, with HBM, advanced packaging, and interconnect scheduled together. The model player has products. That is not the winners' main book. Writing "they train models" as a second profit pool splits the four corners into an ordinary chip firm and an ordinary software firm. The question leans.

The three memory makers who follow the price are the second ring of winners. SK hynix FY2025 official press release: revenue KRW 97.1467T, operating profit KRW 47.2063T, operating margin 49%, net profit KRW 42.9479T. Prior-year control: revenue KRW 66.1930T, operating profit KRW 23.4673T, operating margin 35%. One year from 35% to 49% is a snapshot of HBM pulling memory from a bulk cycle toward a "compute attachment." It is not a permanent tax regime. Hynix itself wrote that HBM revenue more than doubled in a year. New fabs are named for AI. Samsung Memory and Micron actively moving wafers and high-value capacity toward cabinets is the same floor. After the three turn to high price, the residual is thinner. A few dozen brands split what is left. That holds up "no bundle, no commodity-bit long." Once memory walks from shortage back to bulk, 49% can snap back to a 2023-style loss year. Whether chip rent is terminal depends on whether the penalty ratio is still there, not on one year of operating profit.

Packaging is the third ring. TSMC packaging and test from 8% to slightly over 10%. C.C. Wei said it is so tight it limits customer growth, and that the majority is still chip-on-wafer-on-substrate. Advanced-packaging revenue share is still far smaller than wafers. The gate is here. Sell-side puts NVIDIA at about 60%. Once past half, what the second name wants is not a bit more money. It is whether a slot exists. Packaging houses and substrate vendors pulled along eat the overflow. They eat the slice the coach cannot schedule. They do not eat the referee.

Power is a fourth-ring candidate, not a nuclear-rent list already cashed. The master judgment writes "power stops the buildout" as one of five interrupts. It has not won. Counterparties who can sign a baseload long have a chance to collect scarce rent. Unit megawatts and PPA dollars that cannot be written do not enter the fact layer. The draft pointed at 835 MW and 960 MW. This pass does not recheck them. They are not a completed winner cell. Power is a constraint, not the four corners themselves.

### 3.6 First-wave losers: the residual tax, and CPUs that were demoted

Analysis addendum. The first wave is not "manufacturing digitalization paying cloud rent." It is PCs, commercial PCs, general-purpose servers, phones, and game cards, competing with cabinets for the same slice of packaging and the same slice of high-price memory. Those who cannot compete pay a residual tax: longer lead times, higher bit prices, longer replacement. Those who can still get a node down and a bit down. Brand extinction stays a scenario. No names. Not written as a loser list that already happened.

Phones are still here. TSMC's smartphone platform in 2025 was still 29%, still up 11% year on year. The river did not dry. What changed is the increment: HPC up 48%, revenue share from 51% to 58%. Flagships keep paying leading-edge ASP. Mid and low end go back to mature nodes. Draft SoC quotes, controller share of BOM above 30%, replacement beyond 40 months — this pass did not recheck the original reports. They stay draft claims. What can be nailed is the platform table and the packaging sentences: phones are still here, but they are not who the coach feeds first.

PCs and game cards have already been conceded by the athlete. Consumer accelerators and the data center queue in the same supply-commitment book. Official text writes Blackwell and Ada supply as limited. That is not an analyst guess. PCs pay a memory spillover tax, plus missing discrete GPUs. Commercial PCs still have to pass security management and long-cycle driver certification. Advanced packaging will not yield this slice. General-purpose servers and AI cabinets are not the same order, but they compete for memory makers' wafers. TrendForce official copy: 51% of 2026 DRAM bits are HBM plus RDIMM. Registered DIMMs are already inside that half "for cabinets." If a general-purpose server is still buying DDR5 RDIMMs, it is buying residual after the pull, and the price follows the residual. Official cabinet: 13.4 TB plus 17 TB. 10,000 cabinets is 134 PB of HBM. That is the product page multiplied. It is not a shipment forecast.

CPUs are demoted, not extinguished. Data-center revenue has already left gaming and automotive far behind. Grace is still on the cabinet BOM, 36 versus 72 accelerators. It is an accessory. The profit center is not there. Intel 1999 share-era rent does not map onto selling cabinets today. Applied in this volume: CPU vendors are still here, but they are accessories. Writing "already dead" crosses the scenario line.

Traditional seat-license software gross margin from 85% to 55% is a draft assertion. It names no 10-K and no year. The mechanism is plausible: seat-license marginal cost is near zero; an agent has to write every inference into COGS. No sample, and it does not enter the fact layer. Whoever only announces an assistant product and does not change revenue recognition and COGS structure is sticking a label on the old profit pool. This volume does not draw that sentence onto the winner-loser map.

### 3.7 Clouds are buyers. The depreciation book is harder

The four clouds are scale winners and conditional profit winners, not sellers bidding against 75%. Capex scope is in [四云Capex口径核对](四云Capex口径核对.md). ## 4. Analysis addendum (scenarios, objections, testable marks)

Analysis addendum. Push forward from the numbers now on the table. No extra probabilities. A four-scenario probability table does not enter the fact layer.

Scenario A: bottleneck rent continues. NVIDIA gross margin stays near 71% to 75%; Q4 was already back to 75%. Hynix operating margin stays high. TSMC HPC holds the first platform. Packaging and test stays above 10%. C.C. Wei still says tight. Prepayments keep rising. About 100k cabinets take more than half of this packaging line. The first wave keeps paying the residual tax. Phone revenue at 29% is still there. New lines and packaging go to cabinets first. Consumer-electronics brand extinction is still only a scenario, not a base-case fact.

Scenario B: clouds digest internally. The four clouds run past depreciation on ads, office, and internal recommendation. External APIs can lose; internal efficiency still counts as a win. External model firms fare worse. Cloud ASICs stay on 3nm plus HBM and bid against 75%. They do not retreat to commodity memory. Scale winners are still the four clouds. Profit winners are still four-corner hardware.

Scenario C: algorithm deflation. Training and inference costs move down the DeepSeek path. The draft wrote KV-cache compression 93.3% and pretraining converted at about $5.576M. This volume did not recheck the technical report. Hardware ASP is under pressure. Winners move from chips to applications that can still lock data and workflow. This scenario only holds if cabinet prices and prepayments come down together. Supply commitments rose $23.8B in three months. The direction is the opposite.

Scenario D: hard landing. The cell where calls rise and unit price falls appears. Destocking. Memory and foundry are hurt before software. Hynix 49% returns to a cyclical loss. TSMC HPC share falls back. The first wave briefly gets mature nodes flowing back. What flows back is a node down, not same-generation advanced packaging.

Objection 1: clouds are the biggest winners, because customers cannot leave. 2024 and 2025 revenue support "large." Capex and depreciation support "brittle." Winning or losing is whether incremental revenue covers the halls. Do not replace the income statement with market cap. Objection 2: booked 19% means Apple is still there, so it is safe. The annual report itself writes the first name up from 12% and the second name down from 22%, with HPC already at 58%. A safe distance is measured on the increment, not on a full-year blended share. Objection 3: 5.39 is made up. It is an estimate. The numerator 72 is official. The denominator about 13.4 is a geometry working assumption. Using it on the full-factory 15,022 thousand wafers is what is made up. Objection 4: consumer electronics will pass cost through by raising price. What can be nailed is official limited supply on game cards, and half of DRAM bits already written to cabinets. Objection 5: a Samsung Taylor long will split foundry concentration. The announcement is a parallel line. No monthly wafers. It cannot take the Western flagship-chip queue.

Testable marks, observation items only. Go back to the makers and the original press releases. This volume does not audit for them. Whether NVIDIA gross margin is still 71% to 75%; whether supply commitments keep leaving $119.0B. Whether TSMC HPC is still above 58%; whether packaging and test is still above 10%; whether C.C. Wei still says tight; whether the first customer keeps leaving 19%. Whether TrendForce foundry revenue share is still above 70%. Whether Hynix operating margin stays high. Whether game cards are still written as supply-limited. Whether HBM plus registered DIMMs are still written by institutions as more than half of bits. Whether the four clouds' cash-flow statements split out an AI line item — if not, do not invent one.

## 6. Split scale winners from profit winners

Analysis addendum. The map must split scale winners from profit winners, or four-cloud revenue will hide depreciation. On revenue, all four clouds are larger than NVIDIA. On gross margin, free cash, and prepayments, NVIDIA turns the scarce slice itself into cash. TSMC's 59.9% gross margin is manufacturing rent that has to keep sinking heavy capital to hold the bottleneck. Hynix 49% is attachment rent. Once the attachment returns to bulk, the tier collapses. The same firm can win on the revenue layer and lose on free cash flow.

Who the residual is pushed to is the cross-period dark line. NVIDIA is asset-light; residual value is mostly not on its own books. The clouds are asset-heavy; residual value sits in the halls. Phone and auto plants carry chips bought dear and inventory. The winner is the link that can push residual onto someone else and collect only definition-rights cash. That is compatible at once with 71% to 75% gross margin, free cash $96.6B, and supply commitments of $119.0B.

Pricing power sits with NVIDIA, HBM makers, and power counterparties who hold a baseload block. Volume without pricing power: phone OEMs, model-API firms that must buy accelerators at the market, and demoted CPUs. The four clouds have volume and some pricing power, but group-wide capex makes that power conditional. TSMC sits in the middle: 59.9% says it is not a coolie; heavy capital says pricing power has to be bought again every year. Booked 19% is not a safe distance.

The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down. One firm writes the rules. If those five are not done, the line walks past half of the scarce slice. The window is the few years before the next first-to-market node is locked by commitments. If Volume 14 is written again as a four-layer industry catalog, or if the seat-license margin assertion and consumer-electronics extinction are written as fact, the question leans. The map is an arrangement of observation marks, not a ranking.
