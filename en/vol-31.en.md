### 3.5 10% of annual turnover is a statute, not a fine

Regulation 1/2003 Article 23(2): For each undertaking and association of undertakings participating in the infringement, the fine shall not exceed 10 % of its total turnover in the preceding business year. The Commission's fines page writes the sentence again. Article 7 of the same regulation is the clause that can split structure: the Commission may impose behavioural or structural remedies; structural remedies only when behavioural remedies would be equally ineffective or more burdensome. That is a list of powers, not a power already used on NVIDIA. URLs: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32003R0001 and https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/index/fines_en

The French ceiling is L464-2 above. Arithmetic is not a fine. NVIDIA FY26 revenue $215,938M. 10% is $21,593.8M. That is a statutory ceiling times the company's own annual report, not a number issued by the Commission or the French Authority. Without an opening and without a statement of objections, this product cannot be written as "about to issue $21.6B."

Money can be compared. The comparison cannot be written as a sanction decision. Q1 FY27 one-quarter operating income $53.536B. The French or EU statutory ceiling of about $21.6B is about 40% of that quarter's operating income, and about 18% of FY26 net income $120.067B. As long as there is no split, no forced-open interface, and no ban on prepayments locking capacity, a fine is a cost. Buybacks plus dividends returned $41.1B to shareholders in a year. Analysis added here: a fine does not change the bid. The judgment already wrote: while the margin holds, commitments keep rising. Anyone else who wants the same material pays more, or does not get it. Write $21.6B into cost and the prepayment slice that locks capacity is still there.

Analysis added here. The White House on one side dislikes fat margins drawing blood from US clouds and manufacturing. On the other it needs this stack to sit on the East in armament. Antitrust slows the step and forces protection money. It does not split the machine that prints profit. 80% accelerator share is media and sell-side scope, not a DOJ relevant-market definition. Do not write it into a finding. No White House or Pentagon order that says "therefore do not split NVIDIA" was read. ### 3.7 Compute rent: $193.7B is seller rent. It does not match "$215B of AI hardware"

The rent end can only be written as numbers NVIDIA itself reported. FY26 revenue $215.9B, up 65% year on year; data center full year $193.7B, up 68%. Phones and PCs, even if they never buy a GPU, are still paying tax. Residual bits: [Volume 5](vol-05.md). That is memory collective liability, not a check they wrote to NVIDIA.

### 3.9 Prepayments lock capacity. That is not tying inside a subpoena

TSMC's board answers to shareholders. When the first name's profit exceeds the increment from everyone else, there is no financial reason to offend the first name for the second. This is not an administrative takeover. It is prepayments buying the slot. A neutrality pledge has no consideration in front of a check. What is not yet written as fact: TSMC already a captive fab, High-NA bought out in bulk, Apple or Qualcomm already unable to get any leading-edge node. On the public record, do not add these two into one sentence that "NVIDIA has already completed the tying under inquiry by using prepayments." Prepayments lock foundry and inventory. The inquiry asks about sales terms to clouds and networking. The former is in the 10-K supply commitments. The latter is in Reuters and Bloomberg inquiry reporting. The appendix keeps two cells. It does not weld them into one.
