# Essay 4: The clouds’ $750B capex war, the limit of in-house ASICs, and DeepSeek’s architectural shock

## 1. The question this essay answers

What the four clouds have already spent — is it group-wide property-and-equipment purchases, or an "AI hardware" line item? Has a self-built ASIC pulled hyperscale pretraining off Nvidia cabinets? DeepSeek-V3's MLA and the draft's training-cost figure — did they ease this spend, or make already-sunk group capital harder to recover?

This essay does not write the draft's "2024 AI hardware $215B" and "2026 four-cloud $670–$750B" as facts that have already happened. What can be checked is the PPE line on each cash-flow statement, and node and memory on each firm's own product pages. ASIC products exist. Training substitution has not won. Public next-generation specs are 3nm-class logic plus HBM, not a retreat to 5nm plus commodity DDR5.

## 3. Mechanism (physical / commercial / geopolitical)

### 3.1 What can be nailed is group-wide PPE, not an AI line item

What this draft read is each firm's IR earnings cash-flow statement, not a page-by-page audited full 10-K. Line-item alignment follows the check file.

Microsoft's fiscal year ends 30 June. The line is Additions to property and equipment. FY25 official cash-flow statement writes $(64,551) million, versus FY24 $(44,477), FY23 $(28,107). Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2025-q4/cash-flows . FY26 official earnings release writes the same line as full-year $(115,948) million, the quarter $(35,802) million, versus prior full year $(64,551). Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2026-Q4/press-release-webcast . FY26 is July 2025 to June 2026. It cannot be added into the same cell as Amazon, Alphabet, Meta calendar 2025, and still less can FY26 actuals be treated as the fourth item of a "calendar-2026 four-cloud total."

Alphabet is calendar year. The line is Purchases of property and equipment. Q4/full-year 2025 Exhibit 99.1 writes full-year $(91,447) million, versus 2024 $(52,535) million. Full-year 2025 equipment purchases $(69,691) million, finance-lease principal $(2,524) million, company scope $72.215 billion (the release writes $72.22 billion); 2024 equipment $37,256 million + leases $1,969 million = $39.225 billion. The check file's 2026 guidance is the annual-report-time $115–$135 billion (including finance-lease principal). Later official Outlook raised and narrowed: Q2 2026 written as $130–$145 billion. On a re-addable scope, 2025 actuals (Microsoft still FY2025, the rest CY2025): equipment purchases, Amazon net, Meta without leases, 64,551 + 128,320 + 91,447 + 69,691 = $354,009 million (about $354.0B); Meta on company scope about $356.5B. This is already not $215B. 2024 mixed-year summary sentences and 2025 actuals cannot be written as the same fact. 2026 three-name guidance, even using the check file's low bin $490B or high bin $520B, missing Microsoft's official calendar-year guidance, still cannot add the draft $670–$750B. Forcing Microsoft FY26 actual $115.948B into a calendar-2026 sum is still the wrong scope: period-ends differ, line items differ, and Amazon's $200B itself is a group-wide expectation.

The physical object is still a three-piece set: accelerator cards or ASICs, power, packaging. Group-wide PPE getting larger only proves buyers are still paying for halls and machines. It does not prove "AI cabinets" have already been spent on the draft's split. Without a CoWoS official split of the 120k–130k wafers/month class, this essay does not write a capacity-ceiling figure. The mechanism that can be written: PPE is capped by packaging, HBM, and interconnect licenses, not a scale a CFO budget can freely choose.

PPE additions and depreciation expense are not the same row. Microsoft's FY25 cash-flow statement on the same page writes Depreciation, amortization, and other $34,153 million, while PPE additions are $64,551 million; FY26 D&A $38,534 million, PPE additions $115,948 million. Depreciation is the expense of assets already landed. PPE is cash still being formed. Using PPE directly as "annualized hardware depreciation" folds in civil works, construction-in-progress not yet placed in service, and fulfillment networks. Amazon's net definition also subtracts sales and incentives. Meta's company scope adds finance-lease principal back. Four period-ends staggered by half a year: any "four-cloud total" is an analyst addition, not a line the four jointly disclosed. The check file locked this: do not knock it against Nvidia revenue. Buyer and seller line items differ.

### 3.2 Cloud ASIC products exist. Training substitution has not won. Next generation is 3nm + HBM

The judgment and [Volume 4](vol-04.md) spine: public next generation is not 5nm + commodity DDR5. To compare with today's high-end accelerators, you return to HBM and advanced packaging. Third-party estimated nodes, CoWoS share, TCO savings do not enter the fact layer. This draft read no cloud saying "retreat to 5nm + commodity DDR5 as the inference mainstay."

Spec table: [Volume 4](vol-04.md). Two paths off commodity memory: [Volume 32](vol-32.md). This essay only collects: products exist; training substitution has not won. Public next generation is 3nm-class plus HBM, not a retreat to 5nm plus commodity DDR5. What a cloud ASIC lowers is a slice of purchases from a whole-cabinet-margin seller. It does not lower dependence on HBM, packaging, and power.

The audit sentence is only one: products exist (TPU / Trainium / Maia / MTIA). This pass has no shipments, no training share. They hit the athlete's die. They do not hit the referee. They have not won.

The reason they have not won is not only a spec table a slice shorter. The referee is CUDA, NVLink, and the right to define the cabinet. Maia 200 officially stresses Ethernet and Triton: going around proprietary interconnect, not already taking the right to define the cabinet. Trainium3 writes native PyTorch, Neuron SDK: admitting migration cost still sits in the compiler and the kernel, not on a FLOPS poster. TPU's strongest spec slice is a 9216-chip Pod and ICI; external customers have to pass JAX / XLA. The draft's ecosystem wall is still there. MTIA itself wrote the first two generations as recommendation; from the 300 generation it writes HBM bandwidth as the most important factor for GenAI inference, and keeps commercially available GPUs. Four lines point at the same conclusion: an ASIC is a dedicated lane for a known internal load, not a substitute highway for general training. Next generation pushing node and memory toward 3nm and HBM is to run faster on this lane, not to dodge the scarce slice. To truly dodge it, public specs should park at 5nm plus commodity DRAM. Public pages have not parked.

### 3.3 DeepSeek: figures marked draft. The meaning is a public cost-down

DeepSeek figures follow the draft. Do not change them. The technical report (arXiv:2412.19437) matches the draft's scope: 671B total parameters, 37B activated per token; 14.8T tokens pretrain; 2048 H800s; full-text training 2.788M H800 GPU-hours (pretrain 2664K + context extension 119K + post-train 5K); at H800 rent $2 / GPU-hour, converted $5.576M. The report writes: official training only, not prior research, development, and infrastructure. The draft wrote about $5.576 million, a cut of 85%+. Carry and mark draft. MLA \(d_c=512\ll 8192\), KV compressed 93.3%, near 10× concurrency on 8 cards, no auxiliary loss, MFU above 50%: carry from the draft.

Geopolitically, H800 itself is a control-compromise good. $5.576 million proves: on restricted hardware, algorithms can punch through the training bar. That will stimulate tighter controls, and will stimulate more buyers to demand Ethernet and open stacks. Political risk on Nvidia's system premium rises from here. It does not mean training cabinets have already been substituted.

From the equation, what MLA cuts is KV and the training bar, not total energy in the inference era. 93.3% cache compression lets mid-size firms in the door, and gives token unit price a technical condition for falling. For buyers who already spent mixed-year group-wide PPE of about $211.9–$213.9B, then added to about $354.0–$356.5B in mixed-year 2025, this is double-edged: unit intelligence gets cheaper; asset utilization must be made up by usage. The war partly turns from "who can buy a cabinet" to "who first writes Agents as a cash workflow."

The asymmetry: a cloud giant's ASIC lowers its own purchases from Nvidia, not a rival's purchases from Nvidia, so it is a private cost-down. DeepSeek's open source and $5.576 million training cost are a public cost-down. The y-axis (unit price) falls for everyone at once; the x-axis (usage) belongs only to those who write Agents as cash. When a public cost-down has already happened, still adding group-wide PPE on private-arms logic can be read as grabbing quota, or as adding leverage on the wrong clock. A private ASIC cannot save a public price cut. A public price cut makes a private ASIC's dedicated compiler harder to prove the migration cost is worth it.

$5.576 million cannot be extrapolated to "every frontier model needs only five million dollars." The report itself locked the rent assumption at $2 / GPU-hour, and excluded research, aborted experiments, and cluster depreciation. Change an unpublished pretrain recipe, or stretch context and post-train, and the same formula gives another figure. What can stand is only GPU-hours of this one official training run. MLA compresses KV. It does not zero inference-era total energy: once concurrency is up, what still turns in the hall is HBM and power. So what DeepSeek changes is the entry bar and the technical floor on unit price, not already-booked group-wide PPE into a reversible investment. If a buyer cuts cabinets on "the bar has been punched through," they must first see the usage cell, not first see a technical report.

## 4. Analysis added (scenarios, counters, testable indicators)

Analysis added. The 2026 by-firm forecast table is unverified. Do not treat it as audit fact.

Scenario A: group-wide PPE keeps adding on already-disclosed guidance. The gap is filled by ads (the draft wrote Meta gross margin 81.5%) and cloud stock, not by pure AI increment as its own row. ASICs eat internal recommendation and a slice of inference. External still buys training cabinets. DeepSeek forces price cuts but does not trigger destocking. This is "spend is happening; substitution has not won."

Scenario B: usage parks at +50%, unit price falls 50% on open source, the matrix declares overhang. The draft's $115.5B depreciation cannot find $155B of increment — those two figures are draft arithmetic, not 2025 actual depreciation. If order cuts happen, they start with training cabinets. ASIC projects will be cut with them, because internal teams also have to prove ROI. Mixed-year 2025 four-cloud PPE is still adding, which says this cell has not appeared.

Scenario C: the MLA path becomes default. 8-card servers take a large share of inference. The draft's $750B becomes excessive. Capital turns from buying cabinets to buying workflow software. Cloud moats partly retreat from quota to data and distribution. This is the algorithm-breakthrough cell, not the present.

Counters: treating a $750B sum as an already-approved budget; writing mixed-year about $211.9B group-wide PPE as "AI hardware $215B"; extrapolating $5.576 million to "every frontier model needs only five million dollars"; treating a TPU spec table as obtainable training share for external customers; writing Maia 200 / Trainium3 / Ironwood / MTIA 300+ HBM specs as "already won training."

Testable indicators: whether each next cash-flow statement's PPE still adds on guidance; whether there is AI incremental revenue as its own row; whether Trainium / TPU / Maia external instances are still complained about on dynamic shapes; whether open-source model training cost keeps landing below ten million dollars; whether public token prices show a 30%–50% annualized fall; whether any cloud writes on an official page "the training mainstay has left bought-in cabinets." Shipments and training share stay blank this pass. Do not invent them.

The executable sentence for a CFO is only one: do not assume in the same budget "usage +300%, unit price does not fall, and the draft's $750B must be spent." The right order: first take a usage cell you are willing to believe, then see whether that cell still covers already-happened group-wide PPE depreciation when unit price falls 30% and 50%, and only then decide whether next year still approaches the forecast table. ASIC projects use the same order: first ask whether they are truly cheaper than bought-in cabinets on dynamic shapes and long context, then ask whether the compiler team's cost is below the purchase saved. DeepSeek is not a slogan to stop spending. It is a reason to change spend from "buy a full set of cabinets" to "buy enough effective tokens to cover the workflow."

Win and loss are not in a press-release capex figure. They are in depreciation per effective token. Whoever can still make this unit fall after MLA and ASICs is still on the field. Whoever can only let this unit rise with cabinet price is making a market for the seller's high gross margin. The cloud-ASIC fork has not won now, so four-corner rent collection is still there. Not winning is not already closed. Writing $750B, ASICs, and DeepSeek as three parallel stories is wrong. They are three terms of one equation. The left side is already-happened group-wide PPE and the next few years of depreciation. The right side is incremental revenue. The conversion coefficient in the middle is hardware cost per unit of intelligence. ASICs lower the private-purchase term in the coefficient. MLA lowers the memory and training terms. The draft's $750B enlarges the left side. When all three expand in the same direction, the equation is harder to balance. DeepSeek lets the right-side unit price fall, and then requires usage to make it up. If a cloud giant does three things at once — add group capital hard, push self-build hard, cut price hard — it puts mutually fighting demands in front of the same CFOs. This essay only writes the fight down. It does not announce who has already won.
