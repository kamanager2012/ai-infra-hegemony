# Essay 3: Physical manufacturing limits and the fight for the global semiconductor supply chain (TSMC 2nm, CoWoS, and HBM)

This essay does not reopen those tables. This essay only collects: process, packaging, and HBM are three ceilings with different units; the short board that 2025 can nail dead is packaging.

## 1. The question this essay answers

Of advanced process, advanced packaging, and HBM, which one is the real physical ceiling on cabinets around 2026? TSMC's books first customer 19%, HPC already 58%, packaging revenue just over 10% — these three official sets measure different things. They cannot substitute for each other. 5.39 wafers/cabinet is a calculation, not an annual report. 835,000 cabinets used the wrong pool. HBM "3:1" also cannot be promoted to a maker formula. This essay only answers: who is locking increment, which line is locked, and at what order of magnitude halfway falls.

## 3. Mechanism (physical / commercial / geopolitical)

The three ceilings have different physical units. The process ceiling is exposure, overlay, and transistor structure (N2 first-generation nanosheet, then A14). Official sources only report wafer **revenue** share, not units by node. The packaging ceiling is interposers and reticle multiples. The unit is "whether a large chip and HBM can be welded together." C.C. Wei only says tight. He does not report monthly capacity. The HBM ceiling is that the same bits need more wafers and more cleanroom. The unit is how much consumer DRAM is left after memory makers turn lines toward cabinets. All three must be satisfied at once. The short board decides shipments. The short board 2025 can nail dead is packaging: C.C. Wei himself said it already limits customer growth. On the process side 2nm has just entered volume. There is not yet a standalone revenue row that can be used to "lock 50% of 3nm/2nm." On the HBM side makers already stand on the cabinet side, but "3:1" is not a formula they printed in an annual report.

### 3.2 CoWoS: official sources only say tight. 5.39 is a calculation. 835,000 cabinets used the wrong pool

Packaging plus test: 4Q24 call, Huang — the company does not split advanced-packaging segments; 2024 advanced packaging plus test about 8% and above of revenue, 2025 will exceed 10%. 4Q25 post-call correction: 2025 a bit over 10%, 2024 about 8%, 2026 outlook low-teens. What can be nailed is revenue share and "does not split CoWoS." Monthly capacity, full-year packaging wafers, per-customer splits cannot be nailed.

C.C. Wei's original sentences that can be nailed are not the second-hand "sold out through 2026." 4Q24 call: *today is all AI focused, and we have very tight capacity and cannot even meet customers' need*. 2Q26 call (2026-07-16): *our packaging capacity is so tight that now it limits my customers' growth*; same session *the majority is still CoWoS*. Focus Taiwan 2026-06-04 shareholders'-meeting text has no CoWoS and no sold out. What official verbatim can nail: tight, cannot meet, already limits customer growth. Those six English words in second-hand paraphrase are not promoted.

Chapter 5 of the annual report writes CoWoS as a 2.5D service that stacks multiple SoCs and HBM together. CoWoS-S (silicon interposer, eDTC) has been in volume for years; 3.5×-reticle CoWoS-L in volume since 2024; 5.5×-reticle CoWoS-L planned to finish qualification in 2026; 9.5× reticle still in development. A 2Q26 analyst asked about a 14×-reticle roadmap and glass substrates. C.C. Wei only answered that the majority is still CoWoS, and alternatives still need about a year before volume with customers. The reticle limit cuts a large chip into two dies and then stitches them back. That is the physical source of packaging demand. Electronic Design / IEEE Spectrum paraphrase B200 as two dies of about 800 mm² each, package totaling about 1,600 mm² (a reporter geometry sentence, not a 10-K). The official H100 blog writes 814 mm². NVL72 product page official: 72 Blackwell GPUs, 36 Grace, 13.4 TB HBM3E, 17 TB LPDDR5X. 72 GPU packages are the numerator of 5.39 below.

What 5.39 is. The calculation volume's equation: CoWoS wafers per cabinet = 72 GPU packages ÷ how many a CoWoS wafer yields. 72 ÷ 5.39 = 13.36 packages/wafer. 72 is the product page. Around 13.4 is a geometry working assumption: two dies totaling about 1,600 mm², plus 8 HBM stacks, the interposer far larger than a single GPU; 300 mm wafer area \(\pi\times 150^{2}=70{,}686\) mm². There is no official TSMC "how many B200s one CoWoS wafer yields." 5.39 is a calculation, not a fiction, and not a capacity formula in the annual report, and still less whole-fab logic wafers/cabinet. Front-end logic is another line: one cabinet 144 GPU dies, theoretically about 72 per wafer, 70% yield gives about 2.9 wafers/cabinet. The two lines do not match. Mixing them is the wrong pool. Do not write another fake yield formula of the \((0.95)^{12}\) type to "derive" 5.39 — that formula is in the draft. This essay does not use it.

Where 835,000 cabinets came from, and why it is void. 4.5 million ÷ 5.39 ≈ 835,000 cabinets. 4.5 million = 30% of TSMC's 2025 shipments of 15.00 million. 5.39 is CoWoS wafers/cabinet. Most of the 15.00 million is phones, MCUs, auto, mature nodes. Dividing the whole fab, or 30% of the whole fab, by 5.39 yields a cabinet count with no physical meaning. Dividing the full-year 15.00 million by 5.39 would give 2.78 million cabinets, equally meaningless. 800,000 cabinets × 5.39 = 4.31 million wafers, near 29% of the whole fab, also near 3nm+5nm revenue share (24%+36%=60% is revenue, not wafers). 800,000 cabinets cannot be written as "locking 50% of 3nm/2nm."

Use the right pool. TSMC's annual report and calls have no official full-year CoWoS wafer count. Sellers (Longbridge citing Morgan Stanley): 2026 global CoWoS demand about 1.00 million wafers, NVIDIA about 595,000 wafers, about 60%; monthly capacity written from 32k in 2024 to 93k by end-2026. Another house, Mizuho (Investing.com cite), writes monthly capacity 140k, NVIDIA 630,000 wafers at TSMC. The two houses disagree. Neither is a TSMC line item. On 5.39 and the seller's 1.00 million wafers: half = 500,000 wafers ≈ 93,000 cabinets; 595,000 wafers ≈ 110,000 cabinets. Rounded: about 100,000 cabinets past halfway on this packaging line. The judgment: you do not need 800,000 cabinets to lock 3nm. One Rubin cabinet official HBM4 20.7 TB, the interposer larger, CoWoS wafers per cabinet will only be larger than 5.39, not smaller.

Commercial meaning. Prepayments turn the packaging ceiling into quota. NVIDIA FY26 supply commitments from $95.2B to Q1 FY27 $119.0B (judgment; 10-K original prepaid manufacturing and capacity agreements, not split to TSMC or CoWoS). TSMC gross margin is already 59.9%. There is no financial reason to offend the first name for the second. Apple locks phone logic. Newly opened packaging is taken by cabinets first. AMD / Broadcom / Apple leftovers on seller tables are the physical ceiling on a counterattack, not a marketing ceiling. Geopolitically, lithography is in Europe, foundry and packaging in Taiwan, HBM in Korea. A chips act can copy a building. It cannot copy the time of three queues lining up at once. Analysis added: the supply-chain fight is a fight for alignment, not a fight for one isolated fab.

The two lines cannot be added into "eight TSMC wafers per cabinet." 2.9 eats front-end 4nm-class logic, and competes with Apple and Qualcomm for the same Gigafab's leading-edge node, but not for the same reticle queue: phones want small chips and high yield; cabinets want dual dies near the reticle limit. 5.39 eats back-end interposers. Phones almost do not enter this pool. Adding 2.9 and 5.39 and then dividing 15.00 million is the third use of the wrong pool. Analysis added: TSMC can be full on logic and short on packaging at the same time — C.C. Wei in 2Q26 welcomed Intel EMIB "sharing a bit of the load" precisely because the front end still wants to sell more and the back end is already jamming customers. Packaging revenue just over 10%, logic still the company's body, is not a contradiction: the short board can be the small head of revenue.

### 3.3 HBM: maker books stand on the cabinet side. "3:1" is not promoted to official

Maker won and 51% bits: [Volume 5](vol-05.md). This essay takes only: 51% is bits, not revenue. RDIMM is already inside this 51%. Server commodity DDR5 is not another river. If clouds restack inference on DDR5, they are grabbing residual already on this half, adding tax, not opening a new river. "3:1" is not promoted to official.

Micron does not supply an official "3:1" formula. 10-K (fiscal year ended 2025-08-28) original: the same bits, same node, HBM needs more wafers and more cleanroom. Hot Chips 2026 floor notes (ServeTheHome): same-capacity HBM3E consumes about 3× the silicon of DDR5 — design architecture, advanced packaging, and manufacturing complexity together. Tom's Hardware headlines in the same direction. 3× is a conference paraphrase. Do not promote it to a 10-K-checked multiple, and still less to a factory split of "one HBM wafer for three DDR5 wafers." The draft's \((0.95)^{12}\times 0.90\) and "1,200 versus 400" are a diagram, not a warehouse count. This essay does not use them. Working magnifications that stack another yield layer of the 8.6 / 13.2 type: evidence-03 explicitly does not write them.

## 4. Analysis added (scenarios, counters, testable indicators)

Analysis added. 2026 shipments 16–17 million, capex-budget revisions, low-teens packaging revenue: all outlook. Seller 1.00 million wafers / 595,000 wafers / 93k–140k wafers/month fight each other. They are only used to mark the halfway order of magnitude. Do not write them as already-realized capacity.

Scenario A: the short board stays nailed on packaging. C.C. Wei in 2Q26 already said it limits customer growth. Even if CoWoS ramp walks to the seller-written 100k-wafers-per-month class, 5.5× and 9.5× reticles press down how many units one wafer can yield, wafers per cabinet larger than 5.39. Halfway still sits near 100,000 cabinets, not 835,000. The investment watch is lead time and "tight / limits growth" on the call, not whole-fab shipments.

Scenario B: the short board moves from packaging to HBM and power. After packaging loosens a bit, cabinets still cannot ship, because 13.4 TB/cabinet and grid interconnect are still there. Hynix operating margin 49%, HBM revenue doubling, says the maker has no financial reason to cut lines back to consumer. TrendForce has already assigned half of 2026 bits to HBM+RDIMM. Consumer electronics pays residual tax (Huaqiangbei channel oral 16G DDR5 450 yuan to 1,800 yuan, not maker ASP).

Scenario C: N2 / A16 ramp lifts process quotes another bin. Mid-tier buyers exit the first-silicon queue, but packaging is still the quantity gate. Before 2nm has a standalone revenue row, "locking 2nm" cannot be written as already happened. A16's Super Power Rail is for HPC, not for phones. Phones keep locking another queue on 3nm / N2P.

Counters: writing TrendForce 72% as "top-tier AI foundry 95%"; writing 130k wafers/month as already realized in 2026; writing 5.39 into the annual report; treating 835,000 cabinets as a buildable cabinet-count ceiling; writing conference-paraphrase about 3× silicon as a maker 3:1 law; treating "sold out through 2026" as C.C. Wei verbatim. 0.95 and 0.90 in the formula are settings, not a metrology certificate from a line.

Testable indicators: whether TSMC-call 3nm revenue share is still above 24%, and when N2 becomes its own row; whether HPC keeps pressing past phones; whether first customer is still walking up near 19%; whether packaging plus test walks from a bit over 10% to low-teens; whether C.C. Wei still uses tight / cannot meet / limits growth; whether TrendForce foundry share 72% / 6.5% / 5.1% loosens; whether Hynix still discloses a large year-on-year HBM-revenue increase, and whether operating margin stays near 49%; whether HBM+RDIMM is still written as more than half of DRAM bits. Until TSMC itself reports CoWoS monthly capacity, treat it only as seller tracking.

The three ceilings have different observation frequencies. Mixed wafer revenue changes by quarter, good for watching whether HPC is still pulling increment. Packaging watches call wording and revenue share, not outside monthly-capacity tables. HBM watches maker revenue direction and bit structure, not a fake yield formula. Covering all three with one "semiconductor ETF" averages the short-board shift away. This essay's use is to force the three apart: process is an entry ticket for a few buyers; packaging is the quantity gate; HBM is a blood-draw on consumer goods. The short board 2025 official sources can nail is packaging; the memory short board is hardening; 2nm has just entered volume; price screening does not yet have an annual-report row that can be checked.

National strategy in the manufacturing fight is often written as "rebuild a TSMC." On the annual report, this fab must have N2 yield, CoWoS-class packaging, and HBM on the Korea side at once. Even if the entry tickets are assembled, without packaging and HBM it still cannot spit out the dual die NVL72 wants. The correct national question is not "whether to build a fab." It is which of the three ceilings is the shortest short board on one's own side. Fill the wrong short board and you get an asset that can cut a ribbon and cannot supply. For investors: first ask whether the present short board is process, packaging, or HBM, then decide which slice to overweight. Overweighting all three at once is refusing to admit the short board will move. In 2023, whoever saw packaging tight matched lead times. In 2025–2026 official sentences have already upgraded from cannot meet to limits growth. The same discipline still applies. The window is these few years before next-generation first silicon is locked by commitments, not waiting on 835,000 cabinets.

Booked 19% has another layer that is easy to read backwards. First name from 25% (2023) down to 22% (2024) down to 19% (2025) looks like diversification; on the same table second name from 11% up to 12% up to 17% looks like concentration. Split them: 2025 first-name revenue jumped from NT$352.271B to NT$726.974B, almost doubled; share fell only because the denominator (company-wide revenue) rose faster. Second-name absolute amount walked almost sideways (NT$624.345B to NT$645.179B); share was lifted because others rose and it did not. Jensen Huang calling himself the largest customer is another line. The 20-F does not name names. This essay does not write 19% as NVIDIA. What can be written: increment is in HPC; whoever takes increment is writing the year-after-next queue. 19% is a full-year average, not CoWoS share, and not 2nm first-silicon share.

HBM's macro consequence also does not need 3:1 to stand. In a traditional DRAM down-cycle makers cut output, consumer electronics benefits. Now makers turn lines toward cabinets. Consumer electronics is hurt in the up-cycle and is not necessarily compensated in the down-cycle. Memory is no longer only a cycle good. It is also an accessory mine for the cabinet.
