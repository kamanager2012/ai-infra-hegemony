# Macro Essay 8: U.S.–China Compute Competition and the 2036 Endgame Decision Tree

Chinese source edition: [part2-08.md](../part2-08.md)

> Retrieval and revision cutoff: **2026-08-27**. China's consumer-computing supply, workstation stack, and frontier AI stack are evaluated separately. The 2036 framework uses S1–S4 conditional states rather than fixed subjective probabilities.

## 1. The core question

Export controls have increased the strategic incentive to build a Chinese parallel AI stack.

The relevant questions are:

1. Has China formed a second global-scale resource-gravity center?
2. Which layers are already substitutable and which remain constrained?
3. What observable gates would qualify as complete frontier-stack closure?

The analysis must distinguish:

- smartphones and consumer computing;
- PCs/workstations;
- frontier AI systems.

These are not one supply layer.

## 2. Consumer computing and frontier AI have different closure requirements

China has much stronger parallel capability in:

- smartphones;
- consumer devices;
- conventional DRAM/NAND;
- local manufacturing.

Frontier AI requires a more difficult combination:

- advanced logic;
- HBM-class memory;
- advanced packaging;
- high-speed interconnect;
- compiler/runtime;
- large-cluster deployment;
- power.

Strong consumer supply therefore does not prove frontier-system equivalence.

Frontier constraints also do not erase China's strengths in consumer and industrial supply chains.

## 3. Ascend/CANN matters as a system, not a single benchmark

The strategic significance of Huawei's stack is the combination of:

- accelerators;
- compiler/runtime;
- communication;
- servers;
- domestic cloud deployment.

The key indicators are:

- large-cluster utilization;
- framework compatibility;
- compiler stability;
- operator coverage;
- failure recovery;
- production workload share.

Peak single-card performance alone cannot determine system substitution.

The critical question is whether the stack can run large real workloads efficiently and repeatedly.

## 4. The public CXMT filing does not disclose an HBM product line

CXMT's public filing discloses DDR and LPDDR product lines.

The filing does **not** disclose an HBM product line.

That evidence supports a narrow conclusion:

> **HBM-class memory has not yet been demonstrated in the public filing as a scaled commercial product line.**

It does not prove the absence of all HBM research.

For frontier-stack closure, however, R&D possibility is insufficient.

The system needs:

- official product disclosure;
- qualification;
- production volume;
- bandwidth and capacity;
- packaging integration.

## 5. YMTC strengthens NAND supply, not the HBM layer

YMTC is a major NAND producer.

Its public materials support a strong storage position.

NAND/SSD supply can improve:

- consumer storage;
- enterprise storage;
- domestic supply resilience.

It does not substitute for:

- HBM;
- server DRAM;
- the memory requirements of frontier training systems.

“Domestic memory” therefore has to remain a layered category.

## 6. Foundry revenue share is not frontier-AI capability share

TrendForce's 2026 Q1 foundry-revenue estimates were approximately:

- TSMC: **72%**;
- Samsung: **6.5%**;
- SMIC: **5.1%**.

These are revenue shares.

They do not imply equivalent frontier-node capability.

A complete alternative system also needs:

- advanced packaging;
- HBM;
- substrates;
- yield;
- qualification;
- software.

SMIC's overall foundry position is strategically important, but it should not be described as already capable of absorbing the full frontier-AI supply chain that currently depends heavily on TSMC and advanced memory/packaging ecosystems.

## 7. Algorithmic efficiency is a real asymmetric lever

DeepSeek demonstrates why a supply-constrained system can gain strategic value from:

- sparse models;
- memory compression;
- quantization;
- routing;
- communication optimization.

Architecture can raise **effective compute productivity**.

That can narrow a hardware disadvantage without matching every individual specification.

But algorithmic efficiency does not eliminate:

- HBM;
- packaging;
- interconnect;
- power.

It changes the productivity of constrained resources.

## 8. Has a second global-scale resource buyer emerged?

A second resource-gravity center would require domestic demand large enough to support:

- advanced logic at scale;
- HBM-class memory at scale;
- advanced packaging at scale;
- a mature software/runtime stack;
- sustained frontier workloads.

China already has strong domestic demand and a real accelerator/software ecosystem.

What remains incomplete is simultaneous closure across the most advanced physical layers.

The current state is therefore:

> **a strategically important parallel stack, but not yet a globally equivalent second frontier supply system across all critical layers.**

## 9. Seven gates for complete frontier-stack closure

The report uses seven gates:

1. advanced accelerators at scale;
2. HBM-class memory at scale;
3. advanced packaging at scale;
4. production-grade large-cluster interconnect;
5. mature compiler/runtime;
6. stable frontier-workload migration;
7. sustainable cost and utilization.

The system should be considered closed only when these gates are jointly demonstrated.

One successful chip, one model run, or one consumer-market share is insufficient.

## 10. 2036 decision tree

The formal end state uses four conditional scenarios.

### S1 — Competitive Expansion

Multiple global and regional system stacks become viable.

Portability improves and physical supply broadens.

### S2 — Baseline Concentration

NVIDIA and major hyperscaler stacks remain dominant globally while China's parallel stack expands but retains gaps at some advanced layers.

### S3 — Resource Hegemony

Scarce layers become more tightly concentrated and geopolitically segmented.

Parallel stacks exist, but entry barriers rise.

### S4 — Capital Hard Landing

Overbuild and weak monetization cause utilization and investment to fall.

Capital clearing can occur on both sides of a fragmented global system.

These are states, not assigned probabilities.

## 11. What to monitor

The most useful China-stack indicators are:

- official HBM-class product and revenue disclosure;
- advanced-packaging scale and yield;
- large-cluster utilization;
- CANN/compiler portability;
- frontier-model migration;
- foundry production scale at relevant advanced nodes;
- energized data-center power;
- real workload economics.

The global indicators include:

- NVIDIA supply commitments;
- TSMC packaging tightness;
- HBM supply;
- hyperscaler custom-stack substitution;
- export-control changes.

## 12. Current judgment

The most accurate 2026 description is neither:

> “China has already reached complete frontier equivalence”

nor:

> “China has no alternative system.”

It is:

> **China has built a real parallel accelerator/software stack and strong lower-layer industrial capabilities, but full frontier-system closure still requires several advanced physical and software layers to scale together.**

That intermediate state already changes the global strategy.

It creates:

- a durable substitution incentive;
- a separate software ecosystem;
- duplicated industrial investment;
- stronger pressure for regional supply resilience.

The 2036 outcome will depend on which of the seven closure gates are crossed and which global constraints—capital, power, packaging, memory, or regulation—become dominant first.
