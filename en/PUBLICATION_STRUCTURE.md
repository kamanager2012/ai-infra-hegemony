# Research Architecture

## 1. Analytical purpose

The report is organized as a continuous 32-chapter industry analysis.

Its core causal structure is:

```text
Demand Power
→ Physical Allocation
→ System Delivery
→ Capital Formation
→ Industry Transmission
→ Utilization / Monetization
→ Next-Cycle Capital Allocation
```

The structure is designed to keep company-specific evidence subordinate to the larger industrial mechanisms.

---

## 2. Three causal layers

### Layer A — Demand Power

Key questions:

- Which buyers can sustain the largest long-duration AI workloads?
- Which buyers have the strongest willingness to pay, balance sheets, and contract duration?
- How do hyperscaler CapEx and Anchor Compute Buyer commitments affect infrastructure formation?
- Does workload monetization justify the capital base?

Primary chapters:

- Ch10 Model Economics;
- Ch12 Hyperscaler System Stack;
- Ch13 Anchor Compute Buyer;
- Ch14 Agent-Native Software;
- Ch19 CapEx Supercycle;
- Ch23 Capital Financing.

### Layer B — Physical Allocation

Key questions:

- Where is the binding physical constraint?
- How do advanced logic, HBM, packaging, networking, LPS, and power interact?
- Which buyers obtain quantity assurance and earlier delivery?
- Does supply elasticity improve or does the bottleneck rotate?

Primary chapters:

- Ch2 NVIDIA System Stack;
- Ch3 AI Resource Gravity / NARC;
- Ch4 Advanced Logic;
- Ch5 HBM;
- Ch6 Advanced Packaging;
- Ch7 Power;
- Ch8 Quantitative Framework;
- Ch9 Manufacturing Redundancy.

### Layer C — Industry Transmission

Key questions:

- How does AI reshape profit pools and financing?
- What happens to non-AI residual capacity?
- How do consumer, automotive, industrial, labor, and education systems absorb the shock?
- How do China, regulation, and geopolitics alter the long-run competitive structure?

Primary chapters:

- Ch16 Differentiated Competitors;
- Ch17 China Parallel Stack;
- Ch18 Antitrust / Policy;
- Ch20 Value Capture;
- Ch21 Consumer Transmission;
- Ch22 Depreciation;
- Ch24 Capital Clearing;
- Ch25 Labor;
- Ch26 Education.

## 3. Four-part reading structure

### Part I — System Power and Physical Scarcity

Chapters 1–9 establish the system-stack thesis, physical constraints, and resource-allocation framework.

### Part II — Demand, Substitution, and the Second System Stack

Chapters 10–18 examine model economics, hyperscaler substitution, Anchor Buyers, software/workload economics, competitors, China, and policy.

### Part III — Capital Cycles, Profit Pools, and External Transmission

Chapters 19–26 analyze capital formation, financing, depreciation, value capture, consumer transmission, capital clearing, labor, and talent.

### Part IV — Method, Scenarios, Monitoring, and Strategy

Chapters 27–32 define evidence discipline, milestone tracking, scenario weighting, strategic implications, and the final master judgment.

## 4. Core analytical concepts

The report uses a limited set of recurring mechanisms:

- AI Resource Gravity;
- Incremental Scarce-Capacity Access;
- Non-AI Residual Capacity;
- Two-Speed Semiconductor Economy;
- Rolling Bottleneck Cascade;
- Cloud System-Stack Substitution;
- Anchor Compute Buyer Effect;
- Financing Reflexivity;
- AI Software Unit Economics;
- Task-to-Job Translation;
- Production-Grade Parallel Stack;
- Autonomous Frontier Closure.

A concept is defined once and later chapters add evidence, boundaries, counterarguments, or falsification conditions rather than renaming the same mechanism.

---

## 5. Scenario architecture

The report uses four conditional states:

- **S1 — Competitive Expansion**
- **S2 — Baseline Concentration**
- **S3 — Resource Hegemony**
- **S4 — Capital Hard Landing**

These are parallel conditional states, not a deterministic sequence.

The current evidence mix is closest to **S2 — Baseline Concentration**, but scenario weighting must change when observable evidence changes.

---

## 6. Research discipline

The 32-chapter architecture follows several rules:

1. observed facts remain separate from company guidance, institutional forecasts, and report-side calculations;
2. physical pools with different units are not collapsed into one denominator;
3. one high-impact factual table has one primary analytical home;
4. later chapters do not repeat definitions unless the new context changes the mechanism;
5. every strong long-run judgment should have observable falsification conditions;
6. China production-grade substitution and autonomous frontier closure remain separate questions;
7. capital-cycle analysis includes demand-financing quality, not only CapEx and accounting depreciation.
