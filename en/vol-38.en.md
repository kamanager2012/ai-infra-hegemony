# Volume 38: Labor-market reconstruction in the AI era (cognitive-labor substitution, human–machine division, and new production relations)

Rent and tax: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). This volume has no unemployment rate, no jobs-lost count, no wage percentile. Statistics are zero. Do not invent headcount.

## 1. The question this volume answers

Volume 38's title is cognitive-labor substitution, human–machine collaboration, and new production relations. The draft of this volume had almost no labor statistics: no unemployment rate, no jobs-lost count, no wage percentile, no professional-exam pass rate. The audit already wrote it — statistics are zero. Do not invent headcount. This volume keeps that rule.

This volume changes the question. It does not ask "in which year how many people will be unemployed." It asks three things that can be nailed. First, what is rewritten first: tasks or jobs. Second, who holds residual claim — model weights, compute quota, workflow data, or still the people who can design workflows. Third, given that the margin monopoly and compute rent are already standing, and antitrust investigations have not become lawsuits, which layer rewrites labor bargaining: the application layer grinding, or the backend collecting rent.

The judgment's spine: the world is not blowing open. It is an upgrade parked on the next-lower node, with rules set by one firm. The labor market is not another river. It is the same rules landing on employment contracts. What can change the rules overnight is a breakup or a forced opening of CUDA, not a fine, and not a "cognitive leap" course. After national security wrote advanced chips and large compute clusters as license items, a breakup is harder to touch. That is a political constraint, not an already-signed no-breakup order. This volume lands those two sentences on people.

## 2. What the draft already argued (compressed; unverified numbers not promoted)

The means-of-production characterization comes from Part I Volume 1. In the third information revolution, PCs and phones were a passive extension of human brainpower, output bounded by physiology and cognitive bandwidth. 2026–2036 was written in the draft as the first time compute itself could generalize, generate code, induce logic, and decide autonomously. The diagram's endpoint is a supercluster driving Agent workflows, directly substituting junior and mid-level human intelligence. That sentence is a draft strategic line, not an employment census that has already happened.

The cost side gave a financial motive for labor substitution, but the scope must be downgraded. Mixed-year four-cloud figures: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). The depreciation wall: [Volume 30](vol-30.md). Mixed-year capex cannot be added into "AI hardware," and it cannot be used to back out one firm's already-audited depreciation path.

Traditional SaaS gross margin compressed from 85% to 55%, with inference entering cost of sales, is a draft business-model assumption, not one company's already-audited margin path. This volume keeps the mechanism: seat licenses sell person-months; Agent software sells completed steps. It does not write 85% and 55% as an already-settled industry mean.

On the algorithm side, the draft used DeepSeek-V3/R1 training cost and MLA compressing KV Cache to write that "a mid-size firm can also afford the inference that rewrites junior cognitive steps." Those engineering figures were not re-checked against the original this pass. Do not promote them. What can stay is the direction: cheaper inference does not mean human cognitive labor has no value. It only lowers the bar for rewriting a textualizable step as an API call.

On the edge, the draft wrote Jetson Thor power, a humanoid-robot BOM split, flagship SoC purchase prices, and replacement cycles. The original reports were not re-checked this pass. Do not promote them to purchase prices or shipments. The mechanism that can stay: installation, repair, and care in the physical world do not vanish linearly with token price. When edge substitution does not scale, cloud tokens remain the scale entry, and labor substitution concentrates in large firms that already bought cloud seats.

The strategic sentence still works: firms should focus on workflows that produce commercial value directly, and avoid proprietary-API lock-in. The matching sentence for individuals, analysis added, is: do not write irreplaceable judgment as middleware an API can fully recap.

## 3. What public labor literature nails: exposure is not unemployment

A labor volume is easiest to write as "X% of jobs are about to disappear." The public literature itself blocks that cut. This volume only cites the institutions' own sentences. It does not rewrite exposure rates as unemployment rates.

The International Labour Organization's February 2026 research brief *Workers' exposure to AI: What indicators tell us – and what they don't* (ILO, DOI 10.54394/00033279) locked the scope. Exposure indicators estimate on which tasks an AI system can substitute for a person. They do not estimate whether automation pays, whether it brings productivity, or how it will change employment. The brief's original:

> They measure what could be automated technically, not whether automation is profitable, generates productivity gains, nor how it impacts employment.

The same brief's conclusion is harder:

> They should not be used to forecast job losses. They are best understood as early warning indicators that identify occupations where task content is likely to change.

Method disagreement itself is a fact. Earlier computerization indicators pointed risk more at low-pay, repetitive, routine manual or routine cognitive jobs. Newer indicators built on AI capability assign higher exposure to cognitive, analytical, administrative, and managerial jobs. The brief writes: business, finance, computing, mathematics, education stay highest across several indicators; office and administrative support, sales are also fragile, with larger within-group differences. High-exposure jobs often sit at the center of an occupational network; the shock spills along skill and transfer paths. Manual, care, and craft jobs sit at the network's edge; direct spillover is smaller. That is occupational-network structure, not an unemployment map this volume invented.

The ILO report prepared for the 2026 International Labour Conference, *Artificial Intelligence and the world of work* (ILC114-I(B)), compresses existing evidence into one sentence: at this stage AI mainly changes tasks and work organization, rather than wiping out jobs at scale; this is consistent with earlier technological transitions. Outcomes depend on the balance between task substitution and task creation, and on the economic and institutional factors that shape adoption. Even if totals do not move much, distributional effects can still be large, especially where transfer options are few and social protection is weak. This volume treats that sentence as a method constraint, not as a guarantee that "therefore there will be no layoffs."

The IMF's January 2024 Staff Discussion Note *Gen-AI: Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Work* (SDN/2024/001) gives exposure, not unemployment. The staff abstract writes: about 40% of global employment is exposed to AI; about 60% in advanced economies, because more jobs are cognitive-task jobs; about 40% in emerging markets, about 26% in low-income countries. Of exposed jobs in advanced economies, about half may be hit negatively; the rest may gain productivity from complementarity. The same note also splits: in an average advanced economy, high-exposure and high-complementarity is about 27% of employment, high-exposure and low-complementarity about 33%. High-exposure employment in the UK and the US is roughly near 70% and 60%, split about half and half between high complementarity and low complementarity. All of these percentages are exposure and complementarity classifications. The IMF itself did not write them as already-realized unemployment rates. The 14 January 2026 IMF blog *New Skills and AI Are Reshaping the Future of Work* again uses "nearly 40 percent of jobs globally are exposed to AI-driven changes," and adds a sentence this volume needs: competition policy also matters; when firms acquire rivals to grab scarce talent, market power concentrates.

The OECD has written in country studies: AI's largest progress is on non-routine cognitive tasks, so the most exposed are often white-collar; but high exposure is not the same as being substituted. By the time of that research, OECD countries had not yet seen negative aggregate employment results caused by AI. 83% of SMEs reported that generative AI had not changed the total number of people they need — that is firm self-report, not a census. This volume treats it only as side evidence that "aggregates have not yet been punched through by public statistics," not as a global conclusion.

Analysis added. Stack ILO, IMF, and OECD public literature on the judgment and what you get is not a layoff timetable. It is a division of labor: exposure tells you which tasks are sliced first; rent and tax tell you who collects the residual after the slice; antitrust tells you whether this rent-collection structure can be rewritten overnight. Drop any layer and you misread "white-collar exposure is high" as "white-collar is about to disappear," or "aggregates have not collapsed" as "rent does not exist."

## 4. How the judgment's four corners rewrite the employment contract

NVIDIA is athlete, referee, and coach at once, and it also trains models. The labor market is not a separate chapter outside those four corners. It is the four corners rewriting different clauses of the contract.

The athlete sells accelerators, cabinets, and interconnect. Profit and cash: [Volume 1](vol-01.md). The meaning for employment contracts, analysis added: employers who can wait are the clouds, the athlete itself, the foundries, and sovereigns. Those who cannot wait are PCs, mid- and low-end phones, security, and the channel. In the labor market, the first to be squeezed among those who cannot wait are assembly, channel, and installation jobs downstream, not chip-design jobs at the four names. This pass has no shipment or employment statistics for those downstream jobs. The mechanism can be written. Names and headcounts are not invented.

The referee is CUDA, NVLink, and the cabinet standard. A discussion draft already moved the labor volume's collection point: intelligence rent does not land only on model weights. If a workflow is welded to CUDA / NIM, bargaining sits in the stack. Analysis added. A résumé that "can write prompts" is buying intermediate output that can be tokenized. A résumé that "can schedule many models and many chips into a stable business result" is buying scarce skill near referee power. Supply of the former can pile up quickly. The latter has to pass the cluster, the package, and the interconnect. If the education system only produces people who can call APIs, it piles supply onto the side that has already depreciated. That is not an enrollment statistic. It is a price mechanism.

The coach decides which class of engineer the next slice of line absorbs first: advanced packaging, liquid cooling, power, nuclear licenses, HBM process. These jobs eat yield and licenses, not prompts. Packaging tightness: [Volume 24](vol-24.md). When people, tools, and lines are queued for the first name, the second name's software jobs, however hot, cannot buy a process seat on the same packaging line.

The model player has Nemotron / NeMo / NIM. The revenue bulk is still on cabinets. Writing model jobs as a second profit pool is not allowed by the judgment. The labor meaning: hiring expansion at model firms cannot be automatically read as "the new main employer of cognitive labor." They themselves are also cabinet tenants.

The sales unit moved up from the chip to the cabinet. The employment unit is also moving up. Pricing used to be by chip, by card, by person-month. It is now by cabinet, by cluster, by workflow step. Analysis added. Titles can stay. The task mix is hollowed out. That is the same structure as "the weaver is still there, spinning left first" in the industrial revolution, with the object switched from muscle to symbol. What the ILO is saying is exactly this: what changes is tasks and work organization, not the job roster first.

## 5. Compute rent: who pays rent, who draws a wage

Rent and tax: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). Four-cloud scope: the same volume. For the labor market, analysis added three sentences.

First, rent and wages fight over the same corporate P&L. Profit and cash: [Volume 1](vol-01.md). If an employer prepays cabinets on one side and keeps the old mid-level intelligence headcount on the other, depreciation and wages will fight in the same year. What moves first is usually the budget for steps that can be textualized, checked, and rolled back, not capacity commitments already signed.

Second, a rent rate cannot be computed. A rent direction can be written. See [Volume 8](vol-08.md). The direction: a slice of cloud capex becomes cabinets, then becomes seller revenue. That slice no longer comes back as wages to employees who produce intermediate text.

Third, residual tax hits another layer of labor. Residual: [Volume 5](vol-05.md). Phones, PCs, consumer disks, channel boxes, security modules have no prepayments. The queue does not reach them. Assembly workers, channel sales, and installation techs in these industries first meet not "AI stole the white-collar meal," but fewer leading-edge SKUs on the shelf, more expensive controllers, slower replacement. This pass has no employment headcount for these jobs. Do not invent it. The mechanism holds: residual tax is the first wave of the labor shock. It just hits the downstream that cannot wait, not the title wall at the "four compute giants." Analysis added: employees vote with their feet. What they vote on is the employer's queue, not the employer's brand slogan.

## 6. Antitrust and national security: a fine does not change the bid. A breakup changes the rules

If labor bargaining hopes regulation will open the market, the public record does not match. The case file: [Volume 11](vol-11.md). National-security documents: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). For the labor market, analysis added: a fine does not change the bid, and does not change who owns the right to define the cabinet. Employers will not expand mid-level intelligence headcount back because of a statutory ceiling that has not yet been issued. What can change the structure of the employment contract is forced opening of the software stack, a ban on bundled supply, that class of interoperability and structural remedy. None of that has been done. Writing "regulation is about to open the market" as a white-collar transfer timetable exceeds the public record. The labor meaning: administrative lock decides who can use a high-speed interconnect cluster, and who can only work on Ethernet or a domestic interconnect. Export controls do not lay people off directly. They write a productivity gap as a border. That is a layer, not a headcount.

## 7. Three layers of labor, not one "AI job"

The judgment cuts the China market into three layers. At home, phones at most. PCs and AI do not resolve. The layer is wrong. The labor market must be cut on the same knife. "China has programmers" cannot be matched to "global cabinets."

Phone layer. The scarce object is a slice of leading-edge logic and residual LPDDR. Apple locks its own phone logic. The rest is the Smartphone platform. Domestic shipments, brands, and channels are still there. Kirin plus SMIC is this layer's parallel supply. CXMT commodity DRAM can supply this layer. Labor: assembly, test, channel, after-sales are still there. What they pay is a memory tax and slower replacement, not a CUDA tax. They cannot save engineer headcount on the other two layers.

PC layer. The same residual DRAM, consumer SSDs, gaming cards. Official supply of gaming cards is limited. OEMs are still there. They have no accelerator stack of their own, and no CoWoS queue. Still x86, Windows, discrete GPUs. Labor: ODM lines, channel stores, repair shops. When residual thins further, this layer first cuts shifts and inventory, not the "prompt engineer" title. Unresolved: it cannot solve DIMMs, disks, and cards being sucked into cabinets.

AI layer. The scarce objects are packaging, HBM, cabinet fabric, the referee stack. Labor: people at the rules layer sit on cabinets — cluster scheduling, cabinet integration, advanced-packaging process, liquid cooling and power licenses. Three layers and two gates: [Volume 12](vol-12.md). Compound training does not fill a photoresist gate. A cognitive leap does not change the bid.

Analysis added. If policy only subsidizes the "AI job" title, it piles people onto the side that is already exposed, and where bargaining sits in the stack, not in the person. What is truly scarce is the technician who can keep cabinets and the grid running, and the person who can design a workflow between two referee stacks. The former is at the coach corner. The latter is at the referee corner. Neither is "prompts" on an admissions brochure. This volume has no hiring headcount for technician and prompt jobs. It does not invent a ratio. It only leaves a testable item: in public job texts, whether the ratio of "multi-model scheduling / cabinet / liquid cooling / packaging" to "single-framework fine-tune" has risen.

Shop-floor labor deserves its own paragraph. A humanoid robot puts compute in only one slice of the BOM. The rest is motors, reducers, sensors, and structure. Even if edge compute rises, assembly, calibration, repair, and care still need people. Process engineers in factories, grid operators on shift, nuclear licensing teams all belong to the set "a model cannot take the responsibility." There will be many stories of cognitive-labor substitution. The bulk of the employment stock may still sit in these sets. The ILO brief puts manual, care, and craft at the edge of the occupational network, in the same direction as this sentence. This volume does not promote BOM percentages. It only leaves the structure: steps whose responsibility cannot be moved still price as wages and insurance; steps that can be published, checked, and rolled back will price as tokens first.

Unions, professional licenses, and industry standards are speed bumps, not because the technology cannot do it, but because responsibility cannot be moved. Medicine, law, structural sign-off, nuclear operations will all require a human in the loop. Headcount inside the loop will fall. Unit price need not fall. Whether labor law allows seniority to be rewritten as delivery-by-step is each country's own gap. The draft did not write it. This volume does not invent statutes.

## 8. The five interrupt paths are not the same speed for labor

The five interrupts: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). Cloud ASICs: [Volume 4](vol-04.md). Power: [Volume 13](vol-13.md). The China stack: [Volume 12](vol-12.md). For labor bargaining, the five are not the same speed. If a coalition is done, the second name's engineers can buy same-grade packaging, and white-collar exposure can be hedged by multi-stack portability. It is missing now. Employees can only deepen specificity inside the first name's stack. Specificity up, switching cost up. Bargaining looks like a skill premium. It is a lock. Clouds will add a batch of ASIC-compiler jobs. That is job creation. It does not automatically lower the switching tax on the stack. If power jams first, cloud hiring will first cut delivery and civil works, not the right to define the cabinet itself. If the East stands, labor will see two certifications and two grade ladders, not one globally unified talent market. The only one of the five that can rewrite the employment-contract structure overnight is a breakup or a forced opening of the software stack. It has not been done. Once the window closes, the stacks the labor market can choose are fewer, not more.

## 9. Analysis added: scenarios, counters, testable indicators

Analysis added. No unemployment-rate figure. Any quantitative "X% of jobs disappear," other than the exposure rates already sourced above, is not written in this volume. Exposure rates must not be rewritten as unemployment rates.

Scenario A: task substitution faster than job substitution. Firms keep titles, shrink junior headcount, hand mid-level steps to Agents, and use the wage saved against cabinet depreciation and prepayments. Wage polarization happens by layer: workflow designers, cabinet and packaging technicians, on-site responsibility jobs rise; textualizable mid-level jobs stall. This is the path that most closely fits the ILO's "change tasks, not the job roster first" and the draft's "Agents mature period." Four-cloud capex is still adding, which means buyers have not stopped expanding. Financial pressure on this path comes from depreciation and commitments, not from demand that has already collapsed.

Scenario B: usage is not enough. Substitution is called off. Clouds and software firms first cut people unrelated to AI delivery. Customer-side substitution projects stall. What the labor market sees is not "AI stole the meal," but "AI-project layoffs." The trigger is not a token unit price this volume invented. It is buyers cutting group-wide capex. Mixed-year 2025 is still adding, not already cut. Testable: whether Microsoft PPE, Amazon net equipment purchases, Google equipment purchases, and Meta company-definition capex all turn at once. Before they turn, do not write one firm's project layoffs as an aggregate turning point.

Scenario C: edge decentralization. Edge models become strong enough that a device can run top-tier intelligence. Individual workers again own a small piece of the means of production. Seat licenses break down more thoroughly. Income moves to platform take-rates and device installments. The counter is oligopoly endgame: compute locked by a few clouds and Nvidia, workers generally becoming distribution channels. The draft gave probabilities. None were checked. This volume carries no frequencies. It only leaves the switch: if the edge does not scale, cloud tokens remain the scale entry, and substitution concentrates in large firms that already bought cloud seats; if the referee is not forcibly opened, the weight of the distribution-channel scenario rises. Both are hypotheses from here. They are not forecasts.

Five counters to block. Extrapolating a whole occupation disappearing from one demo video. Writing cheaper DeepSeek training as "human cognitive labor has no value." Writing IMF 40% / 60% exposure as already unemployed. Writing 10% of annual revenue as a fine about to be issued, then inferring "regulation will save white-collar." Adding four-cloud mixed-year capex into AI hardware, then inferring "depreciation must cut mid-level intelligence." All five exceed the public record.

Testable indicators take only public, non-invented targets. Whether software firms break inference cost out into cost of sales. Whether firms disclose the number of processes replaced by Agents, not an "AI employee count." Whether cloud incremental revenue and PPE / equipment purchases can be matched in direction — not knocked into a rent rate. The ratio in hiring texts of "multi-model scheduling / workflow / cabinet / liquid cooling / packaging" to "single-framework fine-tune." Whether public hiring for nuclear, liquid-cooling, and advanced-packaging jobs rises in step with GPU software jobs. Whether justice.gov shows *United States v. NVIDIA*. Whether the French authority has issued a statement of objections. Whether the European Commission has a 101/102 opening press release. Whether TSMC's next annual report first-customer share keeps leaving 19%. Whether supply commitments keep leaving $119.0B. For these items: if there is a notice, write the notice. If there is no notice, leave it blank.

## 10. After mid-level intelligence is sliced, organizations flatten. Responsibility does not

Analysis added. The mid-layer that used to coordinate junior staff is itself a textualizable step. If Agent workflows truly run stably, firm titles will fall and project systems will rise. That need not lower total labor compensation. It will change how compensation is paid: more by delivered steps, less by seniority. The IMF 2024 note wrote: if AI complementarity with high-income workers is strong, labor-income inequality may rise; capital returns will push wealth inequality up; if productivity gains are large enough, most workers' wage levels may still rise. That is a conditional. It is not an already-realized Gini coefficient.

Intelligence rent has three collection points. A discussion draft added a fourth. Model weights, compute quota, workflow data, plus the stack at the referee corner. If an employer only takes employee-produced text, fine-tunes it, and rents it back, employees are creating their own substitute. The firm strategic sentence writes "avoid proprietary-API lock-in." The matching sentence for individuals is the same sentence. Steps that can be published, checked, and rolled back will price as tokens first. Steps that must be signed, must be on site, must answer for yield and for bodies still price as wages and insurance. Unions and licenses block the latter being wrongly priced as the former. They can block responsibility. They cannot block exposure.

The China stack's labor ceiling is here too. If domestic R&D catches up and tears open a piece with capacity and price, it can stand a parallel bid and feed engineers on a second referee. If it does not stand, the West has only one bidder left, and domestic cognitive labor at most parks on the phone layer and high-price peer pricing inside the iron curtain. Those who can pay are high-price peers. Those who cannot take residual. It is not that the foundry is unpatriotic. The foundry wants profit. "Domestic CXMT exists, therefore PCs and AI have bits" does not hold. PCs take residual DIMMs. AI does not get equivalent stacks. Still unresolved. People follow the layer: people on the phone layer stay; people on the AI layer cannot buy equivalent tools; a productivity gap becomes a wage gap. This volume has no cross-strait wage-gap survey. It does not invent a multiple.

The five paths: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). A cognitive leap does not fill the window. Admissions do not fill a photoresist gate. What this volume can write is only that these constraints are already standing. Headcount stays blank.

## 11. Gaps and what must not be treated as fact

The draft of Volume 38 had not one labor statistic. This volume does not invent unemployment rates, wage medians, jobs-lost counts, admissions, or starting pay. IMF 40% / 60% / 40% / 26% and advanced-economy 27% / 33% are exposure and complementarity classifications, not unemployment. The ILO explicitly wrote that exposure indicators must not be used to forecast job losses. OECD "no negative aggregate yet" is an observation as of that study's publication, not a permanent guarantee.

Cannot be written as already happened: a DOJ complaint, a settlement, a Mellanox split, a forced opening of CUDA; a French statement of objections, a French or EU fine; "a 10% of annual revenue fine about to be issued"; a White House or Pentagon text already forbidding a breakup; four-cloud "AI hardware capex $215B"; the four clouds paying NVIDIA the entire $193.7B; SaaS gross margin already walking from 85% to 55%; a country where "X% of jobs have already been replaced by AI."
