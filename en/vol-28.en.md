# Volume 28: Two thermodynamic regimes (BOM 35/20/25; Thor versus 120 kW)

Chinese original: [vol-28.md](vol-28.md)

## 1. The question this volume answers

If embodied intelligence is to turn tokens inside a cabinet into torque on a factory floor or in a home, which parts carry the cost and the power? This volume takes apart only three things: the whole-machine BOM the draft gave; the physical break between edge compute and a cloud cabinet; and whether the actuator and sensor supply chain will copy the pattern of NVIDIA collecting a software premium.

The deeper pass already set the tone. Volume 28 hangs only on residual bits. It does not hang a new filing. Thor at 50W to 100W, more than 1000 TOPS, and actuators at 35% remain draft specs. There is no whole-machine dollar figure and no shipment. What can be wired to the main line is only this: the default edge brain sits on the athlete's module; consumer goods and robots compete with phones and PCs for the same residual bits. Cabinet selling prices are high enough that even expensive memory cannot crush the profit on one accelerator board. In PCs and phones, 16G from about 450 to 1800 breaks the whole machine first. Do not write that a robot revolution has already broken out.

Samsung's $16.54B long contract (announced at KRW 22.76T, through 2033) is Tesla. Musk named the Taylor fab for the sixth-generation automotive chip. That is a parallel foundry river for automotive AI. It is not proof of humanoid whole-machine shipments, and it is not a diversion of four-cloud training cabinets. Official cloud-ASIC memory specs (Maia / TPU / Trainium / MTIA) serve inference and training inside halls. They do not serve 50W to 100W next to a joint. Do not write two thermodynamic systems as one.

## 3. Mechanism: two thermodynamic systems; residual bits reach the joint last

### 3.1 Cloud cabinet 120 kW; one edge chip 50W to 100W

A cloud cabinet is 120 kW. One edge Thor is 50W to 100W (draft numbers). Three orders of magnitude. Analysis added here: embodied intelligence cannot move "unified 13.4 TB of memory" next to a joint. The on-body model must be distilled, quantized, and cut into perceive–plan–control layers. Heavy retraining and mass simulation can stay in the cloud. The online loop must live inside 50W to 100W and battery energy density. Whoever controls the cloud training fabric controls the robot's "brain before it leaves the factory." Whoever controls supply and the SDK for the 50W-to-100W module controls the "brain after it leaves." The draft wrote the latter as Jetson Thor, which hands the default edge seat to NVIDIA.

The judgment's official single cabinet is 72 GPUs, 36 Grace, 13.4 TB HBM, 17 TB LPDDR. Grace exists on the server side. That means they do not want to leave the CPU slice to a rival either. A demotion is not an extinction. Thor next to a joint is an accelerator, not a general server CPU. Automotive FY25 $1,694M shows that profit is not in the old general automotive-electronics scope. It also does not prove humanoids have entered that segment. No shipments. Do not write share.

### 3.2 BOM power is not in the GPU. It is in whether the machine can turn

Actuators plus reducers at 35% sit above compute at 25% and perception at 20% (draft numbers). Analysis added here: yield, noise, life, and unit price on harmonic reducers, planetary roller screws, and frameless torque motors decide whether a whole machine can walk from prototype to a line. That is the same class of constraint as advanced packaging being tight and unable to meet demand. What is scarce is repeatable manufacture, not a demo video. C.C. Wei, 4Q24: CoWoS tight / cannot meet demand. 2Q26: limits customers' growth. Packaging and test revenue about 8% in 2024, a bit over 10% in 2025. CoWoS monthly capacity from 13k to 130k wafers is a draft number in the Volume 24 title. It is not a TSMC split. This volume does not promote it to verified. If the 35% band is held by a few reducer and screw makers, the robot industry will have a "non-NVIDIA bottleneck party." Gross margin need not reach 60% to 75%. Lead time can still freeze the whole machine.

The remaining 20% is unsplit. Battery and structure can invert the ranking. 35 / 20 / 25 are shares, not an audited cost book. There is no whole-machine dollar BOM, no annual volume, and no revenue or shipments from any whole-machine company. Frameless torque motors, harmonic reducers, and planetary roller screws have no unit prices, lead times, or supplier shares. Do not invent them.

### 3.3 Perception at 20% is reliability and litigation cost

Six-axis force, tactile, binocular (the draft's list) decide whether a robot can stop its hand under unknown contact force. Analysis added here: a cloud model's error is a wrong token. A robot's error is a newton. Closed-loop frequency, calibration, and redundancy in perception and control are not directly improved by MLA's 93.3% KV compression. Stuffing a language model into a joint does not solve force control. Cloud-side MLA cutting KV by 93.3% and lifting concurrency nearly 10x is a memory ledger for text decode. Joint servos want deterministic latency and force feedback, not more tokens per second. Using a large model as a real-time controller steps on the memory wall and the control-system sampling-period wall at once. The sane split is cloud-side skill learning and simulation; edge 50W-to-100W modules for inference and safe stop. Liquid-cooling CDUs and nuclear metrics in the investment guidance serve the first half, simulation and training, not the joint itself.

### 3.4 Commercially, NVIDIA may repeat the cabinet play. It may not collect 40% to 45%

Software and brand premium inside a cloud cabinet at 40% to 45%, $1.10M to $1.40M (draft numbers), is an estimate, not a 10-K. Thor is written only as 50W to 100W and more than 1000 TOPS. There is no module unit price, no precision (INT8, FP8, or sparse), and no supply volume. Analysis added here: if whole-machine price sits more than an order of magnitude below a cabinet (the draft gave no whole-machine dollar price, so do not invent one), the absolute dollars in a 25% compute BOM are limited. NVIDIA is more likely to collect rent on volume and on a CUDA / Isaac-class software stack than on a $1.10M premium per unit. Whether it locks depends on whether whole-machine makers can run the same control graph off Thor. Nemotron / NeMo / NIM exist. There is no split. Do not write the model player as a second robot profit pool.

FY26 gross margin 71.1%, quarterly 75.0%. However expensive the whole cabinet, a memory price rise can be swallowed. Cabinet selling prices are high enough that even expensive memory cannot crush the profit on one accelerator board. In PCs and phones the memory share is high. 16G from about 450 to 1800 breaks the whole machine first. If a robot stuffs consumer-grade bits into a joint controller, what breaks first is the whole-machine BOM, not cabinet profit.

### 3.5 Residual bits: robots bid against phones and PCs for the same bits

Analysis added here. After makers shift lines to HBM, consumer bits shrink. HBM eats about three times the wafer of ordinary DRAM per GB. Move one line and residual bits shrink more. TrendForce: HBM plus RDIMM about 51% of 2026 DRAM bit supply. More than half of the memory increment already goes to cabinets. Channel: 16G DDR5 about 450 to 1800. Supply cannot keep up with cabinets times spec. What remains is split among phones, PCs, consumer drives, and robots — a few dozen brands. HDDs are not inside the cabinet. They sit next to cloud storage and are still sucked by hall budgets.

CXMT's prospectus: 0 mentions of HBM. 350k wafers per month is not an announcement. Do not write it. Once the international price holds, makers sell first to the high bidder. Domestic memory does not automatically transfuse domestic robots. At home, China can at most supply the phone layer. PCs and AI cabinets do not resolve. The layer is wrong. If a robot whole machine still wants leading-edge bits, it bids at that price. If it cannot pay, it takes residual.

### 3.8 Geography: actuator supply and chip supply sit in different places

Analysis added here. Harmonic and screw capacity has traditionally sat closer to East Asian precision manufacturing. Blackwell and HBM sit closer to TSMC and Hynix. Export controls can freeze Thor and high-end GPUs. They need not freeze motors at the same time. Conversely, if screws and reducers become the bottleneck, a country with precision manufacturing sits stronger in embodied intelligence than it sits on an NVLink fabric. The draft gave no volume numbers for Chinese robot parts. The above is structural inference only. At home, China can at most supply the phone layer. PCs and AI do not resolve. If robots are to close the loop at home, what is missing is HBM-class bits and advanced packaging, not another "humanoid revolution" headline. CXMT follows price. It does not automatically transfuse.

Prepayments: $95.2B at FY26 year-end, $119.0B at Q1 FY27 end. Substantially all to be paid in fiscal 2027. TSMC 2025 HPC 58% of revenue, up 48% year on year. What is being opened is HPC and packaging. If a robot edge module wants the same material, it pays more or does not get it. If capital first protects data-center returns, whole-machine financing is pushed back. That is a scenario, not a measured capital flow.
