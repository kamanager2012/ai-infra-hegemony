# Volume 07: NVIDIA Vertical Expansion and AI Factory Control

Chinese original: [vol-07.md](vol-07.en.md)

Four-corner mechanism: [Volume 2](vol-02.en.md). Moat tables: [Volume 32](vol-32.en.md). The China stack: [Volume 12](vol-12.en.md). This volume does not reopen the full FY26 table. This volume only writes where expansion's cash comes from, and whether DGX Cloud has already become a business.

## 1. Core question

How far has NVIDIA expanded beyond accelerator sales, and which forms of expansion strengthen platform control rather than merely add adjacent products?

The current framework tracks expansion across:
- rack-scale systems and networking;
- CUDA/PTX and deployment software;
- reference architectures and AI Factory design;
- cloud and financing partnerships;
- robotics / automotive / edge platforms;
- model and inference deployment products.

The key measure is not how many markets appear in a press release, but whether these layers reinforce system-stack adoption, resource reservation, switching costs, and recurring workload deployment.

## 3. Dig into the mechanism

### 3.1 The sales unit moves up: from card to cabinet

The main line's first sentence: the sales unit moved from the chip to the cabinet. Customers no longer compare single-card FLOPs. They compare whether they can enter a 72-card high-speed domain. Once inside the domain, the next generation is bought inside the same domain. software/interface layer and system-sales layer are welded together.

This is not "never made cards." Gaming and professional visualization have always been there. What changed is selling training as a cabinet by default. Once the cabinet is the default sales unit, expansion has its first layer: every later vertical story has to pass this layer first. Without the cabinet, auto and robots are only edge modules. With the cabinet, cloud halls, liquid cooling, power, and copper-cable checklists get written as "compatible with this one name."

Hypothesis from here: once the cabinet becomes the checklist, the next tender uses the same sheet. Contract manufacturers, liquid-cooling plants, power-supply plants, and copper-cable plants are organized onto that sheet. NVIDIA need not acquire them. It only has to specify them. This is the supply-chain version of expansion: control the interface definition, not the equity. Full-year purchases of property and equipment were only $6.042B, yet sellers still expand to its mix. The number is already this logic. TSMC 2025 gross margin 59.9%, Hynix operating margin 49%: both are names that make money on its mix, not names bidding against it for cabinets.

The physical unit of expansion is the cabinet, not the chip. Liquid cooling, power conversion, and copper cable write "NVIDIA-compatible" as a data-center engineering standard. Once a customer builds a hall to that thermal design and power feed, the next purchase's switching cost is poured into concrete and busbars. That is harder than a trademark. A trademark can be swapped. A busbar cannot.

Networking needs its own sentence. Inside FY26 data center, compute $162.361B, networking $31.376B. Expansion does not rest on GPUs alone. High-speed interconnect, switch trays, and in-cabinet copper weld 72 cards into one domain. When the customer buys compute, it is already buying networking. Half of the second-layer lock is written in networking: switching vendors is not swapping accelerator cards. It is tearing apart parallelism already written to this name's semantics. [Volume 1](vol-01.en.md) confirm those two rows to the CFO platform table. This volume does not write $31.376B as "interconnect is already a monopoly." It writes that the expanding sales unit already contains interconnect. Without this slice, a cabinet is still a pile of cards. With it, a cabinet is a domain.

### 3.2 Strong cash flow and order certainty can add a tranche of prepayments

The mechanism is in [Volume 1](vol-01.en.md). This volume does not reopen the table. The three-month rise from $95.2B to $119.0B is already an official record of adding a tranche. Expansion is not spending free cash down to build its own wafer fab. Expansion is turning part of free cash into supply commitments, returning part to shareholders, and staying asset-light. That is not the same road as Intel in 1999 turning profit into its own plants.

### 3.3 Buyer and rival in one body

The clouds are the largest buyers, and they are also the owners of in-house accelerators. NVIDIA sells them cabinets, locks their tenants to its own instruction set with CUDA, and keeps an official-cloud product form so the option remains: "I can also rent compute to model firms around you." Model firms therefore face three layers of rent: the cabinet, the cloud markup, and a possible official cloud. That is what "vertical industrial trust" means as mechanism — it need not finish a legal monopoly. It only has to make the second-best choice at each layer lose a slice of efficiency or a slice of schedule. Cloud in-house specs: [Volume 4](vol-04.en.md). Cloud ASICs are the same auction, not another river.

### 3.4 The official cloud is an option, not a business line

Multi-year cloud-service commitments rose from $27.0B to $30.0B. Official text writes them as R&D. Audit marked an earlier commitment of the same kind as the anchor for "R&D plus official-cloud offerings." Neither place gives official-cloud revenue, GPU-hours, gross margin, or a split with the four clouds.

As mechanism it looks like an option: a reminder to Amazon, Microsoft, and Google that model firms can rent cabinets from NVIDIA without going through them. An option becomes a business only with a revenue line. Without a revenue line, "cloud expansion" in the title is only a channel threat. This volume writes this layer as "a product form exists, multi-year cloud commitments exist, disclosed revenue does not." The report does not treat the $30.0B commitment as the size of a cloud business.

### 3.5 Vertical extension is a second curve, not the base

Historical engineering assumptions included an automotive single-chip FP4 figure and an edge-module share of a humanoid-robot BOM. Neither is used as a current fact because there is no matching first-party teardown, contract, or segment disclosure. [Volume 1](vol-01.en.md) has no auto or robot split. The deepening note's FY25 automotive $1,694M against that year's data center $115,186M already shows the scale: the vertical story is large in the narrative and still a corner on the income statement.

Automotive qualification and edge power envelopes vary by product and program. The durable mechanism is that once a vehicle platform standardizes around a compute/runtime stack, qualification cost and software integration can make switching slower than in cloud workloads. That is one lock opened onto two industries, not two new moats. If the unified-compute-architecture sentence "95% of frontier operator libraries bound" holds, auto and robots are only the lock's radius. That sentence has no sample. This volume does not promote it.

Geography will crop vertical expansion. A full 72-card cabinet is an in-quota product. Out-of-quota markets see a cut-down GPU, a broken interconnect, and a cloud that cannot be used. If auto and edge can still ship, the software lock remains. If the cabinet cannot ship, system-level expansion is an empty sentence on the ground, and local clouds are forced onto in-house accelerators and domestic accelerators as the main path. That is not the China market rebalancing global allocation. It is controls cutting the fourth layer in two.

### 3.6 The China market cannot save the globe

The layer is wrong. See [Volume 12](vol-12.en.md). This volume does not reopen it. If expansion is done in the West, the eastern parallel stack serves people who cannot buy. The relevant competitive test is whether alternative Western and Chinese stacks can gain real workloads, supply access, and software portability—not simply whether products exist.

### 3.7 Miss three bundle checks and you hand over the new lines

The three checks and the five interrupts are in [Volume 8](vol-08.en.md). This volume does not reopen them. As long as there is no split, no forced-open interface, and no ban on locking capacity with prepayments, a fine cannot cover the bid.

### 3.8 The profit center moved to the cabinet. A CPU downgrade is not extinction

Discussion asked that this sentence sit in the expansion volume. The profit center is already on accelerators and cabinets. Scheduling, memory management, and the edge still need a CPU. NVIDIA ships Grace itself. The official cabinet is 36 of them. What is downgraded is "the CPU is still the system's main character," not "the rack no longer needs one." If the end-state is written as PC brands becoming system contract manufacturers, the host becoming supreme, and rivals vanishing, mark it as a scenario only. Rivals are here today. The report does not treat who exits in which year.

FY26 R&D of **$18.497B** shows the scale of investment supporting architecture, software, interconnect, systems, and roadmap development in an asset-light operating model. It is not wafer-fab depreciation. The roadmap and supply-coordination layer sets the Hopper-to-Blackwell-to-Rubin mix. The software/interface layer sets the unified compute architecture and the cabinet standard. Expansion uses those two corners to sell the system-sales layer's cabinets. The model/deployment extension is a side move that lays a catalog downstream of the cabinet. Miss one of the platform-control layers and expansion is a cut shorter. All four are in place now. The revenue bulk still recognizes only the system-sales layer. This volume writes to that asymmetry. It does not promote the last three corners to already-split profit lines.

## 4. Scenarios, counterarguments, and testable indicators

the main scenario, 2026 to 2028, keeps stacking revenue in the data center. FY26 is already $193.737B. The last two quarters of gross margin are back near 75%. While the margin holds, supply commitments keep rising. Auto and robots contribute narrative and a second curve. They do not replace the data center at once. Whether a trust exists is not how many industries a press release entered. It is whether the clouds can turn the unified compute architecture from default to optional in outward products.

Counter one: the four clouds move training and inference in volume onto in-house accelerators, and NVIDIA falls back to "selling only peak-training cabinets." The main line already wrote official memory specs for those ASICs. This counter is not a fantasy. Public next generations have not stopped, so it has not won. Counter two: antitrust splits the cabinet bundle, software must be quoted alone, and gross margin gives back from near 75%. Inquiries exist. Complaints do not. Counter three: an open compile stack drops the instruction-set dependency to a portable level. The embryo is there. It is not through. Counter four: if the official cloud never discloses a stand-alone revenue line, cloud expansion in the title is only a channel threat. Counter five: power stops the buildout, cabinet shipments are pinned by plants and transformers, and prepayments can lock wafers but cannot lock watts.

Testable markers: whether data-center revenue is still growing on the FY26 $193.737B scale; whether gross margin still sits in the official 71.1% to 75.0% band; whether free cash is still near $96.575B a year, enough to keep adding supply commitments; whether supply commitments still rise from $119.0B, and whether official text still writes locking inventory and capacity; whether purchases of property and equipment stay far below seller capex; whether public tenders still close on 72-card cabinets rather than loose cards; whether the official cloud has a stand-alone revenue line; whether auto and edge have volume contracts and a revenue line. Cabinet counts, packaging by customer, and a 2nm first-silicon dollar amount are not filled in here.

write expansion as four layers and the levels clear. Layer one is done: the sales unit moved up to the cabinet, the data center is the base, FY26 $193.737B. Layer two is underway: high-speed-interconnect semantics, software/runtime dependence, and long-duration supply commitments raise the performance, engineering, and lead-time cost of switching. The three-month add of $23.8B in commitments is layer two's cash record. Layer three is the official cloud in the title: as mechanism, an option on the clouds; as number, $27.0B to $30.0B of multi-year cloud commitments, not revenue. Layer four is auto and edge: materials and FLOPs are historical engineering assumptions; shipments and revenue are empty. If the word trust is used, it applies only to the present in which layer two is nearly done and layer three is not yet disclosed: rent can already be collected inside the cabinet, and there is still no proof of rent outside the cabinet or on the industry side.

where expansion stops can also be drawn. Stop at the cabinet: while the data center is still the base, margin still 71% to 75%, and commitments still rising, layer one still holds. Stop at the lock: while competing system stacks have not reduced migration cost and resource dependence at scale, layer two still holds. Stop at the cloud: while the official cloud still has no stand-alone number, layer three is still an option. Stop at the industry: while auto and robots still have only FLOP slogans and a materials share, and no revenue line, layer four is still a mock-up. NVIDIA can announce all four layers at once. Filings so far give hundred-billion evidence only for layer one, and a prepayment-add evidence for layer two. This volume respects that asymmetry.

PC brands becoming system contract manufacturers is an end-state scenario, not the present. The shape of the scenario: once demand steadies, the system-brand slice becomes contract manufacturing of certified systems, structured like a brand to a contract manufacturer, with chip foundry still at TSMC. On AI servers and workstations, contract manufacturers shipping certified systems has already started — that is a public contract-manufacturing relation, not an equity buyout. Whether consumer notebooks can still be produced in a non-NVIDIA design, and whether Intel and AMD acceleration-compute revenue disappears from filings, are testable items. If there is no number, leave it empty. Do not forecast rivals exiting. The report does not treat consumer-electronics brands becoming software trademarks. The report does not treat that hardware supremacy is already complete.

buyer-and-rival in one body also has a regulatory interface. The clouds are mixed-year buyers at a two-hundred-billion capex scale, and they are also potential rivals outside the cabinet. If regulation only limits "do not sell to the clouds," the cabinet standard can still detour through industry customers and the official cloud. If regulation limits software bundled with the cabinet, the premium is the thing that gets opened. The main line: inquiries exist, complaints do not. This volume writes regulation as a possible split tool, not as a result already on the ground. A fine does not change the bid. A split or a forced-open interface would. After national security writes advanced chips as national security, the second is harder to land.

Second-best choices also layer. on the data-center side, the second-best is Ethernet plus an in-house accelerator. historical stress-test material wrote Ethernet bandwidth and latency a slice worse, and in-house accelerators still awkward on external dynamic shapes. On the auto side, the second-best is an in-house domain controller or an older platform; the cost is qualification years and a function gap. On the robot side, the second-best is a self-built edge module; the cost is turning historical stress-test material materials share into another supply chain. The expansion strategy is to make every second-best look like "it works, but this generation loses." This is not a closed industrial organization. It is a power design still underway.

The clouds' counters also layer. in-house accelerators hit layer-one volume, compilers hit the layer-two lock, regulation hits the bundle, self-built plants hit the hall's dependence on the checklist. The main line named all four tools. It did not give "how much share has already been knocked off." This volume therefore writes expansion as a power design still underway, not as a closed industrial organization. If those five are not done, the line walks past half of the scarce slice. The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down. Phones and PCs can absorb memory residual pressure while platform rules remain concentrated.

gaming cards giving way is evidence that expansion has already moved, not industry publicity. FY25 official text already admitted: Blackwell and Ada generation gaming cards were in limited supply, material given to the data center. This volume has no new FY26 gaming split. Do not promote that sentence to "gaming has already been abandoned." What can be written is the mechanism: the same leading-edge logic, the same packaging, and the system-sales layer can change priority inside a quarter. Expansion's first cut lands on its own gaming cards, not on someone else's cars. Outside, people still talk vertical industry. Inside, material has already been moved from the end below 75% gross margin to the end near 75%.

why the three bundle checks must arrive together can also be written as a bid. Bind only prepayments, with no software base, and customers can still use another compiler outside the cabinet; the switching cost is only a change of foundry queue. Bind only software, with no prepayments, and TSMC still allocates incremental capacity across customers using commercial and strategic criteria, and weaker buyers may receive less favorable timing or volume. Bind only memory longs, without the first two, and commodity bits can be reserved while training systems still compete for the same advanced-packaging pool. All three together, and TSMC faces the financial fact that it cannot recognize only one buyer, makers face the contract fact that they cannot cut every line over to HBM, and the clouds face the engineering fact that the tax of changing base moves from one vendor to one stack. All three are missing now. While they are missing, ordering alone is handing over each year's new lines and packaging.

"the China market cannot save the globe" has one more layer that must not be written wrong. The East standing up a second bid is one of the main line's five interrupts. It is not already standing. Standing means: the eastern bid is large enough that makers reserve HBM and packaging for it alone, and Western flagship-chip design data pass the gate. They do not pass the gate now. CXMT's prospectus: 0 mentions of HBM. A domestic high-price market can redirect value capture toward domestic suppliers, but it does not by itself create a second global allocation pole. Writing "China is large" as "global cabinets will be reallocated" is filling an AI-layer hole with phone-layer scale. The layer is wrong. If the expansion volume uses China demand to balance the system-sales layer, it writes the main line backwards.

### 3.9 The model/deployment extension is a power extension, not a second profit pool

The platform-control layers have one more: it trains models itself. The product lines exist. Nemotron, NeMo, and inference microservices have public pages. The main line already wrote: the revenue bulk is still on cabinets. This volume does not invent ARR, and it does not write model services as a second profit pool that has already replaced the cabinet.

the expansion volume must write this corner, because it changes the customer's identity, not the income statement's structure at once. Clouds and model firms train on its stack; it then sells adjacent model services and workflows. The conflict of interest is structure: the software/interface layer sets the interface, the system-sales layer sells the cabinet, the model/deployment extension stands downstream of the cabinet. Customers find that "switching stacks" means switching the cabinet, the compiler, and the official model catalog at once. The switching cost moves from changing one vendor to changing one base. That is the same class of tool as the official-cloud option: a product form exists, used to raise the cost of leaving, not equivalent to a disclosed revenue line.

Hypothesis from here: for the model/deployment extension to become a profit pool, it must show up as its own line on the income statement. It does not now. Its present use is stretching expansion from a hardware radius to a workflow radius. If auto and robots are truly to become layer four, they walk this road too — lay the software catalog into the industry first, then wait for materials and contracts. The report does not treat the order backwards.

### 3.10 Compute rent: expansion collects rent, not a share manifesto

Rent scope: [Volume 8](vol-08.en.md). This volume lands it on the sales unit. The collection unit of rent is the cabinet, not the card. Customers pay by cabinet, halls are built by cabinet, memory multiplies by cabinet, packaging is queued by cabinet. Only layer one is done (the sales unit moved up). Layer two is adding commitments. Layers three and four are still an option and a mock-up.

### 3.11 A different expansion from Intel 1999

Intel 1999: revenue $29.389B, net income $7.314B, gross margin 59.7%. It expanded by building its own plants, setting its own socket, and letting customers dual-source. The next slice of capacity was not something one firm could lock with prepayments, so share would break. Today's expansion is the reverse: it almost does not build wafer fabs; full-year purchases of property and equipment $6.042B. Expansion runs on prepayments, a cabinet standard, and a software stack. Foundry sits in someone else's plant, memory at the makers, packaging on the seller's scarce slice. It buys the slot, not the building.

That difference decides how the end-state scenario is written. If PC brands become system contract manufacturers, it is not an Intel-style "the brand is eaten by the fab." It is "the brand is specified into contract manufacturing by the standard-setter." Chip foundry remains TSMC. NVIDIA does not need to become a wafer fab, and it does not need to buy system brands. What it needs is the checklist still there, prepayments still rising, and gross margin still 71% to 75%. Those three can be seen on the official table. Rivals exiting cannot.

This volume closes on one sentence. the part of expansion that has already happened is the sales unit moving up and the prepayment tranche being added. The part that has not happened is the official cloud becoming revenue, vertical industries becoming the base, PC brands becoming contract manufacturers, and rivals disappearing from the income statement. The title wrote the second half as a trust. The body wrote the first half as mechanism. The two halves cannot be welded into one sentence, "already complete." The window is still the few years before the next first-to-market node is locked by commitments. If any of those five is done, the line tilts. If not, the line walks past half of the scarce slice, and rack-scale systems continue to capture substantial value. What this volume can test is whether margin is still 71% to 75%, whether commitments still rise, and whether the data center is still the base. What it cannot test, leave empty.
