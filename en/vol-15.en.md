# Volume 15: 2036 endgame for the global AI industry (the compute map and a new order of production ten years out)

Chinese original: [vol-15.md](vol-15.md)

## 1. The question this volume answers

Set the clock to 2036. Will the global compute map still be "one systems firm, one foundry island, three memory makers, four clouds"? If intelligence truly becomes a means of production, who collects the rent? The Volume 15 draft had almost no endgame detail, yet it spread a forecast with probabilities: a four-axis table walking to 1nm, advanced packaging 450k wafers per month, a 1:5 penalty, four-cloud capex $1.1T, campuses 2–5 GW, plus oligopoly 40%, algorithm breakout 30%, hard landing 15%, dual track 15%. The discussion note and the audit already judged: that whole table does not enter the fact layer. Probabilities are not frequencies the world handed over. This volume changes the question. It does not ask "who takes 80% ten years out." It asks: from the numbers already nailed now, does the line walk past half of the scarce slice; and after it does, do the right to define the whole machine, the memory slot, and the interface standard still sit in the same firm.

The endgame is a set of switches, not a forecast. Until the switches are chosen, a winner list is invention. The draft's four mutually exclusive cells cannot hold a mixed cross-section: North American training cabinets still shipping to the coach's mix, East Asian manufacturing still biting leading-edge logic and stacks, Europe, India, and the Middle East absent from the Volume 15 original, edge inference partly utility-ized, cut orders in some years. Absence itself is a gap. You cannot patch the endgame with one sentence that "they will also build sovereign clusters." Added analysis.

The physical floor of this volume is not the 1nm cell. It is memory residual. Judgment section 4 and source 03 nail the same sentence: server DDR5 is already residual, not another river. TrendForce official note, 2026-08-25: 51% of 2026 DRAM bits are HBM plus RDIMM; memory as a share of major CSP capex is 47% in 2026 and 68% in 2027. Micron 10-K: the same bits of HBM take more wafers. Hot Chips paraphrase: the same capacity takes about 3× the silicon of DDR5. If clouds shift inference onto DDR5, they grab the slice already inside that 51%, or the slice already rising with it. It is a tax, not a new river. CXMT prospectus: 0 mentions of HBM. YMTC eats NAND price-ups. It cannot cover HBM, and it cannot cover the server-stick half of bits. The China market cannot save global cabinets, HBM, advanced packaging, or a unified compute architecture. At home it can at most do phones. PCs and AI do not resolve. The layer is wrong.

The four corners still hold. NVIDIA is athlete, referee, and coach at once, and it also trains models. The athlete sells GPUs, whole cabinets, and interconnect. The referee sets the unified compute architecture, the link, and the cabinet standard. The coach writes the Hopper-to-Blackwell-to-Rubin mix of HBM, advanced packaging, and links into the roadmap. The model player has Nemotron, NeMo, and inference microservices. The revenue bulk is still on cabinets. The sales unit already moved from the chip to the cabinet. A margin monopoly is not a share monopoly. Sellers are already rich: TSMC 2025 gross margin 59.9%, Hynix operating margin 49%. No buyer, selling cabinets, can outbid 71% to 75%. What is done: supply commitments to $119.0B. None of the five interrupts is done. If they are not done, the line walks past half of the scarce slice. The window is the few years before the next first-to-market node is locked by commitments. The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down. Phones and PCs pay a memory spillover tax. One firm writes the rules. Volume 9 residual and Volume 15 endgame share that sentence.

## 3. Push the mechanism forward

### 3.1 Residual is not transitional noise. It is the physical floor of the endgame

Added analysis. The Volume 15 draft wrote memory crowding as one cell on the four-axis table: from 1:3 to 1:4 in 2030 and 1:5 and above in 2036. That cell is a forecast. It does not enter the fact layer. What can be nailed is the present: half the bits already go to cabinets; silicon burn is harsher than bits; the three makers' books stand on the cabinet side. Extending those three sentences ten years does not require inventing 1nm and 5 GW. As long as maker boards still answer to shareholders and high-price longs still hold, the shift will not automatically walk back to consumer. Consumer bits shrink not because "the penalty becomes 1:5 ten years out," but because the same capacity already takes about 3× the silicon. Cut one line and consumer loses more than 1:1.

Server DDR5 is already inside the 51%. The easiest error in an endgame narrative is writing "shift inference onto commodity sticks" as an escape river. Source 03 closed that river. A shift buys RDIMM and consumer residual already inside the half, or already rising with it. Huaqiangbei 16G from 450 to 1,800 is talk that residual dies first. Memory as a share of CSP capex walking from 47% to 68% is an institutional measure of "hall budgets eaten by bits." If that 68% is still there in 2036, the bulk of the rent is not in model licenses. It is in bits and cabinets. Added analysis. On the rentier list, makers and cabinet sellers weigh more than application-layer brands.

3.1, 4.1, and 5.1 only earn the right to move from forecast to observation after 16-Hi is in volume and makers still say "one wafer counts as four." This volume does not write a sketch formula as a law of nature. What can enter the fact layer is the direction "more wafers" and "about 3×." Adding up to 4.1 or 5.1 is marked unverified; do not treat as audited fact. Test and burn-in are a hidden penalty outside the paraphrased 3×. Failures after 12-Hi bonding happen after the stack; what is sunk is twelve layers of die. Actual wafer burn may sit above the paraphrase. This volume does not give another multiple.

### 3.2 The line walks past half of the scarce slice. You do not need half the factory first

Added analysis. The Judgment and the forecast spine share one line: NVIDIA does not first need half of TSMC's whole factory. Occupy the increment and next year's slots are already its. Booked first-customer 19%, up from 12%, looks far. The increment has already tilted. HPC 58%, up 48% year on year: what is being opened is that slice and packaging. Packaging is already so tight it limits customer growth. HBM plus RDIMM already half of 2026 DRAM bits. Voice on the scarce slice is already larger than 19%. The window is not waiting for the book to hit 50%. It is the few years before the next first-to-market node and packaging expansions are slotted dead to the first name. After those years, bundling is also asking for residual.

At 5.39, about 100k cabinets take more than half of this packaging line. You do not need 800k cabinets to lock 3nm. Sell-side estimates 60%, 595k to 630k wafers, are not a TSMC split. The direction is the same line as "past half of the scarce slice, recognize only the first name." This volume does not promote sell-side research to a foundry split. What can be written is the mechanism: when the first name's profit exceeds the increment from everyone else, there is no financial reason to offend the first name for the second. This is not an administrative takeover. It is prepayments buying the slot. A neutrality pledge has no consideration in front of a check.

Acting alone means handing over each year's new lines and packaging. To change the allocation, bind three checks: prepayments large enough that the foundry cannot recognize only one buyer; software bound into one stack, so the switching tax becomes a change of base rather than a change of vendor; memory longs that leave a line for commodity bits. Miss one check and you are still picked off one by one. Apple does not bid against AMD for cabinets. What changes the cabinet is accelerators and cloud ASICs. Phone makers bind memory residual. They cannot bind training-cabinet stacks. None of the three checks is done. What is done: supply commitments to $119.0B.

Acceleration is the engine of the forecast, not a frequency that already happened. Demand rises, profit rises, more capacity and stacks are prepaid, rivals' costs rise, deliveries slow, profits thin, still less money to bind longs and switch stacks, share widens again. Only those five can interrupt it. Until they win, the line walks past half. The endgame Volume 15 should write is the cross-section of that line ten years out, not a point forecast on the 1nm cell.

### 3.3 Shifting onto commodity sticks is a tax. Cloud ASICs have not stopped either

Added analysis. The escape narrative has two versions. First: shift inference onto DDR5 and dodge HBM. Source 03 says that is a tax. Second: cloud ASICs open a new river of 5nm plus commodity sticks. Judgment section 5 says public next generations point the other way. Microsoft Maia 200, Ironwood, Trainium3, Meta MTIA 300: what they write is more advanced logic plus more HBM. The official shift from DDR to HBM is AWS Inferentia2. No firm was found saying it would retreat to commodity sticks as the inference mainstay.

What would actually dodge the scarce slice is a retreat to 5nm, less packaging, less stacking. Public next generations have not stopped. To beat today's cabinet you return to 3nm, HBM, and advanced packaging, and you bid against 75% gross margin. The result of that bid is not "the clouds took training off the cabinet." It is "the clouds and the cabinet seller add price on the same fab and the same stack line." Training is still on the cabinet. Inference is also leaning onto the cabinet. The switch "cloud ASICs take training" has not won. Until it wins, Volume 15 cannot write an obituary for the four corners.

Samsung's Tesla long is one parallel bid. KRW 22.76T through 2033. Musk named the Taylor fab. 16k, 24k, and 40k wafers per month are not announced. SMIC 5.1% cannot take Western reticles. If a parallel bid stands, the world is two stacks, not one stack rewritten by the second bid. If it does not stand, the West has only one bidder. Neither cross-section is "the world blows up."

### 3.4 The China bid is a parallel stack. It cannot save global cabinets

Added analysis. Judgment section 6: the China market cannot save global cabinets, HBM, advanced packaging, or a unified compute architecture. At home it can at most do phones. PCs and AI do not resolve. The layer is wrong. CXMT prospectus: 0 mentions of HBM. 350k wafers per month is not an announcement. YMTC official text writes above 200 layers. It has no monthly wafer-capacity in the tens of thousands. Once the international price holds, makers sell first to the high bidder. Huawei's high price is a rent unpaid to NVIDIA inside the iron curtain. Allocation keeps domestic bits from flowing out. It does not mean CXMT has been nursed to HBM.

Near gate: an administrative lock. Far gate: everything that is not silicon. If domestic advanced packaging and HBM-class bits can already hold a training cabinet in volume, the administrative lock is enough to keep a closed loop. If not, the lock only keeps the phone layer. This volume puts CXMT on the phone layer and the PC layer, not the AI layer. The layer is wrong. The sum is meaningless. Matching domestic phone shipments to HBM on global training cabinets is the wrong layer.

A trust gate, not a yield gate. To get AMD, Intel, Apple, Qualcomm, and MediaTek out of TSMC's queue, a China fab must do all three at once: process at the top, capacity that can take them, and the United States and those firms willing to hand over reticles. The third item is export control and the foreign direct product rule. Catch the first two and you still cannot take Western flagship chips. Those firms are still in the TSMC queue, and in that queue they still sit behind NVIDIA. So even if Eastern capacity rises, it is a parallel bid, not a substitute seat for these names at TSMC. However large China is, what it changes is who at home buys a domestic accelerator and who cannot buy NVIDIA. It does not change North American cloud plus unified compute architecture plus packaging plus HBM. Global training-cabinet mix is still set by the coach, the interface by the referee, and the bid still pressed by 71% to 75%. This is an AI game: the rule layer and the compute layer have already fused in one Western firm. If domestic R&D catches up and tears a piece open with capacity and price, a parallel bid can stand. If it cannot, the West has only one bidder. If it stands, the global market is still two stacks. "Cannot be broken" names this structure, not the absence of a China industry.

Profit has no nationality. After the three giants pull good capacity to HBM, conventional bits are expensive, and making conventional still pays. Domestic makers follow the floor. That is not a lack of patriotism. It is a demand for profit. Huawei pays and it gets the bits. It cannot pay and it takes residual like a phone maker. Cheapness alone does not punch through a unified compute architecture. The architecture is a ten-year developer stack. UXL and oneAPI exist. They are digging. They have not dug through. Hardware can be cheap. The switching tax is still there. A 75% pad means it can also follow a price cut down. The China stack lives on people who cannot buy NVIDIA, not on cheapness punching through the stack.

### 3.5 Three allocation rights: power-on, compile, regulate

Added analysis. The draft's first essay said that when compute itself yields cognitive value, how supply is allocated decides the redistribution of profit and wealth. 2036 has three allocation rights: who owns clusters that can be powered (PPAs and interconnection); who owns workflows and data that compile onto those clusters; who owns the regulatory stamp that defines precision and safety. The oligopoly branch stacks all three on the same set of North American firms. The edge branch dilutes the first right into a utility and returns the second to device makers and the open-source commons. The dual-track branch copies the three rights into two sets. The hard-landing branch temporarily breaks the capital structure of the first right. The physical plant need not change owners.

Labor is empty in the Volume 15 original. Titles for Volumes 38 and 39 appear only later in the book. This volume does not cross the border to invent employment elasticities. What can be said is mechanism: if agent workflows replace mid-skill cognitive labor, rent moves from the wage bill onto cluster depreciation, model licenses, and grid contracts. The split ratios are unknown. Concrete unemployment rates and tax bases are not in the draft. This volume does not invent them.

Energy rationing is a fifth order, consistent with Volume 10, and is not assigned a probability. If the 2036 map is colored by grid and nuclear contracts rather than by unified-compute-architecture share, it will look like oligopoly, but on the rentier list nuclear and ISOs will weigh more than GPU brands. Power interrupts "profit to prepayments to locked capacity" if and only if: cabinets that have been built cannot be powered, the athlete's revenue stops in inventory, and the coach can keep slotting and still cannot feed it out. That fork has not won. 2,600 GW of queue is not split. It does not enter the fact layer. PPA megawatts were not read from originals in this pass. Do not write power as a fourth corner. Nuclear counterparties are constraint-sellers, not coach or referee.

Time is inconsistent. The hard depreciation constraint sits in 2025–2028. A 500 MW SMR, if written in 2030–2035, and 1nm sit in the 2036 cell of the table. Endgame narratives easily read the last cell backward over the first ten years, as if the path were already fixed in the rear-view mirror. An honest reading: the first half is set by depreciation already spent, packaging tightness, half the bits already going to cabinets, and 5-to-7-year grid queues; only the second half reaches those 1nm and 5 GW cells on the table. If the first half takes a hard landing, the second half's $1.1T of capex will not appear. That $1.1T is a draft forecast cell, unverified; do not treat as audited fact.

### 3.6 The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down

Added analysis. The main line already wrote: the world is not a physical destruction. Upgrades stop, profit is extracted, and one firm writes the rules. Volume 15 should write that sentence as the default endgame cross-section, not as one cell among four mutually exclusive cells. The parts of the default cross-section are all present now: HPC eats the increment, packaging is tight enough to limit growth, half the bits go to cabinets, consumer sticks pay a 3× tax in the channel, CXMT follows price and does not open a large HBM project, public next-generation cloud ASICs still walk toward stacks, and none of the five interrupts is done. Put those parts ten years out and what you get is not "the globe stops." It is phones and PCs paying a memory spillover tax, replacement cycles lengthening, some advanced SKUs leaving inventory, general-purpose servers stepping down, and training cabinets still shipping to the coach's mix. Factories, channels, and whole-machine makers face three things: pay the tax, step down, or slip the date. There is no fourth item called "sit and wait ten years for a global shutdown." Brand death is a tail scenario. Do not name names.

Who can wait: clouds, cabinet sellers, memory makers, sovereign projects — they lock HBM, server sticks, and enterprise drives with longs. Who cannot wait: PCs, mid- and low-end phones, security, consumer storage, channel merchants. Those who cannot slip the date and cannot swap a qualified bit (auto, medical, industrial) hurt more, but that is the second wave. The first wave is still PCs, commercial PCs, general-purpose servers, and phones. Five to ten years is the longest hypothesis, not a published restart calendar. The only thing that overturns the empty window is makers expanding consumer capacity early. A 71%-to-75% versus 49% profit structure says there is no such motive now. Maker 49% is already fat, and they are still sellers. Sellers have no financial reason to offend a whole-cabinet contract for a consumer stick.

For persons and firms, the endgame implication is translated once: do not take a single interface, a single cabinet type, or a single-jurisdiction power contract as the ten-year default. Whichever switch it lands on, switching costs will be higher than in 2026 — either because oligopoly locks the stack, or because dual track splits the stack, or because a hard landing fragments the secondary ecosystem. Elastic scheduling is not a slogan. It is insurance against endgame uncertainty. This volume does not write the insurance as a product pitch.

First principles rewrite the switches. Do not assign percentages. Added analysis.
