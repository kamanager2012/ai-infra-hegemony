# Essay 6: The energy war and physical grid constraints (the 120 kW heat island, nuclear PPAs, and SMRs)

Chinese original: [part2-06.md](part2-06.md)

Whether the power interrupt has been done, mechanism: [Volume 13](vol-13.md). Three nuclear-deal narratives and the queue: [Volume 35](vol-35.md). This essay does not reopen contract originals. This essay only collects: the mechanism is there; it has not won now. SMRs are an option for the next decade, not this decade's supply.

## 1. The question this essay answers

Can power jam expansion dead? The judgment wrote it as one of five interrupts: if cabinets cannot take power, the athlete's revenue parks in inventory, and the coach can queue CoWoS and HBM and still cannot feed them out. This essay, as a macro piece, answers only that sentence. Volume 35 listed three nuclear-deal narratives. What the macro layer must ask is not "data centers have already gone nuclear." It is: after cabinet power density enters the hundred-kilowatt class and campuses enter the hundred-megawatt class, have interconnect licenses and nuclear contracts already become a third quota alongside packaging and HBM; are SMRs this decade's supply, or the next decade's option.

Nail the answer first. The mechanism is there. It has not won now. Without checked effective interconnect capacity, without a checked energization-failure rate, "the queue is long" cannot be written as "US power is exhausted," and unread PPA megawatts cannot be added into "already several GW of compute power." The draft's 2,600 GW queue, Three Mile Island 835 MW, Susquehanna planned 960 MW, Kairos combined 500 MW do not enter this essay's fact layer. They can stay in the draft's claims, used to check how far a public process has walked.

## 2. What the draft already argued (compressed; no key number dropped)

Density. Traditional cabinets 5 to 10 kW air-cooled; NVL72 120 kW, direct liquid cooling mandatory. 500 cabinets, 36,000 GPUs, instantaneous 60 to 80 MW, annual energy above 600 million kWh, about 60,000 US households. Copper versus optics saves 20 kW per cabinet. Power 6 × 33 kW, 480 V to 48 V, 97.5%. All of the above are draft numbers. NVIDIA's rack user guide separately writes: NVL72 rack power about 120 kW, power shelves six 5,500 W supplies, eight shelves, input about 33 kW per shelf. That is an approximate from product documentation, not a ten-year forecast, and not a thermal ledger this essay newly invented.

Queues. The draft wrote PJM, ERCOT, CAISO queues above 2,600 GW, average approval-and-build 5 to 7 years, data-center civil works 12 to 18 months. 2026 top campuses 100 to 300 MW, 2030 500 MW to 1 GW, 2036 2 to 5 GW. 2,600 GW and the last two campus-power bins are not promoted in this essay. 5 to 7 years is a draft engineering range. The direction matches Lawrence Berkeley Lab *Queued Up: 2026 Edition*: interconnect from application to commercial operation is years, not a few quarters; the queue has generation and storage, not a data-center-only list.

PPA narratives. Microsoft September 2024, a twenty-year contract, Three Mile Island Unit 1 restart as Crane; the draft wrote 835 MW dedicated, a diagram wrote amount above $16 billion. Amazon March 2024, the draft wrote $650 million buying Cumulus, next to Susquehanna 2.5 GW, planned direct supply up to 960 MW. Google October 2024, Kairos seven fluoride-salt reactors, the draft wrote combined 500 MW, in service 2030 to 2035. These three megawatt and dollar sets: PPA contracts and acquisition files were not read this pass. They do not enter the fact layer.

Finance. The draft wrote 2024 four-cloud $215 billion capex; 2026 forecast power and civil about $207.5 billion; stress-test power at 8 cents/kWh, PUE 1.2, about $28 billion a year. Nuclear operators were written onto the bottleneck-controller list at 60% to 75% gross margin. $215 billion is mixed-year group-wide equipment purchases rounded, not "AI hardware." See the judgment and the four-cloud scope check. Nuclear gross margin has no standalone statement. It cannot be set beside NVIDIA 71% to 75%.

## 3. Mechanism (physical / commercial / geopolitical)

**Physical meaning of a third quota.** Without a feeder, an advanced-packaging quota is interposers in a warehouse, HBM is chips in a warehouse. 120 kW lifts power from an opex line to a "can it be racked" constraint. Analysis added. Four-year hardware depreciation against a 5-to-7-year license clock is the most dangerous accounting mismatch: the asset-life financial clock is shorter than the power-license clock. Whoever first shortens the license clock (restarting an existing pile, building next to one, buying a campus that already has power) privatizes the third quota. That is a mechanism, not an already-happened outage table.

**Three contracts represent three technical maturities, not three GW already delivered.** Analysis added. Restarting an existing pile is fastest, the political symbol heaviest; next-to-pile direct connect is medium speed, and tests the grid's rules for large-user direct supply; SMRs are slowest, an option for next-decade campuses. Writing the three contracts as "tech firms have already solved energy" is treating an option as spot. What 2026 to 2028 can use is mainly the small slice of the first two that has finished licensing and actually delivers power. The last one does not match the packaging window in front of us.

How far public process can be nailed. NRC site page: former Three Mile Island Unit 1 permanently shut down 26 September 2019, licensed thermal power 2,568 MWt; late 2024 Constellation filed a restart intent; 13 May 2025 approved the rename to Christopher M. Crane Clean Energy Center; restart must simultaneously restore the operating-license basis, restore equipment to safe operation, and complete necessary upgrades. The NRC stood up a restart group. 3 June 2026 issued a draft environmental impact statement, comments due 8 July, final outlook the same September; several licenses to restore power operation still in review. 1 July 2026 approved receipt of new fuel. The page still also hangs SAFSTOR decommissioning status. Retrieval cutoff 26 August 2026: this is "walk regulation after signing," not already dedicated delivery to a data center. Source: https://www.nrc.gov/info-finder/reactors/ccec

On the Susquehanna path, what public news can nail is the regulator blocking a direct connect, not planned megawatts already interconnected. November 2024, FERC denied a revised interconnection service agreement 2–1, on the ground that the RTO had not shown the necessity of departing from the standard text. The denial was later upheld; the owner sued in the Fifth Circuit. Other reporting said the parties moved to a front-of-meter PPA and no longer the same behind-the-meter expansion. This essay did not read the full commission order or later PPA contracts. Do not write planned megawatts as already delivered. The mechanism that can be written: a large user next to a pile will be stopped by reliability, cost, and fair access. Signing is not energization.

On the Kairos path, what public sources can nail is a demonstration license, not a fleet in service. Federal Register: 21 November 2024 the NRC issued Kairos construction permits for two Hermes 2 test reactors at Oak Ridge. The company held a Hermes 2 groundbreaking on 17 April 2026, called it the first delivery under a Google master development agreement, and power to the TVA grid. The company's own window still points at the end of this decade into the first half of the next. Fluoride-salt reactors lack a continuous commercial-operation sample. Analysis added: tying national strategy to a 2030–2035 in-service window is isomorphic to tying supply to next-generation lithography delivery. SMRs are an option. Writing an option into a 2026 supply plan is the wrong clock.

**Why 2,600 GW cannot be treated as a power shortage.** Analysis added. The draft's set was not split into generation, storage, load, double-counting. Queue capacity is not data-center shortage, and still less "the athlete can no longer sell cabinets." LBNL interconnect reports exist, used to prove "queues are years, projects exit." They cannot be used to add the national queue into "potential compute" and then subtract "minus data centers and it is enough." What matters for AI is the small slice of high-voltage dedicated lines and main transformers that can actually deliver inside a few years. Treating total queue GW as potential compute is an order-of-magnitude error. This essay therefore leaves 2,600 GW at the draft layer.

**Commercially, a twenty-year power contract against a four-year server.** Analysis added. In a destocking scenario the power contract becomes a long liability; in an oligopoly scenario the power contract becomes a moat. Four-cloud scope: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). This money has to cover halls. It will also become civil works and power. It cannot be knocked into "GW already paid to a nuclear plant."

**Geopolitically, a nuclear license is national capacity, not cloud-vendor capacity.** Analysis added. The US still has restartable commercial piles and a relatively complete regulator. That is the premise of the Three Mile Island story appearing. FERC blocking a direct connect says the same national capacity can both permit and stop. Other economies with only chip subsidies and no license path will be absent from a far-dated large-campus scenario. Do not write power as a fourth corner. The nuclear counterparty is a constraint seller, not a coach or a referee. Hegemony's scope does not include every dollar of the grid.

Liquid cooling is the energy chain inside the cabinet. The draft wrote Auras, AVC, Vertiv into the BOM, $40,000 to $55,000 per cabinet. Analysis added. On 500 cabinets that is lead time and leak risk. The energy war is not only at the plant. It is also in cold plates and quick-disconnects. It explains "why a cabinet cannot make do with an ordinary air-cooled hall." It does not explain "power has already jammed expansion dead."

## 4. Analysis added (scenarios, counters, testable indicators)

Analysis added. Power extrapolations unverified. Do not treat them as audit fact.

Scenario A: existing-pile restarts finish on the regulatory calendar; next-to-build finds an executable path in front-of-meter or direct-connect rules; SMRs land on time or slip a little. This decade sees a batch of campuses with a nuclear label. GW-class is still a case. The third quota becomes joint rent for clouds and nuclear operators. This is "power starts to look like quota," not yet "power has already jammed expansion dead."

Scenario B: regulation limits large-user queue-jumping; the next-to-build model is retroactively failed; planned direct supply falls back into the queue. Chips keep waiting on power. Civil spend becomes concrete. A hard landing and an energy constraint happen at once. This branch is the closest to the judgment's "power jams expansion dead." There is now no public energization-failure rate to support that it has already happened.

Scenario C: algorithms and the edge pull cabinet power back from the hundred-kilowatt class. Nuclear long contracts become overhedge. The contract is still there. The load is not enough. This is destocking's mirror on the power ledger.

The five paths: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). Evidence that the power fork has not won is not "nobody talks nuclear." It is: cabinets are still shipping; the NRC site page still writes a potential restart; SMRs are still on a demonstration license; FERC can still send a direct connect back. The athlete's revenue has not parked in inventory.

Counter one: substituting RECs for watts. RECs solve book emissions. They do not solve feeders and main transformers. Counter two: using 2,600 GW to prove power is ample. A queue is not dispatchable capacity. Counter three: using a 2036 2-to-5 GW plan to prove 2026 already has power. A plan is not delivery. Counter four: nuclear operators have already entered the 60% to 75% gross-margin band. No plant statement. This essay deletes that juxtaposition.

Testable indicators list only observation items. Do not pre-fill results. Whether the NRC issues a license to restore power operation, and whether commercial delivery appears, rather than remaining parked on an EIS and fuel receipt. Whether the Susquehanna path stays denied, is rewritten by a court, or walks front-of-meter instead. Whether the Kairos window is still written in the first half of the next decade, and whether the demonstration pile is still parked on a construction permit. Whether public queue times are still years. Whether newly filed campus power leaves the draft's 100 to 300 MW. Whether NVIDIA next quarter is still adding supply commitments, rather than explaining revenue as "waiting on power." These indicators go back to regulatory originals and company releases. This essay does not audit them in their place.

The energy essay must be read with the manufacturing essay and the capital essay at once. Packaging loosening one notch only sends more hundred-kilowatt cabinets to knock on an already-long queue. The manufacturing ceiling loosens, the power ceiling tightens. The draft's 2026 power-and-civil $207.5 billion is an admission of this transfer, unverified. If that money is spent on campuses without a license, the accounting result is concrete. Spent on executable watts, the accounting result is a third quota. The same forecast, spent in the wrong place, the sign flips.

For non-US readers, this essay's evidence is all in three US regional grids and the NRC and FERC. Other grids' queue years, nuclear licenses, and large-user direct-supply rules all differ. 5 to 7 years and unchecked PPA megawatts cannot be carried over directly. What can be carried is the mechanism: once cabinet power enters the hundred-kilowatt class and campuses enter the hundred-megawatt class, the power-license clock will be slower than the chip clock, and a direct-connect existing pile will look more like "spot" than new wind and solar. SMRs in any jurisdiction look more like an option.

The sentence for nations is the same as Volume 40: licenses and main transformers must be written together with chip subsidies. Write only subsidies, and you get cabinets waiting on power. Write only environmental targets, not executable watts, and you get RECs. Neither fills four-year depreciation. This is not "power has already won." It is "if this fork is truly to win, what is missing is executable watts, not a slogan."

## 5. Gaps and what must not be treated as fact

PPA contracts and acquisition files were not read. $16 billion appears only in diagram text. 835, 960, 500 MW are nameplate or planned. They do not enter the fact layer. 2,600 GW includes non-AI projects, not split, does not enter the fact layer. 8 cents/kWh and PUE 1.2 are assumptions. 2030 and 2036 power are forecasts. China, Europe, India grids are not in the evidence. Nuclear-operator gross margin has no standalone line. 60 to 80 MW, 600 million kWh, 60,000 households are draft conversions; grid originals not checked this pass. This essay does not claim a nuclear-engineering audit, and does not claim a ten-year term was already read.

Writing nuclear as absolute political correctness or absolute political error helps no depreciation model. This essay only requires three things checkable: whether watts were actually delivered, whether the term is longer than four years, whether the license is executable. The three contracts currently sit in three states — "signed restart, regulation unfinished," "acquired next-to-build, direct connect blocked by the regulator," "far-dated pile order, demonstration just broken ground" — and cannot be added into GW already delivered to a hall. Adding them is publicity. Splitting by state is an operations map.

This essay still outputs no year's "cabinets stopped for power" unit count or GW. Without an energization-failure rate, a hard-written production stop can only be fiction. If a reader sees this essay writing 2,600 GW or the three PPA megawatt sets as evidence that expansion has already been jammed dead, they may judge the writer out of bounds. Power is one of three forks. It has not won now.
