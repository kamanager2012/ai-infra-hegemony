## Scope

> **Positioning boundary:** this report does not claim to be globally unique, the world's most comprehensive AI-infrastructure report, or the deepest source on every layer. Specialist research programs in power markets, semiconductor supply chains, compute datasets, sell-side finance, and policy can be materially stronger within their own domains. The report's contribution is the **cross-layer integration** of physical supply, system-stack competition, capital formation, downstream transmission, evidence identity, denominator discipline, and explicit falsification rules.

This report is a 32-chapter long-form industry study of AI infrastructure, semiconductors, memory, advanced packaging, hyperscaler system stacks, power, capital, Agent-Native Software, Physical AI, labor and talent systems, and structural industrial change.

**Analytical hierarchy:** the report is broad by design, but the chapters do not carry equal evidentiary weight. Physical-resource allocation, system substitution, capital formation, and clearing form the load-bearing industrial spine. Agent-native software and Physical AI are demand-side extensions; labor and talent chapters are second-order transmission modules. The full role map is stated in the Table of Contents.

The report separates:

- observed and verifiable facts (F — Fact);
- explicit forward assumptions (H — Hypothesis);
- causal transmission mechanisms (M — Mechanism);
- conditional future states (S — Scenario);
- observable reverse signals that would force judgments to change (X — Falsification).

### Language and editorial architecture

The **English edition is the sole canonical analytical text**. New claims, mechanism definitions, denominator choices, evidence boundaries, scenario language, section order, and falsification logic are resolved in English first because the primary evidence base—filings, earnings materials, product documentation, technical papers, contracts, and industry terminology—is predominantly English.

The Chinese edition is the **professional Chinese translation of the English canonical text**, not an independent analytical edition. Chinese syntax may be localized and standard industry terms such as HBM, CoWoS, CapEx, TCO, utilization, qualification, system stack, scale-up / scale-out, and workload may remain in English when that preserves precision. The translation must not independently add, remove, reorder, or redefine claims, mechanisms, denominators, evidence identities, scenarios, falsification boundaries, or section hierarchy. Any substantive revision is made in English first and then translated into Chinese.

Editorial rule:

> **Simplify navigation, not analysis. Do not explain what the target professional reader is expected to know; explain what is easy to mis-measure, mis-denominate, or infer incorrectly.**

The objective is not to produce a single deterministic 2036 forecast. It is to build an industrial-analysis framework whose judgments can be tested against later public evidence. Facts emerging after **August 28, 2026** belong to a later evidence cycle; they may be used to re-evaluate the framework, but they are not backdated into this edition's evidence set.


### Entry point for end-system and industrial-supply readers

This report is not arguing that “only AI matters.” Smartphones, PCs, game consoles, vehicles, industrial equipment, robotics, and long-lifecycle high-reliability electronics remain important.

The core question is:

> **When AI/HPC demand with higher willingness to pay, longer capacity commitments, and stronger capital formation changes the priority of shared or adjacent upstream resources, can non-AI end systems still obtain enough, stable, affordable supply on their own BOM, qualification, lifecycle, and product cadence?**

If you come from consumer electronics, game consoles, automotive, industrial systems, robotics, or high-reliability supply chains, a useful first pass is:

**Ch01 → Ch03 → Ch21 → Ch20 §10 → Ch32**

NVIDIA's system stack, hyperscalers, China's parallel stack, and S1–S4 can be read on a second pass as mechanism background.

## Evidence boundary

The report relies primarily on:

- annual reports, 10-K / 20-F filings, quarterly results, and official investor materials;
- regulatory, court, and government documents;
- formal contracts and project disclosures;
- official product specifications and technical documentation;
- high-quality institutional research;
- report-side models and stress tests when explicitly labeled.

Institutional forecasts, sell-side estimates, and report-side calculations are not promoted into company facts.
