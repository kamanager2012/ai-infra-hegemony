## 1. The question this volume answers

After the coach queues CoWoS, the first-to-market node, and HBM for cabinets first, who is squeezed in the first wave? Not whether factories on the cloud will be charged rent. It is whether personal computers, commercial PCs, general-purpose servers, and phones are fighting accelerators for the same kit. This volume answers three things. First, TSMC already wrote the 2025 increment on HPC; is booked first customer 19% enough as a safe distance. Second, 5.39 wafers per cabinet is a CoWoS estimate, not a division of the whole factory's 15,022 thousand wafers; why 835,000 cabinets is void. Third, whether consumer and general-purpose compute pay a residual tax, or sit on another river. The digital-transformation trap is a side branch. It is carried inside the mechanism. It does not occupy the question.

## 7. How the residual tax walks from packaging to the shelf

Packaging first, then memory, then the box. Analysis added here. Packaging is the shortest gate. C.C. Memory is the second gate. HBM eats more wafers per bit. The house writes half of 2026 DRAM bits as HBM+RDIMM. Makers turn to the high price. Consumer DIMMs and some server DIMMs are leftover. The box is the third gate. A maker that cannot buy same-generation material either raises price, or specs down, or stretches replacement. That is the shelf form of centralized inflation. It is not a PMI series. It is BOM and lead time. Volume 9 has no newly checked box units. Do not invent how many global phones fell. Antitrust cannot knock this slice out. ## 9. Why whole-factory division cannot tell the manufacturing shock

Divide 15,022 thousand wafers by 5.39 and you get a large cabinet count that sounds like "every factory in the world is feeding cabinets." That is the 835,000-cabinet error, amplified. Inside 15,022 thousand wafers sit phones, MCUs, auto, mature nodes, IoT. C.C. Wei also said in 2Q26: among mature nodes, only what is AI-related is tight, first power management, because data centers need large volumes of power chips. That sentence shows two things. First, the siphon spills onto some mature nodes, not only 3nm. Second, what spills is "the few parts related to the cabinet," not a whole 28nm fab converted to GPUs. The manufacturing shock is therefore selective: parts that fight cabinets for packaging, HBM, and power management move first on lead time and price; MCUs and display drivers unrelated to cabinets walk their own cycle. If Volume 9 writes "the whole factory's 15 million wafers" as the shock total, it writes a selective shock as a full stop. A full stop is not the judgment, and it is not source 02.

The annual report writes 2025: 305 processes deployed, 12,682 products built for 534 customers. The customer count is still there. One name did not eat the factory. What was eaten is increment slots and packaging slots. 534 minus 1 is still not 0. That is how "booked 19% is not a safe distance" and "the whole factory was not bought out by one name" can both hold: full-year blended share is still 19%; voice on the scarce slice is already larger than 19%. What a manufacturing buyer feels is the second sentence, not the first.
