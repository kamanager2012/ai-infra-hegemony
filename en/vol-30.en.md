## 1. The question this volume answers

Stack the physical walls and the depreciation wall already named in earlier volumes onto 2036. Who still stands at the bottleneck? This volume invents no new share numbers. It gives no 2036 revenue, share, or installed base. "Oligopoly concentration, edge decentralization, hard capital landing" in the table of contents can be scenario titles. They cannot be probabilities already issued.

The endgame is not a company list. The endgame is whether three walls are still there. If the memory wall is still there, HBM and advanced-packaging pricing power will not return to the 2022 DRAM cycle. If the interconnect wall is still there, a fabric that can offer in-cabinet unified memory still collects system-level rent, whether or not the brand is still called NVIDIA. If the power wall is still there, high-power cabinets and multi-year interconnection keep nuclear and long-cycle power operators on the "bottleneck controller" line. The judgment writes those three walls as an allocation already visible now. You need not wait for 2036. What this volume adds is the clock: depreciation on the 2024–2025 capex wave hits mainly in 2025–2028. It does not automatically decide who is still standing in 2036.

## 2. The draft depreciation template, and cells already struck

The original Volume 30 had no independent section 2, and no 2036 revenue, share, or installed base. It repeated four cross-volume sentences, all marked "draft numbers." This volume does not promote them to a verified 10-K, and does not write them as a 2036 center:

- Bottleneck controllers (TSMC, NVIDIA, SK hynix, nuclear operators) enjoy 60%–75% gross margin and pricing power.
- Clouds and model firms bear depreciation on 2024 $215B of capex and accelerate ASICs and algorithm cost-down.
- Downstream consumer electronics and manufacturing bear chip purchase costs up 20%–30%, and replacement cycles lengthen.
- After dense depreciation in 2025–2027, high-cost models that miss ROI are washed out; the industry enters a high-efficiency Agents mature period. Technical direction: CoWoS/CoPoS, CPO, MLA/MoE.

$215B is not an addable formula across four same-calendar-year, same-line-item, AI-split filings. Mixed year (Microsoft FY24 + the others CY2024), equipment purchases, Amazon on a net basis, Meta without leases = 44,477 + 77,658 + 52,535 + 37,256 = 211,926 (million dollars), about $211.9B. Meta on the company figure is $213.9B. 2025 mixed year about $354.0B–$356.5B. All four lines are group-wide capex. $45B–$55B annual depreciation is a template of 4-year hardware plus 15-year civil works, not a sum of four 10-K asset classes. Microsoft "broke $55B" does not match the cash-flow statement: Additions to property and equipment, FY24 $44,477M, FY25 $64,551M. NVIDIA FY25 data center exact is $115,186M, not "1,150+". BOM, software premium 40%–45%, CoWoS monthly 1.3万→13万 and a 60% lock, are not TSMC or NVIDIA splits. 85%→55% has no sample. DeepSeek-V3 $5.576M is the draft's own claim. This pass did not re-check the paper. This volume keeps these cells as comparison only. It does not write them as quantities that still hold in 2036.

FY26, TSMC 2025, Hynix FY25, NVL72 single-cabinet spec, supply commitments 952→1,190, and the 10% statutory ceiling are already in the judgment and [Volume 1](vol-01.md). This volume does not copy them again.

## 3. Mechanism: the depreciation wall decides 2025–2028. It does not automatically decide 2036

$215B on a 4-year hardware depreciation hits mainly in 2025–2028. That is the draft template. After 2030 those assets are fully, or nearly fully, depreciated. 2036 winners and losers depend on whether a second and third wave of like-sized capex arrive, and on whether Agents actually fill machine-hours. The 2025 mixed year is already about $354.0B–$356.5B. Pushing 2026–2028 from 2024's 2150 understates the base. Concrete 2026–2028 expense: unverified. Do not treat as audited fact.

If the Agents mature period holds and inference cheapens through MLA-class methods, capex may shift from "bid for training fabric" to "buy power and packaging." If Agents miss enterprise ROI, the second capex wave does not come, and bottleneck-party gross margin of 71.1%–75.0% falls first. This volume does not forecast which path happens. What is testable: whether four-cloud group capex falls two years running; whether NVIDIA data-center revenue leaves the FY26 $193,737M magnitude; whether gross margin leaves the 71.1% to 75.0% band. The last two are comparison only, not a forecast.

The depreciation wall and half of the scarce slice are not the same thing. Half of the scarce slice asks: in the few years before the next first-to-market node is locked by commitments, has half of CoWoS / leading-edge logic first silicon / HBM already been slotted for the first name. The depreciation wall asks: in which years the PPE buyers already put down becomes expense, and whether it forces a second wave. The first question is answered in [Volume 15](vol-15.md). The second: the first wave hits 2025–2028. 2036 needs another wave, or no wave.

## 4. Winner portraits can only be written as functions, not market caps

Function seats already named in the judgment and the draft. No 2036 market cap or share:

- Manufacture and packaging: who can still expand CoWoS-class advanced packaging and decide who gets the increment. TSMC itself only admits a rising packaging-and-test share and tight CoWoS. Sell-side research puts NVIDIA at about 60% of 2026 CoWoS. That is not a TSMC split. The direction is the same line. 1.3万→13万 wafers is not a TSMC disclosure.
- Memory: who still collects HBM's wafer-penalty rent. Micron 10-K: the same bits of HBM take more wafers. Hot Chips paraphrase: about 3x DDR5. Hynix HBM revenue more than doubled in a year.
- System: who can still write cabinet standard, NVLink fabric, and a compiler stack as non-substitutable interfaces. Compute & Networking in FY25 was $116,193M, a neighboring segment, not an audited software-tax value. FY26 data center splits into Compute $162,361M and Networking $31,376M.
- Power: who holds contracted zero-carbon baseload, not a queue number. Contract detail on 835 MW / 960 MW / 500 MW: [Volume 35](vol-35.md). This volume did not re-check it.
- Algorithm: who can talk training bills at a millions-of-dollars magnitude rather than a whole cabinet. $5.576M is the draft's own claim. This pass did not re-check it.
- Edge and embodiment: who can ship at 50W–100W and more than 1000 TOPS. No whole-machine dollars, no shipments. Do not write that a revolution has broken out.

Loser portraits can only be written as mechanism too. Seat-based SaaS that cannot rewrite an 85% gross-margin story into a measurable workflow will be punched through by usage cost. 85%→55% has no sample. Do not treat it as a statistic. Consumer-electronics whole machines that keep eating a memory spillover tax and a lengthened replacement cycle are not AI demand. They are the supply side being siphoned. Channel-verbal 16G DDR5 from 450 to 1800 is not a 10-K. It only shows residual bits are rising in price. Snapdragon 8 Elite $180–$190 and replacement cycles above 40 months are draft numbers. This volume did not re-check ASP.

Microsoft especially cannot be written as a loser. Intelligent Cloud FY25 $106,265M; Azure above $75B, up 34% year on year. Xbox can thin. Azure will not. Sony thinning and Microsoft thinning are not the same sentence. An endgame "Sony becomes a software games brand" can be marked as a scenario. Delete that sentence for Microsoft.

The sales unit has already moved up from the chip to the whole cabinet. Official cabinet: 72 GPUs, 36 Grace, 13.4 TB HBM, 17 TB LPDDR. If the endgame holds it is cabinets, not cards. Q4 FY25 Blackwell $11.0B in the quarter shows the increment is already counted by generation, not by card. Cloud ASICs hit the die. They do not hit the whole-cabinet fabric, and they do not hit the cash used to prepay at 71.1%–75.0%. Prepayments are reproduction. PPE is halls. The two sit on different accounting lines. 5.39 is a CoWoS-wafers-per-cabinet estimate, not a TSMC disclosure. The binding line is packaging. You do not need 800k cabinets to lock 3nm. Tonnages for 10k, 30k, and 50k cabinets are the judgment's official-spec times a hypothetical count, not disclosed sales. This volume does not write hypothetical cabinet counts as 2036 installed base.
