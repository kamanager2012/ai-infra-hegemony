# Core Hypothesis Matrix (2026–2036)

> This matrix defines forward-looking input variables only. None of the levels below should be treated as already-observed facts.  
> Purpose: force all future-facing chapters to use one common set of assumptions rather than mixing incompatible growth, supply, and end-state assumptions.

## 1. How to use the matrix

Each scenario is a combination of H variables.

```text
Fact F
+ Hypothesis H
+ Mechanism M
→ Scenario S
→ Falsification / Update X
```

Every 2030 or 2036 conclusion should be traceable to a combination of variables below.

## 3. Four standard scenarios

### S1 — Competitive Expansion

Typical combination:
- H1: high growth, then gradual moderation
- H2: supply eventually exceeds demand growth
- H3: 40%–50%
- H5: advanced packaging loosens
- H6: power improves
- H7: cloud system stacks create real diversion
- H8: migration cost falls
- H12: a scalable second advanced-manufacturing source emerges

Result:
- AI remains enormous, but concentration weakens;
- Non-AI Residual Capacity recovers;
- NVIDIA remains strong, but excess platform value capture declines;
- the industry returns toward multipolar competition.

### S2 — Baseline Concentration

Typical combination:
- H1: sustained high growth
- H2: supply broadly catches up but often lags one cycle
- H3: 50%–60%
- H4: high HBM resource intensity persists
- H5: packaging remains periodically tight
- H6: power becomes a major constraint gradually
- H7: cloud system stacks divert internal inference and part of training
- H8: CUDA migration cost remains high

Result:
- leading platforms expand value capture;
- non-AI industries experience counterfactual crowding-out;
- bottlenecks roll across logic, HBM, packaging, and power;
- traditional industries do not disappear, but upgrade costs rise.

### S3 — Resource Hegemony

Typical combination:
- H1: high growth persists for multiple years
- H2: effective supply remains below demand
- H3: 60%–80%
- H4: HBM trade ratio stays high
- H5: advanced packaging remains quota-like
- H6: power becomes a hard ceiling
- H7: cloud stacks fail to dislodge the core training stack
- H8: software migration cost stays high
- H14: excess AI returns persist
- H15: prepayments and long contracts strengthen
- H17: decade-scale model-company commitments expand

Result:
- resource-allocation power concentrates;
- competitors fall behind first on product cycles rather than on the ability to design a chip at all;
- Non-AI Residual Capacity remains structurally low;
- capital formation tilts further toward AI;
- the Installed-base Flywheel reinforces itself.

### S4 — Capital Hard Landing

Typical combination:
- H1: demand materially misses expectations
- H9: token prices fall rapidly
- H10: usage growth fails to offset price declines
- H14: AI returns fall
- H15: supply commitments slow or are rescheduled
- H17: long-duration model-company commitments shrink, are rescheduled, or run at low utilization
- H6: previously signed long-duration power and construction commitments remain

Result:
- GPU/rack pricing and lead times soften first;
- HBM/packaging expansion slows later;
- installed hardware continues to depreciate;
- long-duration power and buildings become the hardest assets to exit;
- if clearing removes only the second tier, leading platforms may emerge more concentrated afterward.

## 4. How “80%” may be used

Allowed:

> In the H3 extreme stress test, if a leading platform captures 70%–80% of a category of incremental advanced HPC resources for two or three generations, competitors may enter capacity-foreclosure pressure.

Not allowed:

> NVIDIA already owns 80% of TSMC N2 capacity.

Also not allowed:

> NVIDIA will inevitably own 80% of all advanced-chip capacity in 2030.

## 5. Condition for writing “non-AI supply has recovered”

Only if:

```text
ΔEffective_Supply
>
ΔAI_Absorption + ΔOther_Priority_Demand
```

and the condition persists long enough for the absolute level or demand-relative shortfall of NonAI_Residual to improve materially.

Otherwise the report may say:
- total supply increased;
- the shortage narrowed;
- lead times improved;
- pricing pressure eased.

It may not automatically say “recovered.”

## 7. Falsification and updating

The matrix is not designed so that every possible future can be “explained.” Every variable must be updateable by reality.

If we repeatedly observe:
- AI demand slowing;
- CoWoS/HBM supply loosening;
- a mature second advanced-manufacturing source;
- cloud system stacks migrating large training/inference workloads;
- materially lower CUDA migration cost;
- power expansion outpacing rack deployment;

then S3 should receive less weight.

If instead:
- supply commitments keep expanding;
- advanced packaging continues to limit customer growth;
- HBM keeps consuming higher wafer resources;
- AI customers continue receiving the incremental first-to-market capacity;
- grid connection remains slower than data-center construction;

then S3 should receive more weight.

The value of this matrix is not a fixed probability. It is a ten-year industrial state machine that can be continuously updated and falsified.
