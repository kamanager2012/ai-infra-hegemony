# Core Hypothesis Matrix (2026–2036)

> This matrix defines forward-looking input variables only. None of the levels below should be treated as already-observed facts.  
> Purpose: force the report's future-facing judgments to use one common set of assumptions rather than mixing incompatible growth, supply, financing, and end-state assumptions.

## 1. How to use the matrix

Each scenario is a combination of H variables.

```text
Fact F
+ Hypothesis H
+ Mechanism M
→ Scenario S
→ Falsification / Update X
```

Every 2030 or 2036 conclusion should be traceable to a combination of variables below.

## 3. Four standard scenarios

### S1 — Competitive Expansion

Typical combination:

- H1: high growth, then gradual moderation;
- H2: supply eventually exceeds demand growth;
- H3: multiple buyers obtain qualified supply reliably;
- H5: advanced packaging loosens;
- H6: power improves;
- H7: cloud system stacks create real diversion;
- H8: migration cost falls;
- H12: a scalable second advanced-manufacturing source emerges;
- H13: production-grade parallel stacks broaden;
- H19: critical upstream continuity improves.

Result:

- AI remains enormous, but concentration weakens;
- Non-AI Residual Capacity recovers;
- leading platforms remain strong, but excess platform value capture declines;
- the industry moves toward multipolar competition.

### S2 — Baseline Concentration

Typical combination:

- H1: sustained high growth;
- H2: supply broadly catches up but often lags one cycle;
- H3: leading buyers have clear priority, while substitute buyers still obtain effective supply;
- H4: high HBM resource intensity persists;
- H5: packaging remains periodically tight;
- H6: power becomes a major constraint gradually;
- H7: cloud system stacks divert internal inference and part of training;
- H8: migration cost remains high;
- H17: Anchor Buyer commitments remain substantial;
- H18: seller support exists but remains consistent with healthy utilization and monetization.

Result:

- leading platforms retain strong value capture;
- non-AI industries can experience counterfactual crowding-out;
- bottlenecks roll across logic, HBM, packaging, and power;
- traditional industries do not disappear, but upgrade costs and quantity-assurance risk rise.

### S3 — Resource Hegemony

Typical combination:

- H1: high growth persists for multiple years;
- H2: effective supply remains below demand;
- H3: access across several scarce layers concentrates toward a small set of buyers;
- H4: HBM trade ratio stays high;
- H5: advanced packaging remains quota-like;
- H6: power becomes a hard ceiling;
- H7: cloud stacks fail to dislodge the core training / high-value workload stack;
- H8: software migration cost stays high;
- H14: excess AI returns persist;
- H15: prepayments and long contracts strengthen;
- H17: decade-scale model-company commitments expand;
- H18: financing coordination expands but is still supported by real utilization;
- H19: autonomous closure remains incomplete or only partial in important competing stacks.

Result:

- resource-allocation power concentrates;
- competitors fall behind first on product cycles and delivery certainty rather than on the ability to design a chip at all;
- Non-AI Residual Capacity remains structurally tight;
- capital formation tilts further toward AI;
- the Installed-base Flywheel reinforces itself.

### S4 — Capital Hard Landing

Typical combination:

- H1: demand materially misses expectations;
- H9: unit prices fall rapidly;
- H10: usage growth fails to offset price declines;
- H14: AI returns fall;
- H15: supply commitments slow or are rescheduled;
- H17: long-duration model-company commitments shrink, are rescheduled, or run at low utilization;
- H18: supplier equity / credit support rises while independent monetization and utilization weaken;
- H6: previously signed long-duration power and construction commitments remain.

Result:

- GPU/rack pricing and lead times soften first;
- HBM/packaging expansion slows later;
- installed hardware continues to depreciate;
- long-duration power and buildings become the hardest assets to exit;
- if clearing removes mainly the second tier, leading platforms can emerge more concentrated afterward.

## 4. How H3 should be used

H3 does **not** use one composite percentage for advanced logic, HBM, advanced packaging, networking, and power.

Correct use is layer by layer:

- how many qualified suppliers exist;
- whether competitors obtain effective quantities on schedule;
- whether lead time / allocation fulfillment diverges materially;
- whether long-duration commitments pre-reserve future supply;
- whether supply elasticity improves;
- whether customer concentration actually enters physical allocation.

The following substitutions are prohibited:

> revenue share → wafer share → packaging allocation → HBM share → power access

If reliable customer-level allocation data becomes available for one physical layer, a percentage may be used for that layer only. It cannot be generalized into a synthetic full-stack "resource capture rate."

## 5. Condition for writing "non-AI supply has recovered"

Only if:

```text
ΔEffective_Supply
>
ΔAI_Absorption + ΔOther_Priority_Demand
```

and the condition persists long enough for the absolute level or demand-relative shortfall of NonAI_Residual to improve materially.

Otherwise the report may say:

- total supply increased;
- the shortage narrowed;
- lead times improved;
- pricing pressure eased.

It may not automatically say "recovered."

## 7. Falsification and updating

The matrix is not designed so that every possible future can be "explained." Every variable must be updateable by reality.

If we repeatedly observe:

- AI demand slowing;
- HBM / advanced-packaging supply loosening;
- a mature second advanced-manufacturing source;
- China production-grade substitution and autonomous closure improving across several layers;
- cloud system stacks migrating large production workloads;
- materially lower software migration cost;
- power expansion outpacing deployment demand;

then S3 should receive less weight.

If instead:

- supply commitments keep expanding;
- advanced packaging continues to limit customer growth;
- HBM keeps consuming higher wafer resources;
- a small set of buyers retains materially stronger quantity assurance and earlier delivery across several scarce layers;
- grid connection remains slower than data-center buildout;

then S3 should receive more weight.

If utilization, market clearing, accounting losses, forward commitments, and demand-financing quality deteriorate broadly and persistently, S4 should receive more weight.

The value of this matrix is not a fixed probability. It is a falsifiable industrial state framework for the next decade.
