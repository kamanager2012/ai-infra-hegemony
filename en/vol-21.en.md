## 1. The question this volume answers

From which side does money enter this chain, in whose margin does it stop, and in what form does it flow back as depreciation, prepayments, and wafer bills? The title promised a full-chain cash-flow and profit-pool estimate. The draft actually gave one cross-company comparison table and one four-year depreciation causal sentence. This volume does not invent a falsely precise input-output table. What it does: read that table as a flow map, then replace the nodes on the map with official numbers that can be checked now, using the Judgment and the memory-residual evidence. How cloud capex becomes NVIDIA data-center revenue, TSMC HPC wafers, and HBM and server sticks on Hynix and Micron books — and which links look like a profit pool but are only cash in transit.

The main line is not denied. A margin monopoly is not a share monopoly. Sellers are already rich. No buyer, selling cabinets, can outbid 71% to 75%. The sales unit moved from the chip to the cabinet. Server DDR5 is already residual, not another river. Shifting onto commodity memory is a fight with phones and PCs for the slice already rising. It is a tax, not a new river. The China market cannot save global cabinets, HBM, advanced packaging, or the software stack. Numbers only reuse public notes already nailed in the Judgment, the profit-and-cash evidence, and the memory-residual evidence. What cannot be found stays empty. Won are not privately converted to dollars. NVIDIA revenue is not knocked against four-cloud capex into a share.

The honest unit of a data-version model is layered retention, not an added-up market size. Adding NVIDIA, TSMC, Hynix, and four-cloud revenue together counts the same cabinet three times: the cloud books capex, the athlete books system revenue, and foundry and memory each book once more. Whoever leaves margin on a layer owns that layer's pricing power. Whoever is only in transit should not be written into the profit-pool numerator. The layers this volume can nail stop at the gross-margin and operating-margin bands the official notes have already printed.

## 3. Mechanism (flows, retention, refill, residual)

**First hop: cash starts from the cloud balance sheet, not from model firms.** Model firms do not even have a row in the profit-pool main table. Their compute bills are wrapped inside cloud capex and the athlete's data-center revenue. The first hop is: cloud capex to the whole cabinet, then to foundry and packaging, memory, liquid cooling, power, copper, and contract assembly. Four-cloud scope is [四云Capex口径核对](四云Capex口径核对.md). The draft $215B can no longer be the profit-pool denominator. The line is group-wide, not a GPU split.

**Knocking is forbidden: athlete revenue is not a half-share of four-cloud capex.** FY26 data center is [Volume 1](vol-01.md). Given that four-cloud capex is not all accelerator cards, the relative position only shows that systems ate the fattest slice of capex. It cannot be written as a share. The lines differ, the years differ, buyer and seller each book their own. If a profit-pool model knocks here, every later percentage is pulled off.

**Retention: the gross-margin stack is the profit-pool stack. The ruler is margin, not share.** Intel 1999 and the FY26 table are [Volume 1](vol-01.md). This volume does not reopen them. Once the sales unit moves from the chip to the cabinet, the collection unit of the profit pool also moves from a single die to one cabinet. Whoever can prepay, and whoever can swallow memory and packaging price-ups into the whole cabinet, can add a tranche.

**Refill: asset-light outsources line refill.** The profit pool has two faces. The income statement is thick. The cash-flow statement must keep refilling lines. NVIDIA itself is asset-light. Most refill work is not in its own plants. TSMC must turn margin back into the next leading-edge process and packaging. Memory makers must turn operating profit back into HBM stack lines. The second hop of cash is: the athlete's prepayments and the cloud's purchase orders become foundry and memory capex, then become the next, more expensive cabinet. Depreciation bites on the cloud side. The depreciation wall is [Volume 30](vol-30.md).

**Second profit pool: half the bits already go to cabinets.** 51% and 47%/68% are [Volume 5](vol-05.md). If clouds shift inference onto DDR5, they grab residual already inside that half. The China stack is [Volume 12](vol-12.md). This volume does not invent an input-output table.

## 4. Analysis added (scenarios, the other side, testable marks)

Four profit-pool scenarios (analysis added; no new probabilities; no invented new percentages):

First: the pool keeps concentrating upstream. Cloud capex does not fall. NVIDIA data-center revenue walks another step from $115.186B to $193.737B. TSMC HPC and Hynix HBM shares keep rising. If the move of supply commitments from $95.2B to $119.0B still repeats, prepayments lock the next packaging and stack lines first. Whether the software premium is still the draft's about 40% has no official split table. It cannot be treated as already held. What can be an anchor: gross margin still in the 71% to 75% band; maker operating margins still in the Hynix 49% and Samsung Memory high-value-add-priority band. The intelligence-rent story holds on this band.

Second: the pool is thinned by algorithms. A jump like compressing KV cache lets the same service buy less HBM and fewer whole cabinets. The HBM slice of bit supply loosens first. Hynix 49% gives back. Cloud depreciation pressure eases. Athlete shipment growth falls. Even after a break, the residual tax does not vanish at once: makers have already turned lines toward high value-add, and cutting back takes time. Micron's 10-K itself writes the reverse sentence: only when demand weakens do wafers return to conventional DRAM. Until the return is done, phones and PCs still pay the tax.

Third: the pool breaks at the cloud layer. If enterprise incremental revenue stops at the draft's $35B to $50B row, while capex still charges ahead at TrendForce's another +98% and another +50%, the flow will first be cut in cloud boardrooms, and the upstream profit pool will shrink with it. $35B to $50B and $670B to $750B are both extrapolations, unverified; do not treat as audited fact. Observable substitutes: whether four-cloud mixed-year capex turns from the $354.0B order of magnitude, and whether TrendForce's memory-as-share-of-cloud-capex 47% to 68% is held or revised in later notes.

Fourth: the residual tax holds on its own. Even if cabinet growth slows, as long as makers do not cut HBM lines back, conventional bits remain a scarce residual. Huaqiangbei talk and TrendForce contract-price outlooks can both be true: the tip smashes first, contract prices follow later. In this scenario the profit pool does not return to the 2023 Hynix operating-loss page. It only moves from cabinet fat to stick-and-drive fat. The watch item is whether Hynix still writes in the press release both HBM doubling and a sharp rise in conventional server memory.

Three other-side sentences must enter the model, or the profit pool will be frightened by whole-cabinet unit prices. First: drawing four-cloud gross margins and NVIDIA gross margin in the same profit pool misleads. The denominators differ. Amazon includes retail. Meta includes ads. NVIDIA is almost data-center systems. The correct comparison is AI-related segments. The only segment this pass can align is NVIDIA data center $193.737B. Microsoft Intelligent Cloud, Google Cloud, and Meta AI have no peer segment margins newly opened in this volume. Leave them empty. Second: the whole-cabinet BOM is an engineering-estimate range. $1.10M to $1.40M of software premium cannot be added straight into company-level 75%. Third: ASICs shunting internal load is real, but public next generations have not retreated to commodity memory. Firms and states that bet they can leave the whole-cabinet system next year and shift onto DDR5 have misread the river.

Testable marks refill only the columns that can be checked against a table each year. NVIDIA: whether revenue is still walking up from the $215.938B band, whether data center is still walking up from the $193.737B band, whether gross margin is still 71% to 75%, whether free cash is still on the order of $96.575B, whether supply commitments still add from $119.0B. Four clouds: whether mixed-year equipment purchases stay near $354.0B to $356.5B or turn. TSMC: whether HPC is still 58%, whether the first customer is still 19%. Hynix: whether operating margin is still 49%, whether HBM revenue is still written in the press release as more than doubled year on year. Micron: whether the cloud-memory slice is still far larger than the phone-and-PC slice. TrendForce: whether 51%, 47%, 68% are held or revised in later notes. CXMT: whether a prospectus or later statutory disclosure shows a standalone HBM row. Until a reliable segment disclosure of enterprise AI software revenue appears, the $35B to $50B row cannot enter the profit-pool main table.

Cash flow still lacks several edges that should exist and that official notes do not give: the dollar amount of HBM logic base dies flowing back to TSMC, the packaging split inside TSMC revenue, liquid-cooling and copper-cable vendor revenue, and annual power bills on nuclear offtakes. Without those edges, so-called full-chain true upstream and downstream only covers the four or five brightest nodes. The honest use of the model is to watch direction year by year: whether the spend side is still expanding, whether the collection-side margin stack is still the 75%, 59.9%, 49% band, whether bit supply is still half to cabinets. If the direction changes, rewrite the profit pool. If it has not, do not invent a falsely precise percentage for more precision.

The five interrupts are the Judgment and [Volume 8](vol-08.md). The case file is [Volume 11](vol-11.md). The use of a profit-pool model inside the window is not to forecast an end price. It is to check each year: whether money still stops at the whole-cabinet band that can swallow a price-up, and whether the residual is still taxing phones and PCs.

If a data-version model must be given a skeleton that can be hand-calculated, it can only stop at already-allowed sums. It cannot invent coefficients. Known spend side: four-cloud mixed year $211.9B to $213.9B, and the next mixed year $354.0B to $356.5B. Known collection side, brightest: NVIDIA data center $193.737B, TSMC 2025 gross margin 59.9%, Hynix 49% operating margin. These three cannot be added. Currencies differ, scopes differ, and they contain a large non-AI mass. Hand calculation only asks direction, not a falsely precise global AI market size.
