## 1. The question this volume answers

Has advanced packaging already replaced the leading-edge node as the real quota on AI hardware? This volume answers only six things. First, what TSMC itself admits: C.C. Wei in 4Q24 said CoWoS tight, cannot meet demand; in 2Q26 he said packaging capacity is so tight that it limits customers' growth. The financials only split "advanced packaging and testing," about 8% in 2024, a bit over 10% in 2025. The CFO refused on the spot to break CoWoS out. Second, where the increment is: 2025 HPC 58% of revenue, up 48% year on year; first customer 19%, second 17%. Booked 19% is not a safe distance. Third, whether cloud custom silicon opened another packaging river: Microsoft's official Maia 100 writes TSMC N5 and CoWoS-S; Maia 200 writes TSMC N3 and HBM3e. Google, Amazon, and Meta public next generations are HBM, not commodity DDR5. Fourth, where the binding line is: 5.39 is a CoWoS-wafers-per-cabinet estimate, not a full-factory shipment division. About 100k cabinets take more than half of this packaging line. You do not need 800,000 cabinets to lock 3nm. Fifth, whether Eastern fabs can take Western GDS: TrendForce 2026 Q1 pure-play foundry, TSMC 72%, Samsung 6.5%, SMIC 5.1%. Samsung's $16.54B long (announced KRW 22.76T, through 2033) is Tesla, not the four clouds. Sixth, where the profit pool sits: official text writes packaging-and-test gross margin "better than before, still below the corporate average." NVIDIA FY26 gross margin 71.1%, Q4 back to 75.0%. The quota can sit on the interposer. The profit still sits with the system definer.

The question changes from "are 130k wafers and 60% exclusive true" to "is the tightness official text already admits enough to carry past half of the scarce slice recognizing only the first name." The deepening note already wrote: tightness is enough. You need not lean on the 13k-to-130k ramp table. If China phone makers bundle again, they bind residual memory. They do not change the CoWoS queue.

## 3. Push the mechanism (official text only admits tight; cloud custom silicon is still on the same slice)

### 3.1 Official text can nail only three sentences

On the 4Q24 call, an analyst asked directly for CoWoS revenue share and margin. CFO Wendell Huang's official sentence: we don't break down in different segments of the advanced packaging; advanced packaging and testing over 8% of 2024 revenue, and it will account for over 10% this year; gross margin better than before, still below the corporate average. The source is the investor-relations transcript the audit opened. C.C. Wei on the same call: today is all AI-focused, and we have very tight capacity and cannot even meet customers' need. On the 2Q26 call, C.C. Wei wrote tightness as a hard constraint on customer growth: our packaging capacity is so tight that now it limits my customers' growth. The main line nails those two sentences. "Sold out through 2026" at the shareholders' meeting has no official transcript. This volume does not write "sold out" as TSMC's own sentence.

In the 2025 capex guide, about 10% to 20% goes to advanced packaging, testing, mask making, and others. That is a capex basket, not CoWoS monthly capacity, and still less the 13k-to-130k table. The ceiling official text will admit stops here: tight, limits growth, packaging and test past 10%, no segment split, margin still below the corporate average. Using an undisclosed share to explain official "tight" is circular. This volume does not take that road.

### 3.2 The increment is HPC and packaging; booked 19% is not a safe distance

TSMC's books: [Volume 4](vol-04.md). Profit and cash: [Volume 1](vol-01.md). What is being opened is HPC and packaging. Apple locks phone logic. Booked 19% is not a safe distance. Occupy the increment and next year's slots belong to the first name. Packaging is one slice of the coach's mix. It is not another fab that can be handed to the second name at will.

### 3.3 Physics: a chip past the reticle must first become a packageable puzzle

Analysis added. A single die is stuck at the reticle. The main line writes B200 as two dice of about 800 mm² each, near the reticle limit (Electronic Design / IEEE Spectrum paraphrase); H100 814 mm² (NVIDIA blog). The draft writes Blackwell as dual dice of about 1600 mm², then rings HBM around them. Without a silicon interposer or RDL large enough, these objects are only a pile of dice. CoWoS-L claiming 3.3 times the reticle (draft number) is trading package area for logic area. Expansion is therefore not "buy a few more scanners": you need interposer wafers, bonders, test stations, and dedicated sites. The draft wrote the 2023 52-week lead time as packaging, not "TSMC cannot do 4N." The mechanism direction is right. The week count itself is draft, not an earnings call.

Official one-cabinet bill: 72 GPUs, 36 Grace, 13.4 TB HBM, 17 TB LPDDR. 10k cabinets: 134 PB HBM, 170 PB LPDDR. 30k cabinets: 402 PB HBM, 510 PB LPDDR. 50k cabinets: 670 PB HBM, 850 PB LPDDR. That is tonnage multiplied from the product page, not shipments. Without CoWoS, HBM stacks cannot be bonded; fuel cannot enter the engine. Without HBM, the interposer is empty silicon. When both are tight, the prepayment queue decides who first gets a working set.

### 3.4 5.39 maps to the packaging line, not 30% of the factory

5.39 is a CoWoS-wafers-per-cabinet estimate: 72 packages divided by about 13.4 packages per wafer. Front-end logic is another line: 144 dice, 70% yield, about 2.9 wafers/cabinet. 835,000 cabinets equals 4.5 million wafers divided by 5.39. The pool is 30% of factory shipments. The pool is wrong. The binding line is CoWoS. Sell-side puts NVIDIA at about 60%, 595k to 630k wafers. TSMC has no split. At 5.39, about 100k cabinets take more than half of this packaging line. You do not need 800,000 cabinets to lock 3nm.

The estimate note writes the identity hard: cabinets times wafers per cabinet equals demand on this slice; supply minus demand equals residual left for others. 5.39 does not map to front-end 2.9, because it maps to packaging, not 4nm-class front-end. A 300 mm wafer is about 70,686 mm². Dual dice of about 1600 mm² plus many HBM stacks make a large interposer. Theory is cut by edge loss, the interposer, and HBM footprint down to about 13 to 15 packages a wafer. 5.39 is that coefficient. There is no official TSMC sentence "one CoWoS wafer yields how many B200s." 5.39 is an estimate, not a fiction. The identity is real. Official Rubin one-cabinet HBM4 is 20.7 TB (the estimate note, from the product page). The interposer is larger. CoWoS wafers per cabinet will only be larger than 5.39, not smaller.

Reading the tenfold 13k-to-130k ramp as "shortage is already over" is the most common misread. Analysis added. Even if year-end truly reaches the draft's 130k wafers, one Blackwell-class package consumes more interposer area than a generation of H100. The same monthly capacity does not mean ten times the shippable training cards. If 60% exclusive is still only sell-side tracking, most of the increment can still flow first to the same buyer who already paid. The ramp solves whether a packaging hall exists. It does not solve whether the market is open. For firms, lead times may shorten. Queue eligibility itself is not cancelled. For rivals, you look for Broadcom or custom silicon in the residual, rather than waiting for TSMC to announce tenfold capacity and then automatically receiving a slot.

### 3.7 Packaging quota times interconnect and the compiler stack becomes market structure

Analysis added. Getting CoWoS only lets you bond HBM and logic. To treat 72 bonded GPUs as one unified memory, you still need NVLink and sub-100-nanosecond access (draft numbers: 130 TB/s per cabinet, 13.8 TB memory, below 100 ns). Ethernet, however fast, is another latency and bandwidth. So "packaging tight + proprietary interconnect + CUDA" is three doors. An ASIC that opens only the first may still lose at cabinet level to the whole cabinet. Foxconn and Quanta system-integration capacity is written in the draft as "very few can deliver at million-cabinet scale." That is another system door after packaging. The delivery claim itself is draft, not a 10-K.

Copper cable or co-packaged optics saves power inside the thermal wall. It does not replace CoWoS. Treating optics modules or CPO as a way around the interposer misreads this volume's bottleneck layer. If CoPoS panel-level is still only pre-research or a pilot, it cannot be written as relief inside the depreciation window. On the 2Q26 call, C.C. Wei said the majority is still CoWoS; the alternative is a pilot line, about another year from maturity. That is a direction word, not a second river already in 2026 volume.

Geographically, advanced-packaging capacity is highly concentrated on Taiwan sites. That is the same geographic risk as 2nm fabs and EUV, not a line two years of subsidy can copy. Arizona, Kumamoto, and Dresden are front-end and geographic scatter, not an already-disclosed second CoWoS main plant. This volume invents no overseas packaging monthly capacity.

### 3.8 Profit sits with the system definer, not in the packaging hall

Analysis added. TSMC 2025 gross margin 59.9%; official text writes packaging and test below the corporate average. The margin monopoly: [Volume 1](vol-01.md). Four-cloud scope: [Volume 8](vol-08.md). The clouds are not poor. That a buyer can pay does not prove a buyer, selling cabinets, can outbid 71% to 75%.

While the margin holds, commitments keep rising. Anyone else who wants the same material pays more, or does not get it. Expanding the packaging hall dilutes TSMC's own blended margin, not NVIDIA's whole-cabinet premium. That is why this volume must write "the quota sits on the interposer" and "profit sits with the system definer" as two sentences, and must not merge them into "packaging has already become the main profit pool." Whether TSMC itself will change corporate character because of packaging, public financials cannot answer. The blended book is still a manufacturer's balance sheet. If a future call starts disclosing CoWoS utilization and revenue on their own, this volume's "quota on the interposer" can be upgraded from mechanism to an auditable segment. Until then, 60% and 130k wafers can only stay at supply-chain paraphrase.

### 3.9 Acting alone hands over new lines; China TAM is not a rebuttal

Acting alone means handing over each year's new lines and packaging. Changing the allocation requires three checks: prepayments large enough that it cannot recognize only one buyer; software bound into one stack, with an open accelerator interface as the embryo; memory longs that leave a line for commodity bits. Miss one check and you are still picked off one by one. Apple does not fight AMD for cabinets. What changes cabinets is the accelerator-and-cloud-ASIC bundle. Phone makers bind residual memory. If China phone makers bundle, they at most change residual allocation. They do not change the increment lock behind 19%.

The five interrupts: the main line and [Volume 8](vol-08.md). The case file: [Volume 11](vol-11.md). If those five are not done, the line walks past half of the scarce slice. The window is the few years before the next first-to-market node is locked by commitments.

However large the China market is, it does not become any of those five. It is not Western bundling, not cloud ASICs taking training, not the grid, and not a second foundry seat Washington trusts. So "the China market is large; consumer electronics will refill itself" denies the main line. This volume does not do that. However large domestic shipments are, they do not become "Washington will hand over GDS." If domestic DRAM adds volume, CXMT follows the international floor. It will not give the HBM line back for the world's phones. A parallel stack does not save Apple's or AMD's seat at TSMC. What the global market has to break is the AI game: cabinets, the stack, packaging, HBM. Not selling a few more phones.

### 3.10 Prepayments lock queue rights, not a disclosed packaging share

Analysis added. Supply commitments rose from $95.2B to $119.0B. Official text writes prepaid manufacturing and capacity agreements. The $23.8B added in those three months is mostly due through fiscal 2027. Official text does not split it to TSMC, and does not split it to CoWoS. Some agreements can be rescheduled. So what prepayments prove is: the seller is locking inventory and capacity, and the lock is speeding up. Prepayments cannot be inverted into "TSMC has already written 60% of CoWoS into a contract schedule." Writing "hundreds of billions of dollars of cash prepayments lock 60%" as already-checked fact welds two facts of different hardness: one is the supply-commitment balance in NVIDIA's own annual report; the other is sell-side tracking of packaging wafers. The first is A-grade. The second is C-grade. This volume writes them apart.

The queue-rights mechanism does not need 60% to hold. TSMC 2025 HPC is already 58% of revenue, up 48% year on year. The first customer rose from 12% to 19%. What is being opened is HPC and packaging. After people, tools, and lines are queued to the first name, the second name who adds price still buys residual. C.C. Wei in 2Q26 said packaging is so tight it limits customer growth. Among those limited are, obviously, the string that can pay and still cannot get a packaging slot. The clouds are not poor. Microsoft FY25 net income already passed a hundred billion dollars. Money does not solve the physics of "not enough interposer to cut this quarter." What prepayments can do is take next quarter's and next year's slots first. Occupy the increment and next year's slots belong to the first name. That is the main line's section-3 sentence. This volume lands it on packaging.

Official limited supply on gaming cards is the same queue projected onto the consumer side. The coach feeds the 75.0% gross-margin data center first, then gaming. The audit already wrote: limited supply on gaming cards does not need 60% exclusive to hold. This volume agrees. Consumer cards and training cabinets fight for the same front-end and a slice of packaging, not two fabs. This volume invents no "already cut how many tens of thousands of gaming cards." Official limited supply is enough.

### 3.11 Residual memory is rising in price; packaging slots have not become residual

The residual tax: [Volume 5](vol-05.md) and [Volume 22](vol-22.md). The memory side is already pulling wafers. Server DDR5 is already residual, not another river.

The packaging side has no matching "residual river." Advanced packaging and testing is only about 10% of TSMC revenue, yet C.C. Wei called it tight on two calls in a row. Share of revenue is small; the constraint is hard, because this slice has no substitute: HBM must be bonded on an interposer before it can enter a training cabinet. Phone logic can skip CoWoS. Apple locks phone logic. It cannot lock the increment. If clouds move inference to ASICs plus conventional DDR5, they stop bidding against NVIDIA for full HBM output. That is this road's only physical benefit. What they add, though, is residual already rising; and if a 5nm die still wants high-density interconnect, they still return to the queue. Source 04's conclusion: public next generations have not stopped. If they do not stop, the memory tax and the packaging quota are collected together.

CXMT follows the international floor. The prospectus: 0 mentions of HBM. It can pull some low-end demand off the consumer platter and let the three majors leave good wafers for HBM and server DIMMs. That is residual reallocation. It is not a tax-free DDR5 river for cloud ASICs, and still less a no-queue CoWoS river for AMD. If domestic advanced packaging can already volume enough to hold training cabinets, an administrative lock is enough to keep a closed loop. If it cannot, the administrative lock leaves only the phone layer. This volume writes that sentence as the wrong layer, not as a moral judgment.

### 3.12 Why the three doors cannot be pulled apart

Analysis added. The first door is packaging. Without an interposer, dual dice and HBM are only a pile of dice. Microsoft itself wrote Maia 100 as CoWoS-S, which is admitting that the first door also catches custom silicon. The second door is interconnect. The draft writes NVLink 5.0 as 130 TB/s per cabinet and access below 100 ns, and Ethernet as 100 GB/s, 1.5 to 3.0 microseconds. Even if those numbers are only draft, the mechanism does not depend on nanosecond precision: inside the cabinet, 72 chips treated as one memory must have latency an order of magnitude below inter-cabinet Ethernet. The third door is the compiler stack. CUDA, NVLink, and the cabinet standard are the referee. Nemotron, NeMo, and NIM are the model player. The revenue bulk is still on cabinets. An ASIC that opens only the first may still lose at cabinet level to the whole cabinet. A software alliance that opens only the third cannot burn without a packaging slot.

That is why "Broadcom helps a cloud make a chip" must be demoted when it enters this volume. What Broadcom can do is design service and some packaging coordination. TSMC does not split CoWoS by customer. Sell-side writes Broadcom as about 11% or about 15%. The two sets do not match. This volume promotes neither to fact. What cloud custom silicon can change is who writes the spec and who collects the software rent. What it cannot change is that the stacks still have to be made by Hynix, Micron, and Samsung, and packaging still has to be queued by someone. Maia 200 itself writes 216 GB HBM3e. Ironwood itself writes 192 GB HBM3E and eight stacks. Trainium3 itself writes 144 GB HBM3e. From MTIA 300 on, Meta itself writes HBM stacks. The four sentences together are not "custom silicon equals a breakout." They are "custom silicon turns the scarce-slice buyer from one firm into five."

### 3.13 The world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down

The main line closes: the world does not blow up. Upgrades stop one node down. One firm writes the rules. Phones and PCs that never buy a GPU still pay a memory spillover tax. If those five are not done, the line walks past half of the scarce slice. This volume lands "one node down" on packaging: front-end 3nm is already 24% of revenue, 5nm 36%, so capacity looks open; packaging and test is only about 10%, yet C.C. Wei says it limits customer growth. One node down is not "the world cannot do 3nm." It is "a die that can be done at 3nm cannot be bonded into enough interposer." Phones can stop on a package that does not need CoWoS. Training cabinets cannot. So consumer electronics pays a memory tax. Training cabinets pay a packaging quota. The two taxes have different collection points and different collectors. The memory-tax collector is makers shifting output. The packaging-quota collectors are TSMC's queue and NVIDIA's prepayments. This volume writes the second.

The window is the few years before the next first-to-market node is locked by commitments. If Rubin's mix keeps enlarging the interposer and HBM4, 5.39 will only walk up. The window is not waiting for 835,000 cabinets. The window is, before the next generation is written into the $119.0B commitment queue, whether the West binds the three checks, or whether clouds truly stop at 5nm. Neither is done now.
