# Volume 34: Global Semiconductor Supply-Chain Restructuring — Equipment, Materials, Manufacturing, and Geopolitical Redundancy

Chinese original: [vol-34.md](vol-34.en.md)

Packaging tightness: [Volume 24](vol-24.en.md). The CHIPS Act takes only official award notices. This volume does not promote a High-NA verbal price, and does not promote a CoWoS ramp table.

## 1. Core question

A frontier AI system depends on a much longer industrial chain than the accelerator vendor's logo suggests: lithography, optics, wafers, gases and chemicals, photoresists, leading-edge foundry capacity, advanced packaging, HBM, substrates, networking, cooling, and power.

This volume asks:
1. where the most difficult single points of failure remain;
2. which parts can be geographically duplicated within the next decade;
3. how industrial policy changes resilience without assuming that a subsidy immediately creates equivalent yield, tooling, or customer qualification;
4. how export controls and geopolitical fragmentation change the cost of redundancy.

The report distinguishes announced projects, awarded subsidies, installed capacity, qualified production, and economically competitive volume.

## 3. Long-duration commitments reshape allocation before spot markets clear

Supply commitments: [Volume 1](vol-01.en.md). When buyers purchase quota with prepayments, the price signal distorts. Spot shortage is covered by contracts until some cloud cuts orders. Only then do foundries and memory makers see inventory at the same time. What is visible now is commitments still rising, not already cut. Multi-year cloud is another line, used for R&D compute, not wafer prepayments. Both are long money. Neither is spot.

## 4. Lithography is a single point. There is no official price list

ASML is the sole commercial supplier of EUV lithography systems, making lithography an unusually concentrated upstream layer. High-NA tools are the next exposure step, used to print finer lines. The company does not publish an official per-tool price list. Several public reports in February 2024 wrote that the company verbally put a High-NA Twinscan at about reported High-NA tool-price estimates, roughly twice a then Low-NA tool; and that it had already taken 10 to 20 early orders, planning about 20 units a year by 2028. These are reports, not a price list. The report does not treat $3.8B as a first-party factory sticker. Installation itself takes hundreds of crates, hundreds of engineers, and months. The terror of the single point is not one machine's sticker. It is that globally deliverable units, installation engineers, and matching optics all sit on the same Europe-to-East-Asia corridor.

Export controls have already written EUV as a license item that cannot ship to specified markets. The Bureau of Industry and Security October 2022 interim final rule wrote advanced-compute chips and certain semiconductor manufacturing equipment into the Export Administration Regulations. Without this machine, leading-edge logic is not "switch foundries and resume." The whole exposure track does not exist. A chip act can subsidize buildings and tool purchases. It cannot subsidize ASML's delivery queue or the Zeiss layer of mirrors. A late home fab must bid the same queue as the incumbents. The timetable may be stretched by political orders, not shortened.

2nm-fab investment and historical wafer price tables were not read as TSMC or ASML official splits in the current evidence review. They are not brought back from old drafts. The annual report only writes that 2nm entered volume in 4Q25. What can be written: the exposure single point decides who is eligible for the next generation. It does not decide that a second fab already exists that can volume-produce the same-generation AI logic.

## 5. Japanese materials: company filings exist; global share uses survey scope

The title names Japanese semiconductor materials. What can be confirmed to a company's own release starts with Shin-Etsu Chemical. For the fiscal year ended 31 March 2025: operating profit JPY 742.1B, net income attributable to owners JPY 534.0B, equity ratio 82.6%. The annual report writes silicon wafers, photoresists, and mask blanks as the semiconductor-materials spine, and writes expansion in growth markets. In 2025 the company announced a fourth photoresist-materials site in Isesaki, Gunma; phase one including land about JPY 83.0B, phase one planned complete in 2026, all own funds. That is an incumbent expansion, not an already-published global share.

The Nikkei 2025 major-goods-and-services share survey writes: silicon wafers, Shin-Etsu 26.3%, SUMCO 17.8%, the two together 44.1%; photoresists, Tokyo Ohka, JSR, and Shin-Etsu together 60.5%. This is a newspaper survey, not an official share line in those four companies' annual reports. This volume takes it only as survey scope. It is not promoted to first-party proof of a global monopoly. What the survey can show as mechanism: tolerance for impurities and defects tightens by node. Once materials are bound by an export license, a fab does not resume by switching reagent vendors. It must re-qualify the whole line. The cycle is counted in quarters to years. This is not the same as a banned chip design. Design can change architecture. Materials qualification cannot skip steps.

Japan's place on this chain is materials and equipment subsystems, not a GPU brand. Control a design firm and the hit is the banned buyer. Control photoresist or mask blanks and the hit is the common gate of global top-tier AI foundry. A materials license is therefore a tool discussed less than the Entity List and, once used, more global. This volume does not write it as an embargo that has already happened. Shin-Etsu already has a third photoresist-materials plant in Yunlin, Taiwan. Isesaki is the fourth. The incumbent is using multiple sites to cut interruption. It has not already moved the chain out of East Asia.

## 6. The US CHIPS Act: the appropriations are real; copying the single points is not done

The 2022 CHIPS and Science Act manufacturing incentives at Commerce: the Congressional Research Service writes an appropriation of $39.0B for financial incentives to domestic fabs and the supply chain; another $11.0B to the National Institute of Standards and Technology side for R&D. Commerce's implementation talk also uses $39.0B for manufacturing incentives, of which up to $6.0B may be used for the cost of loans and guarantees, with a loan-principal cap of $75.0B. These are statute and departmental scope, not cash already fully spent.

Commerce announced in November 2024 up to $6.6B of direct funding for TSMC Arizona, plus up to $5.0B of proposed loans, against the company's planned investment of more than $65.0B in three greenfield leading-edge fabs in Phoenix, paid on milestones. That is an official award notice. Whether the three fabs can, inside this decade, spit out HPC wafers of the same generation as Taiwan is a question of shipments, not investment dollars.

Intel press release, 26 November 2024: Commerce awarded up to $7.86B of direct funding, covering manufacturing and advanced packaging in Arizona, New Mexico, Ohio, and Oregon. The company's 8-K writes up to $7.8B of direct funding plus $65M for workforce development. On 27 August 2025 the company's 8-K writes: on the closing date of the transaction with Commerce, an accelerated $5.695B was funded, and common stock and warrants were issued to Commerce. The money is moving. The equity arrangement is moving. That does not equal Ohio or Arizona already being able to volume-produce the dual-die that training cabinets need.

What a statute most easily promises is land, buildings, and tax. What is hardest to deliver is engineer density, the materials-qualification network, and the lithography delivery queue. A subsidized fab without High-NA, without materials qualification, and without the same class of advanced packaging is only an ordinary fab. News can hold many home fabs. The line that can ship the dual-die for a training cabinet still sits on the original single points. Buyers adapt with dual-track quotes: political purchases take the high home price; real training cabinets stay on the original chain. That is not a conspiracy. It is a rational choice of yield and lead time.

A sovereign can wait ten years to climb the ladder. Endpoints that cannot wait cannot wait for an act. That is first principles, not another invented appropriation.

## 7. The EU Chips Act: Dresden is an auto and industrial node, not a training cabinet

European Commission press release, 20 August 2024: approval of EUR 5.0B of German state aid to support European Semiconductor Manufacturing Company building a fab in Dresden. The joint-venture parties are TSMC, Bosch, Infineon, and NXP. The Commission writes: 300 mm wafers; nodes covering 28 or 22 nm and 16 or 12 nm, finFET, serving automotive and industrial demand; planned full production in 2029, 480,000 wafers a year; an open foundry, not limited to shareholder orders. Reuters the same day wrote a total project investment of about EUR 10B; the aid was then the largest under the Chips Act, and Germany's first.

What can be confirmed: Europe used large money to invite TSMC to Dresden. What cannot be confirm: this fab will produce top-tier AI logic. The Commission itself wrote the nodes at 28 to 12 nm. In TSMC's 2025 wafer revenue, 7 nm and below already account for 74%; HPC accounts for 58%. Dresden takes auto and industrial, not the scarce slice of the training cabinet. The EU Chips Act mobilization figure is often written as more than EUR 43.0B, with a 2030 global-share target of 20%. EUR 43.0B is a mobilization target, not cash already spent. 20% is a policy target, not an already-achieved foundry share. This volume marks a target as a target.

More advanced European projects such as Intel Magdeburg: the current evidence review did not read a Commission approval notice at Dresden's grade, and does not fill in amounts. Without an approval notice, there is no sentence that "Europe has already copied the single point."

## 12. The reproducibility ladder: an act can climb the bottom, not the top

Drawing the chain as US design, Taiwan manufacturing, Korea memory, Dutch lithography, Japanese materials is only geography labels. What helps a decision is a reproducibility ladder. Assembly and test can change place. OEMs have already shown they can deliver qualified systems. Advanced packaging is the next hardness: officials admit tightness and that it limits customer growth; ramps are counted in years; the company gives no monthly capacity. Wafer foundry is harder: HPC already 58%, 7 nm and below 74%, first customer 19% and still rising. Materials and lithography are hardest: not because they are expensive, but because the qualification network and installation engineers cannot be copied overnight with an appropriation.

CHIPS-style policy most easily promises the bottom of the ladder. The US $39.0B manufacturing incentive, TSMC Arizona up to $6.6B, Intel up to $7.86B, Europe Dresden EUR 5.0B, all land on the bottom and the middle: build fabs, buy tools, invite foundry onshore to ship auto and industrial nodes. The top is still the EUV queue, Japanese materials qualification, and Taiwan's advanced-packaging slots. Dresden writes 28 to 12 nm. That is exactly the point: money can invite TSMC. It cannot invite the training-cabinet line.

A latecomer must climb the same ladder and also cut into an equipment queue the incumbent has already locked. Supply commitments rose in three months to $119.0B. That shows how strong cash flow and long-duration commitments can reinforce access to future capacity. A new domestic fab may improve geographic diversity before it becomes a production-equivalent frontier-AI second source. The result can therefore be political plurality without immediate technical substitutability.

## 15. Gaps and what cannot be treated as fact

ASML has no official per-tool price list. $3.8B is a 2024-reported verbal round number. 2nm-fab investment and historical wafer price tables were not checked in the current evidence review and are not written. Chip-on-wafer-on-substrate monthly capacity, and NVIDIA at 60% of packaging, are not a TSMC split. Nikkei silicon wafers 26.3% and photoresists 60.5% are a survey, not company annual reports. The CHIPS Act $39.0B is an appropriation authorization, not cash already spent. The older $670B–$750B four-cloud CapEx figure is a stress-test forecast; current company guidance uses incompatible accounting windows and cannot be forced into that total. $215B of AI hardware does not hold. Already sued, about to issue 10%, the White House forbidding a split, a materials embargo already in force: none of these can be written. A backdoor is an allegation. It does not enter the fact layer.

The judgments this volume can leave are only these sentences. The lithography single point is one firm. Materials qualification cannot skip steps. An act can build a fab. It cannot build the training-cabinet line. Dresden itself proves that. Endpoints that cannot wait cannot wait for the ladder.

## 16. Packaging estimate against the acts: taking half does not require locking the whole fab

The estimate pool: [Volume 16](vol-16.en.md) and [Volume 24](vol-24.en.md). Half of the scarce slice is packaging, not full-year shipments.

The act comparison writes this shut. Arizona up to $6.6B plus a proposed $5.0B loan, against more than $65.0B of investment and three greenfield fabs, aims to move leading-edge logic to the United States. Dresden EUR 5.0B of aid, nodes written at 28 to 12 nm, 480,000 wafers a year, full production in 2029, serving auto and industrial. Both are real money. Neither equals already copying the 5.39 packaging line onto home soil. Intel's $7.86B award covers manufacturing and advanced packaging; $5.695B was already booked in August 2025, and equity was handed over. Money and equity are moving. The packaging slot a training cabinet needs is still tight on the original single point. C.C. Wei in the summer of 2026 was still saying: packaging is so tight that it limits customer growth.

So the present tense of supply-chain reconstruction is: prepayments buy the slot, an act buys the building, scarcity value is still collected at $193.7B. If reconstruction were done, it would show as a home fab beginning to limit customer growth, not as one more fab in a press release. The second official "limits customer growth" has not yet appeared in Arizona or Dresden. This volume leaves that sentence for a later earnings call. It does not write it as already happened.
