# Research Methodology and Evidence Grading

> Editorial units, terminology, and publication language should remain consistent with [the editorial guide](../编辑与单位规范.md).

> Applies to the full *Age of AI Infrastructure Hegemony* monograph. The retrieval cutoff follows the root README.

## 1. Research object

This report is not a study of one chip, one company, or one market. It studies how AI infrastructure transmits across semiconductors, memory, advanced packaging, hyperscaler CapEx, data centers, power, software, capital markets, and labor—and how those transmission mechanisms may reshape industrial structure from 2026 to 2036.

Core questions:

1. Is AI becoming one of the buyers with the highest willingness to pay, strongest order certainty, and longest-duration demand for advanced means of production?
2. If so, will profit-seeking resource allocation continue to tilt scarce capacity toward AI/HPC?
3. Can effective total supply expand faster than AI absorbs the increment?
4. Do non-AI industries receive true incremental supply—or only the residual left after AI and other priority demand are allocated?
5. If a leading platform repeatedly captures a large share of incremental scarce resources, can a reinforcing loop emerge: resources → installations → ecosystem → cash flow → stronger resource reservation?
6. Which variables can interrupt that loop?

## 2. Five-layer structure: F → H → M → S → X

### F — Fact

Observed claims that can be verified.

Includes:
- financial statements and cash-flow statements;
- official product specifications;
- formal contracts, regulatory records, and court documents;
- company disclosures on capacity, revenue, and customer concentration;
- high-quality institutional historical statistics.

Fact-layer requirements:
- every number needs a date or period;
- accounting scope must be explainable;
- revenue share must not be converted into wafer share;
- guidance must not be written as actual;
- secondary estimates must not be rewritten as issuer disclosure.

### H — Hypothesis

Explicit future input variables.

Examples:
- AI demand growth;
- effective leading-edge logic supply growth;
- 50% / 60% / 70% / 80% stress levels for a leading platform's share of incremental advanced HPC resources;
- HBM wafer trade ratio;
- advanced-packaging expansion;
- actual grid-connection progress;
- token-price decline.

A hypothesis is not a fact. It may be aggressive, but it must satisfy two conditions:
1. it does not conflict with known physical, financial, engineering, or industrial constraints;
2. it states what observable change would invalidate it.

### M — Mechanism

The causal chain connecting current facts and future outcomes.

Key mechanisms in this report include:
- **Super-buyer Effect**
- **AI Resource Gravity**
- **Capacity Preemption**
- **Advanced Capacity Capture**
- **Non-AI Residual Capacity**
- **Scarcity-layer Value Capture / Compute Rent**
- **Rolling Bottleneck Cascade**
- **Installed-base Flywheel**
- **Hysteresis**
- **Cloud System-Stack Substitution**
- **Anchor Compute Buyer Effect**
- **AI Software Unit Economics**
- **Task-to-Job Translation**

### S — Scenario

A future state generated under an explicit set of hypotheses.

The report does not ask “what must happen.” It asks:

> If H1, H2, and H3 hold simultaneously, and transmission occurs through M1, M2, and M3, what industrial state follows?

Baseline, strong-concentration, competitive-reversal, and hard-landing states should all remain available.

### X — Falsification

Every strong claim must answer:

> What evidence would force us to downgrade, overturn, or rewrite this conclusion?

Examples:
- Samsung, Intel Foundry, or another supplier becomes a large-scale frontier-AI second source;
- AI compute-demand growth stays below effective supply growth for multiple periods;
- HBM or advanced packaging ceases to be scarce;
- cloud system stacks achieve large-scale training and inference substitution;
- leading compute performance no longer depends materially on the latest process nodes;
- power/interconnection constraints are relieved at scale and low cost.

## 3. Evidence grades

### A — First-party confirmation
Company filings, 10-K/20-F, regulatory announcements, official product pages, government documents, formal contract disclosures, and court materials.

### B — High-quality independent research
OECD, IEA, academic work, industry associations, TrendForce, and similar institutions with explicit methodology and sustained coverage.

### C — Sell-side / supply-chain estimates
Broker research, channel checks, and media-reported capacity allocation, customer shares, prices, lead times, and similar estimates. Useful for scenario anchoring, but not issuer disclosure.

### D — Report hypotheses and model outputs
Stress tests, range assumptions, geometry calculations, counterfactual outputs, and forward scenario parameters.

## 4. Three prohibitions

1. **No dimensional substitution:** revenue share ≠ wafer share ≠ leading-node share ≠ packaging-wafer share.
2. **No prediction-as-fact:** 2027E, 2030E, and 80% pressure tests must remain hypotheses or scenarios.
3. **No “total supply growth = non-AI recovery”:** AI absorption and other priority demand must be subtracted first.

## 5. Core models

### 5.1 Non-AI Residual Capacity

```text
NonAI_Residual(t)
= Effective_Supply(t)
- AI_Absorption(t)
- Other_Priority_Demand(t)
```

If the increment in AI absorption remains larger than the increment in effective supply, total supply can rise while non-AI residual supply still deteriorates.

### 5.2 Advanced HPC Incremental Capacity Capture Ratio

```text
AHICR
= Head_Player_Incremental_Advanced_HPC_Capacity
/ Total_Incremental_Advanced_HPC_Capacity
```

The report uses 50% / 60% / 70% / 80% as stress levels. The denominator is the incremental resource pool being modeled—not all TSMC capacity.

### 5.3 Reinforcing loop

```text
More scarce resources
→ more shipments and installations
→ more developer/customer feedback
→ stronger software and next-generation products
→ stronger cash flow and order certainty
→ stronger prepayment and reservation capacity
→ more scarce resources in the next generation
```

This is the core dynamic behind the report's “infrastructure hegemony” scenario. It is not a simple extrapolation from current market share.

## 6. How external research is used

External reports serve three purposes:

1. **Fact calibration:** add data or accounting scope not covered elsewhere.
2. **Mechanism comparison:** test whether independent institutions observe the same competitive structure or bottleneck.
3. **Counterargument and falsification:** prioritize research that challenges the report's conclusions.

Evidence grade is not upgraded merely because a source agrees with the report, and disagreement is not grounds for exclusion.

## 7. Responsibilities of the three core indexes

To avoid one file acting simultaneously as evidence table, bibliography, and opinion digest, the monograph separates three roles:

- [Core Evidence Ledger](核心证据台账.en.md): high-impact claims, current values, periods, grades, and what each claim does or does not support.
- [Core Source Index](核心参考文献索引.en.md): maps high-impact claims back to first-party or high-quality institutional sources.
- [External Research Comparison Index](../外部研究对照索引.md): compares frameworks, counterarguments, and methodological boundaries in OECD, IEA, Stanford, ILO, SIA/Deloitte, and similar work.

These files are not substitutes for one another. A source appearing in the bibliography does not mean it supports every inference in the book; a claim appearing in the evidence ledger does not mean its future scenario has been proven.
