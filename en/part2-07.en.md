## 1. The question this essay answers

Volumes 36 and 37 wrote valuation and destocking. This essay only does a macro stress test, and asks three things. First, already-happened four-cloud capex: which line items match, which do not. Second, the duration mismatch of 4-year hardware against 15-year civil works: on whose books the risk stays. Third, which usage and unit-price combinations crack buyer depreciation first, rather than seller gross margin first. The draft treated "$215B already happened, 2026 $750B" as two anchors of the stress test. This essay does not promote those two figures to official. What official sources can nail is each firm's own reported profit, cash, and equipment purchases. Mixed-year sums and forecast sums are only draft scenarios.

## 4b. Why mixed-year cannot be divided by four

Analysis added. The step the draft most easily treats as "already happened" is taking $215B as AI hardware, then dividing by 4, getting $45B–$55B annualized depreciation. That step has three breakpoints. Break any one, and the breakeven revenue and token matrix after it break with it.

First: the numerator is not AI hardware. Section 4 already nailed it. $211.9–$213.9B is mixed-year group-wide equipment purchases. Fulfillment networks, Reality Labs, office buildings are inside. Dividing group-wide by 4 does not give "AI server depreciation." It amortizes warehouses, glasses, and conference rooms over four years too.

Second: the denominator is not a unified four years. On the same Microsoft cash-flow statement, purchases $64,551M, depreciation, amortization, and other $34,153M. If four-year straight line held, one firm's already-landed depreciation would not just equal one-fourth of that year's purchases. Civil works are even less four years. Bundling a 15-year power contract and a four-year GPU into one divisor is duration mixing itself, not accounting.

Third: the seller almost never enters this division. Nvidia FY26 itself spent only $6,042M on equipment, free cash $96,575M. If a stress test starts from "industry depreciation" and then treats seller profit as cash the industry has already recovered, it writes already-booked rent as an investment not yet recovered. Rent is $193,737M. Investment is each buyer's own PPE. The two rows cannot be each other.

So this essay does not use $45B–$55B, and does not use $115.5B. Depreciation that can enter the fact layer is, for now, only this Microsoft row $34,153M. The other three names' D&A were not nailed in this pass's evidence. Leave them blank. Four-cloud total depreciation has no official total. Do not invent one.

## 5. The bubble's micro-mechanism is duration mixing

Analysis added. A bubble is not four characters, "priced too high." It bundles three durations into one narrative. First: a 2–4 year GPU generation, profit already confirmed on the seller. FY26 free cash $96,575M, Q1 FY27 one quarter of operating income $53,536M, last two quarters' gross margin back near 75%. Second: the buyer's IT depreciation of about 4 years. Microsoft FY25 depreciation, amortization, and other already reported $34,153M, clearly up from prior-year $22,287M; that is one firm's official depreciation, not a four-cloud total, and still less the draft $45B–$55B. Third: 15–20 year civil works and power contracts. Using the first duration's profit to prove the third duration's valuation is the error this essay takes apart.

Physical stickiness decides destocking is not symmetric. HBM lines and advanced packaging cannot be switched back to phones by the week; a high-power campus cannot be turned into apartments; a long power contract cannot be turned into spot. What can clear quickly is cabinets not yet racked and model-firm interfaces. So the first photograph of a "burst" should be shorter lead times and spot concessions, not a plant demolished. Fourth-layer assets will stay on the books, becoming a long liability or a moat after reconcentration, depending on the switches later, not on a one-week stock-price drawdown.

Commercially, usage sits in the workflow; unit price sits in open source and inference efficiency. The seller cannot control usage, yet locked its own shipments through prepayments. Profit confirms on the seller first. Risk bursts later on the buyer and the line. That is a classic capacity cycle. The draft wrote DeepSeek training about $5.576 million, KV compressed 93.3%, as the technical condition for a price cut. Those are draft numbers. This essay does not newly check training-cost page numbers. The mechanism can stay: a lower entry fee presses unit price. It does not automatically push usage to the matrix's +300% cell. Without that cell, the draft's "down 50% still earns $15B" does not exist.

Geopolitically, dual-track expansion may let two overhangs appear globally at once. Controls cut the return channel. Unilateral clearing is steeper. Sovereign-fund holdings were zero in the draft. Do not invent amounts. Analysis added leaves only the structure: if you only hold tech stocks of the first duration, you are on the same side as retail; if you hold power and licenses of the third duration, that is what may still be left in a stress test.

## 6. How prepayments move the stress-test clock onto the buyer

Three-month commitments from $95.2B to $119.0B, an add of $23.8B, about four-tenths of Q1 FY27 operating income $53,536M. This is not a bet under cash stress. It is the person with the highest profit adding one more bin of prepayments. Official text writes substantially all will be paid through fiscal year 2027; of quarter-end $119.0B, public footnotes have written the remainder pays through 2028–2031. Same note: in certain instances, these agreements are cancellable, able to be rescheduled, or adjustable. Not every dollar is irrevocable. Do not write it as already buying TSMC's whole factory dead.

Hypothesis from here: as long as gross margin stays in the 71%–75% bin, commitments will keep adding. Anyone else who wants the same material pays more, or does not get it. TSMC has no financial reason to offend the first name for the second. A neutrality pledge has no consideration in front of a check. If buyer usage later cannot fill, what is cut is their own depreciation and power contracts, not free cash the seller has already booked. What a stress test must truly watch is whether the buyer can cover already-taken depreciation with workflow cash; not whether the seller will fall from 75% first.

Microsoft's comparison kills this. Net income $101,832M, operating cash $136,162M, PPE added $64,551M in a year, D&A already at $34,153M. The cloud is not unarmed. This hundred billion builds halls. NVIDIA FY26 net income $120,067M is earned selling the scarce slice itself. In the same year's free cash $96,575M, $41.1B went back to shareholders; it itself spent only $6,042M on equipment. A seller stress test can almost never do "capex crushes itself." A buyer stress test can, at any time.

## 7. Stress test: switches, not a fate table

Analysis added. The draft token matrix is scenario arithmetic, unverified. This essay does not invent a new unit price, a new call volume, or a new four-cloud total depreciation. Downgrade the matrix to a checklist: does your usage assumption match your unit-price assumption, and does it match already-taken depreciation. If you cannot answer, do not talk supercycle.

Switch A: soft landing. Usage keeps up with already-happened equipment purchases. The unit-price cut does not eat gross margin bare. Mixed-year 2025 four-cloud already added to about $354.0B, which says buyers were still adding then, not already cutting. On this path seller gross margin gives a little back. It does not collapse. The Agents mature period is the draft's narrative, not this essay's forecast.

Switch B: destocking. Usage parks on a low cell. Unit price falls clearly. What is testable is not "$750B becomes a bad debt" — that figure cannot be added — but shorter lead times, slower prepayment growth, second-hand cabinets appearing at a concession. HBM and advanced packaging show empty slots. Power contracts become idle watts. This is the buyer cracking first.

Switch C: a wrong-kill then reconcentration. Second-line model firms and seat-license software without workflow cash exit first. Quota and power contracts stay with clouds and makers who can wait. A bubble strengthens four-corner rent collection. It does not prove AI is useless. 40% oligopoly is draft discussion weight. This essay assigns no probability.

Switch D: rates. The draft had no ten-year Treasury or tech implied vol. This essay invents no basis points. The mechanism holds: 4-year depreciation looks like growth investment in cheap money, and like leverage that must produce cash at once in expensive money. Mixed-year $211.9–$213.9B was made under some cost of funds. If 2026's un-addable $750B is stacked with a higher discount rate, the forecast table will collapse itself, without waiting for tokens down 50%. Writing a bubble entirely as a technology story or entirely as a speculation story both miss this term.

Counters: calling a stock-price drawdown a burst; treating $750B as already happened; writing mixed-year $215B as "AI hardware"; writing +300% / down 50% still earning as "a price cut is riskless."

## 8. Who exits first: order can be written. Headcount is not invented

Analysis added. By mechanism, not by shipment statistics.

The first wave is model firms and seat-license SaaS without workflow cash. The draft wrote margin walking from 85% toward 55%. That is a business-model assumption, not one firm's already-audited path. When usage cannot fill and unit price falls, this side first cuts people unrelated to delivery. Customer-side substitution projects stall. What the labor market will see is "AI-project layoffs," not "AI has already finished substituting."

The second wave is hardware resellers with no quota, piecing Ethernet clusters from spot. No prepayment, no queue. The moment lead times loosen, their inventory becomes a spot concession first.

The third wave is those among the clouds who filled 2026 spend to the un-addable $750B, while usage parked on a low cell. Depreciation precedes revenue. Microsoft has already reported one firm's D&A at $34,153M, which says D&A on buyer books is rising. It is not a narrative. Four-cloud total depreciation draft $45B–$55B is not promoted to official.

Only the fourth wave is Nvidia and the memory makers. Only when buyers cut prepayments, and advanced packaging and HBM show empty slots, will 71%–75% and Hynix 49% give back at once. Until then the seller is still the bidder with one year of $96,575M free cash and one quarter of $53,536M operating income. Plants and 15-year civil works almost never die. They only change owners.

This order is a reminder about "overweight physical scarcity." Overweighting assets after the fourth wave will look like a stupid long duration through the first three waves; in reconcentration they become what is still there. A stress test does not tell you which wave to stand on. It only tells you not to price the fourth wave with the first wave's stock price. Essay 7 and Volume 36 are two scales of the same thing: one writes the valuation clock, one writes who exits first.

The five paths that can interrupt this acceleration, the judgment already wrote: a real Western coalition; a cloud ASIC pulling training off; power jamming expansion dead; the East standing a second bid; a business split or a forced opening of CUDA. None of the five has been done. What has been done: supply commitments added to $119.0B; the investigation is there, a complaint is not. A fine is a cost. It does not change the bid. If those five are not done, the line walks the scarce slice past halfway. The window is these few years before next-generation first silicon is locked by commitments. A stress test that only watches the stock price will lose the window.

Only public, non-inventable. Whether Nvidia supply commitments keep leaving $119.0B, or growth slows. Whether free cash still sits near the one-year $96,575M bin, and whether operating income is still read by the quarter, not by equity marks. Whether Microsoft PPE keeps leaving FY25 $64,551M, and D&A keeps leaving $34,153M. Whether Amazon net, Alphabet purchases, Meta equipment are still adding, or turning. Whether three-name 2026 guidance still parks near the evidence-nailed $200B / $175–$185B / $115–$135B, or later changes its mouth; a change of mouth is still group-wide, not an AI line item. Whether justice.gov shows a complaint is unrelated to this essay's depreciation, but what can change the rules is not a fine. HBM lead times, second-hand cabinet prints, public token prices: write them when there is a public series. If there is not, do not invent them.
