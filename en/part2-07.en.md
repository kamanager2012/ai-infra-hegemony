# Macro Essay 7: The Capital Supercycle, Bubble Mechanisms, and Extreme Stress Tests

Chinese source edition: [part2-07.md](../part2-07.md)

> Retrieval and revision cutoff: **2026-08-27**. This essay treats the bubble question as a duration, utilization, and cash-flow problem. Earlier stress-test arithmetic is not promoted into reported financial fact.

## 1. The core capital question

The AI-infrastructure capital cycle combines three very different clocks:

1. short-cycle compute hardware;
2. medium-duration IT and data-center assets;
3. long-duration buildings, grids, and power contracts.

The central risk is not merely “valuations are high.”

It is:

> **Can productive workload and cash flow grow fast enough to cover assets whose economic lives are longer than the technology cycle that justified them?**

That is the microeconomic mechanism behind an infrastructure bubble.

## 2. The four-cloud CapEx number has to be scoped correctly

Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta report different capital-spending categories and use different fiscal windows.

On a mixed-year 2025 basis, comparable PPE/equipment purchases reached roughly **$354B** in aggregate scale.

That number is useful as evidence that buyer-side capital intensity has risen sharply.

It is not:

- one official “AI hardware” line;
- one same-period four-cloud accounting total;
- a valid base for a universal four-year depreciation formula.

The report therefore tracks the four companies individually.

## 3. Seller cash flow and buyer asset burden are asymmetric

NVIDIA represents the seller side.

FY26 free cash flow was **$96.575B**.

FY27 Q2 GAAP operating income was **$63.734B**, with Data Center revenue of **$89.0B** and GAAP gross margin of **75.0%**.

The hyperscalers represent major buyer-side balance sheets.

Their capital pays for:

- servers;
- networking;
- buildings;
- land;
- power;
- custom silicon;
- third-party systems.

The seller can recognize margin before the buyer has proven the full return on the infrastructure.

This timing difference is a defining feature of the cycle.

## 4. Why “CapEx ÷ 4 = AI depreciation” fails

The shortcut fails for at least three reasons.

### 4.1 The numerator is mixed

Company-wide PPE includes assets other than AI servers.

### 4.2 The denominator is mixed

Servers, buildings, grids, and power contracts do not share one useful life.

### 4.3 Seller profit and buyer depreciation are different accounting objects

NVIDIA's revenue and free cash flow should not be netted against hyperscaler PPE as though they were two sides of one consolidated statement.

A useful first-party buyer-side anchor is Microsoft's FY25 **$34.153B** of depreciation, amortization, and other.

The report does not construct an unsupported four-cloud depreciation total from mixed accounting categories.

## 5. The bubble mechanism is duration mismatch

The three clocks can be summarized as:

### Hardware clock

Accelerators can become economically obsolete within a few years.

### Data-center clock

Buildings, cooling, and electrical infrastructure last much longer.

### Power-contract clock

PPAs and other energy commitments can extend for a decade or more.

Using short-cycle seller profits to justify the long-duration value of the entire infrastructure stack is analytically dangerous.

If workload disappoints, the hardware reprices first while the long-duration obligations remain.

## 6. What clears first?

A plausible clearing sequence is:

1. new hardware orders slow;
2. delivery lead times shorten;
3. second-hand system prices fall;
4. model and application firms without workflow cash cut spending;
5. data-center utilization weakens;
6. long-duration power and real-estate obligations remain and may change owners.

This is why the first sign of a hard landing should be searched for in:

- order behavior;
- utilization;
- residual values;
- commitments;

rather than in the demolition of power plants or data centers.

## 7. Supply commitments move the clock forward

NVIDIA's manufacturing, supply, and capacity commitments rose from **US$95.2B** as of 2026-01-25 to **US$119B** as of 2026-04-26 and then to **US$279B** as of 2026-07-26. The FY27 Q2 filing says the latest commitments are primarily tied to memory and manufacturing facilities. Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm .

These commitments show that the seller is reserving future supply.

They are not all irrevocable, and the company does not disclose a full supplier-level split.

The strategic point is:

> **seller-side cash generation is being converted into forward physical commitments before the buyer-side asset base has fully monetized.**

That strengthens the boom while also increasing the importance of future utilization.

## 8. Anchor Compute Buyers are a new leading indicator

The capital cycle can no longer be monitored only through hyperscaler CapEx.

Large model companies increasingly anchor infrastructure through long-duration demand.

Examples include:

- Stargate-scale infrastructure commitments;
- Anthropic/AWS long-duration compute commitments;
- multi-GW capacity plans.

The positive loop is:

**long-duration model demand  
→ project financeability  
→ earlier data-center/power/chip construction  
→ higher fixed commitments  
→ high utilization  
→ further buildout**

The negative loop is:

**commitments weaken or ramp slips  
→ utilization falls  
→ buyer ROI deteriorates  
→ CapEx slows  
→ upstream commitments soften**

Anchor Buyer behavior is therefore an important S4 leading signal.

## 9. Stress tests should be switches, not destiny tables

The report does not use one fixed token-price and usage matrix as a forecast.

Instead, it treats the variables as consistency checks.

### Switch A — Soft landing

Usage grows enough to absorb the installed asset base despite lower unit prices.

### Switch B — Inventory and asset clearing

Usage disappoints, unit prices fall, and buyers reduce new commitments.

Lead times shorten and second-hand prices weaken.

### Switch C — Clearing followed by reconcentration

Weaker model/software firms exit first, while scarce assets and long-duration contracts move toward better-capitalized platforms and infrastructure owners.

A hard landing can therefore increase concentration.

### Switch D — Higher cost of capital

Long-duration infrastructure becomes harder to justify even before token demand collapses.

The discount rate matters because AI assets span very different durations.

## 10. Who is most exposed first?

The likely sequence is structural, not a fixed company list.

### First

Model companies and application firms without durable workflow cash flow.

### Second

Resellers and capacity intermediaries dependent on continued scarcity.

### Third

Hyperscaler projects with high fixed commitments and weak utilization.

### Later

System sellers and memory suppliers—if buyer cuts finally reach upstream orders and physical scarcity eases.

The timing matters.

Seller margin can remain strong well into a period when buyer ROI has begun to deteriorate.

## 11. What would confirm S4 Capital Hard Landing?

The strongest evidence would be a synchronized deterioration in:

- hyperscaler CapEx guidance;
- NVIDIA supply commitments;
- Anchor Buyer commitments;
- utilization;
- second-hand system prices;
- cloud discounts;
- impairments;
- enterprise AI monetization.

A technology-stock drawdown alone is insufficient.

The bubble question is ultimately:

> **Has the industry built more long-duration infrastructure than economically productive workloads can support?**

As of August 2026, the evidence still supports **capital expansion with rising ROI pressure**, not a confirmed full clearing.
