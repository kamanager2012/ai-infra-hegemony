# Phase II · External Reference Landscape

> **Purpose:** benchmark this research program against current public reference systems without turning comparative claims into self-promotional assertions.  
> **Status:** research-side benchmarking only; not part of the frozen 32-chapter formal report.  
> **Last checked:** 2026-08-30.

## 1. What this file is — and is not

This is **not** a claim that no other work is “more comprehensive.” That would require an exhaustive universe that cannot be proven from public search.

The defensible statement is narrower:

> The report combines a breadth of physical infrastructure, system-stack competition, capital formation, workflow economics, labor/talent transmission, explicit evidence grading, claim traceability, falsification logic, and bilingual publication architecture that is unusual among the major public reference systems reviewed here.

The correct benchmark is therefore **dimension by dimension**, not “one report beats everyone.”

## 2. Current benchmark map

| Reference system | Where it is stronger / more specialized | What this report should learn from it |
|---|---|---|
| **SemiAnalysis** | Bottom-up accelerator/HBM, datacenter, power, cooling, facilities, cloud economics and supply-chain models; its Datacenter Industry Model covers >5,000 facilities and maps IT power, PUE, utility power and buildout constraints | Engineering granularity, continuously updated physical-infrastructure telemetry, customer/SKU/site-level modeling |
| **Goldman Sachs Research / Global Institute** | Company/market finance, hyperscaler CapEx, valuation, cost of capital, monetization debate, data-center power demand | Macro-financial transmission, valuation sensitivity, return-on-capital discipline, explicit linkage between CapEx and monetization |
| **IEA** | Electricity-system and regional demand/supply forecasting; 2026 work treats AI/data centers as a material driver of power-demand growth | Stronger power-system denominators, grid/regional forecasting, explicit energy-policy and reliability constraints |
| **Epoch AI** | Public datasets on AI models, training compute, GPU clusters, AI data centers, chip sales/components/owners; several datasets updated through Aug. 2026 | Empirical compute scaling, cluster/site validation, longitudinal training/inference infrastructure datasets |
| **NBER / academic LLM-market work** | Econometric identification of model/API pricing, demand and substitution using OpenRouter and Azure usage data | Actual elasticity estimation, identification discipline, confidence intervals rather than scenario-only coefficients |
| **CSIS / export-control-focused policy research** | Export-control implementation, China semiconductor policy, geopolitical and alliance mechanics | Convert geopolitics from background constraint into explicit scenario variables and update rules |

## 4. Benchmark facts verified in the 2026-08-30 sweep

- SemiAnalysis describes its Datacenter Industry Model as an end-to-end view across **>5,000 facilities**, including IT power, PUE, utility power, accelerator deployment and capex by power/cooling/facilities.
- Goldman Sachs Research reported in 2026 that hyperscaler/AI infrastructure CapEx estimates had risen sharply and separately published work focused on when AI investment will produce economic returns.
- IEA’s **Electricity 2026** explicitly identifies AI and data centers as major drivers of renewed electricity-demand growth in advanced economies.
- Epoch AI’s public data catalog in August 2026 includes AI Models, AI Data Centers, AI Chip Sales, AI Chip Components, AI Chip Owners and GPU Clusters; its GPU-cluster database covers **500+ clusters/supercomputers**.
- NBER Working Paper 34608 uses OpenRouter and Microsoft Azure API data and reports preliminary **short-run price elasticities just above one**.

## 5. Research discipline

Do not convert this benchmark into claims such as:

- “the world’s most comprehensive report”;
- “no competitor has comparable evidence”;
- “unique globally”;
- “better than SemiAnalysis / Goldman / IEA / Epoch.”

Those are not required for the report’s value and are difficult to prove.

Use this file only to answer:

> **Which outside system is the best benchmark for each missing empirical layer, and what should Phase II import from its discipline without copying its scope?**
