# Phase II · Ramp-Period Funding Gap v0.1

> **Status:** Research-side validation layer; not part of the frozen 32-chapter formal report, and does not change the formal 2026-08-28 evidence cutoff.  
> **Purpose:** put `15`'s required-cash floor on `17`'s energization clock, so that "does the contract cover the debt service" becomes a **time-phased cash curve** — the cumulative funding requirement during the ramp, its peak, and whether it ever closes.  
> **Reproducible artifact:** `scripts/recompute_ramp_gap.py` over `data/ramp-gap-inputs.json`, reading its anchors from `15`'s and `17`'s inputs and asserting against their published values. Outputs are D-grade report-side calculations.  
> **Chinese translation (derived):** [18-ramp-period-funding-gap.zh.md](18-ramp-period-funding-gap.zh.md)

## 0. Ownership contract v0.1

This file owns:

- the **time-phased composition** of `15`'s required-cash floor with `17`'s energization schedule — the cumulative funding-requirement curve `F(t)`;
- the **peak funding requirement**, its date, the amount still outstanding at contract end, and the crossover year;
- the **ramp penalty**: the extra cash generated by the fact that the required side attaches to deployed capital while the revenue side attaches to revenue-bearing capacity.

It does **not** own: the required cash floor or the contracted revenue per GW-year (`15`), the energization schedule or the conversion factors (`17`), facility or borrower state (`04C`/`04D`), capital structure (`11`), or scenario aggregation (`10`). It creates **no new anchors** — every input is read from the two upstream layers and asserted against their published values.

**Why a new owner.** `15` answers a snapshot question — *at full deployment, does revenue per GW-year cover required cash per GW-year* — and answers it with a dial ("GW served"). `17` answers a physical question — *when does capacity arrive* — and never prices it. Neither owns the object that a financing decision actually turns on: **the cumulative cash that has to come from somewhere else before the contract's own revenue covers debt service.** That object has a peak, a date, and a term-end balance, and none of them can be read off either layer alone.

## 1. Why the composition is required

`15` §5's coverage surface reads, at the mid-coupon US$30B/GW regime and the full 5 GW ceiling: **0.50×**. `17` §5 reads: an announced cohort converts to revenue-bearing capacity through a product of three sub-one factors, mid case **0.315**.

Each number alone is interpretable and neither is alarming in isolation. Composed they are the same quantity viewed twice — the coverage ratio and the conversion factor multiply — and the result is **0.158×**. That is what makes the composition necessary rather than decorative: the two layers were measuring different halves of one division.

## 2. The attachment mismatch

The mechanism the composition exposes is not a ratio error. It is an **attachment mismatch**:

- after first energization, the whole **drawn facility** pays debt service — including the GW that has not yet reached a revenue-bearing state;
- revenue accrues only on the share of capacity that **has**.

Everything in §4–§6 follows from that one asymmetry. It is why a coverage ratio computed per *served* GW-year (the `15` form) systematically flatters a facility that is still ramping: the served GW-year is the denominator that makes the number look best, and it is exactly the denominator that does not exist yet during the ramp.

## 3. Model conventions — stated, not hidden

| Convention | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Clock origin | `t = 0` at first debt draw | Cash service cannot begin before the facility is drawn |
| Deployment | linear over `D` years (`17`'s construction stream: 2–3) | No disclosed spend curve exists |
| First energization | `t = D` | Construction completes |
| Energization ramp | linear over `P` years (`17`'s ramp dial: 2–4) | `17`'s dial, reused unchanged |
| Contract clock | ten years from **first revenue**, not from announcement | The convention most favourable to the facility, and therefore the base |
| Capitalisation | share `kappa` of pre-energization service capitalised; base `kappa = 1.0` | Construction-period interest is normally capitalised, and a DSCR covenant is tested post-COD. `kappa = 0.0` shows the same curve with no capitalisation |
| Revenue attachment | contract ceiling × energization share × the three `17` factors | `17` §5, unchanged |

**Single-tenant reading.** The whole contract value is held against the whole cohort, so this is a single-tenant reading in which the anchor contract is the facility's only revenue source. `15` §5 already states that framing and it carries forward. Other tenants, equity, or non-debt capital can carry the difference.

**The comparison remains a necessary condition.** It ignores opex, tax, equity return, refresh capex, and residual value. Failing it is not a prediction of failure; it means *something other than this contract* must carry the difference.

## 4. Composed coverage

Coverage at full deployment is `15`'s ratio times `17`'s product: **`v · f / r`**.

| Required cash `r` | f = 1.000 | f = 0.720 | f = 0.315 | f = 0.105 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| IG-backed long @ US$20B/GW (1.87) | **1.067** | 0.768 | 0.336 | 0.112 |
| IG-backed long @ US$30B/GW (2.81) | 0.711 | 0.512 | 0.224 | 0.075 |
| Mid-coupon @ US$30B/GW (3.98) | 0.503 | 0.362 | 0.158 | 0.053 |
| Mid-coupon @ US$40B/GW (5.30) | 0.377 | 0.272 | 0.119 | 0.040 |
| High-coupon project @ US$30B/GW (4.22) | 0.474 | 0.341 | 0.149 | 0.050 |
| High-coupon project @ US$50B/GW (7.03) | 0.285 | 0.205 | 0.090 | 0.030 |

**One cell in twenty-four clears 1.0×**: the cheapest observed financing regime at US$20B/GW **and** perfect conversion (`f = 1.000`, i.e. every announced GW energizes, is utilized, and is monetized). At `17`'s mid case that same regime reads **0.336×**.

`f = 1.000` is included as a boundary, not as a case: it is the availability-billed reading in which the contract pays on energized GW regardless of utilization. It is the most favourable assumption available and it still only clears in one row.

## 5. Annual contractual shortfall

The same result in cash, which is how a facility experiences it — US$B per year at full deployment:

| Required cash `r` | f = 1.000 | f = 0.720 | f = 0.315 | f = 0.105 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| IG-backed long @ US$20B/GW | **−0.63** | 2.17 | 6.22 | 8.32 |
| IG-backed long @ US$30B/GW | 4.06 | 6.86 | 10.91 | 13.01 |
| Mid-coupon @ US$30B/GW | 9.88 | 12.68 | 16.73 | 18.83 |
| Mid-coupon @ US$40B/GW | 16.51 | 19.31 | 23.36 | 25.46 |
| High-coupon project @ US$30B/GW | 11.08 | 13.88 | 17.93 | 20.03 |
| High-coupon project @ US$50B/GW | 25.14 | 27.94 | 31.99 | 34.09 |

Negative means the contract covers. **The best case is US$0.63B of annual headroom on a US$10B/yr contract — 6.3%.** In the mid-coupon US$30B/GW regime the contract revenue is **US$10.00B/yr against a US$19.88B/yr requirement**, so US$9.88B/yr has no contractual source at full delivery.

## 6. The ramp-period peak

Cumulative funding requirement `F(t)` = ∫ (required cash − revenue) dt. In the one covering regime the closed form is exact and worth stating:

```text
peak F = C r^2 P / (2 v f)       at  tau = r P / (v f)  after first energization
       = C r D / 2 + that         when nothing is capitalised (kappa = 0)
```

*`C` = contract GW ceiling (5), `r` = required cash per GW-year (`15`), `v` = contracted revenue per served GW-year (`15`), `f` = conversion product (`17`), `P` = ramp width (`17`), `D` = deployment duration.*

| Case (base `kappa` = 1.0, `D` = 3, `P` = 4) | Coverage | Peak | Peak date | Outstanding at term end | Crossover |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| IG @ US$20B/GW, f = 1.000 | 1.067 | **US$17.58B** | y6.75 | **US$13.75B** | year 34.9 — **21.9 years after the contract ends** |
| IG @ US$20B/GW, f = 0.720 | 0.768 | — | — | US$36.1B | does not close |
| IG @ US$30B/GW, f = 1.000 | 0.711 | — | — | US$60.6B | does not close |
| Mid-coupon @ US$30B/GW, f = 1.000 | 0.503 | — | — | US$118.8B | does not close |
| High-coupon @ US$50B/GW, f = 1.000 | 0.285 | — | — | US$271.4B | does not close |

**On the rows that do not close:** with coverage below 1.0× the shortfall accumulates at the §5 annual rate for as long as the contract runs, and the cumulative figure is the mechanical consequence of that rate. Read those rows as **"does not close"**, never as a financing plan — a facility in that state would be financed differently, renegotiated, or not built, and the arithmetic is not a forecast of any of those.

**On the one row that does close:** the contract does not close it either. The requirement peaks at **US$17.58B** in year 6.75; the US$0.63B/yr surplus that follows the peak then runs for only **6.25 years** before the contract ends, retiring US$3.83B of it and leaving **US$13.75B outstanding** — 13.75% of the contract's entire US$100B nominal value, still owed with no contractual source. Full crossover arrives at year 34.9.

### Structural properties

- **The peak is invariant in `D` and exactly linear in `P`.** Under full capitalisation nothing is paid in cash before energization, so the deployment period is cash-neutral and only the ramp width moves the number — doubling `P` doubles the peak (US$8.79B at `P` = 2 → US$17.58B at `P` = 4). This is the sharpest link between the two layers: **`17`'s ramp dial is a linear multiplier on the funding requirement.**
- **The peak is quadratic in `r`.** A doubling of the required-cash rate quadruples it.
- **Capitalisation is worth 44% of the peak** (US$31.64B at `kappa` = 0 → US$17.58B at `kappa` = 1). This is the model's largest single convention, and it is stated rather than buried.

## 7. What the composition does and does not say

**It does not say any facility is in this position.** No named project discloses the three inputs jointly: realized capital per GW, energized capacity, and contract value per energized GW-year. The single-tenant reading is an assumption, not a description.

**It does not produce a verdict, an index, or a score.** The output is a curve and three scalars — peak, term-end balance, crossover. Collapsing them into one number would destroy the only thing the artifact is for, which is showing *where* the gap sits and *when* it closes.

**It does say something falsifiable and checkable:** at the observed financing terms, and reading the anchor contract as the facility's revenue source, the contract does not carry the facility's required cash floor **except in one cell of twenty-four** — and even there the un-retired ramp balance at contract end is US$13.75B.

**It explains a number the earlier layers could only state.** `15` showed coverage falling from 2.51× to 0.30× as the served-GW denominator rises. The composition shows why the same facility cannot simply wait: **the required side has already attached to the deployed base, and the ramp is where the two sides are furthest apart.**

**Composition is not aggregation.** `17` §10 and the README forbid multiplying `17` and `15` into a composite index. A cash-flow composition is the opposite move: it keeps both quantities in their own units, on a common time axis, and refuses to net them into a scalar. The prohibition is against the scalar, not against the time axis.

## 8. Falsification conditions

1. **A named facility discloses realized capital per GW, energized capacity, and contract value per energized GW-year jointly** — the composition becomes an observation for that facility and the dials collapse.
2. **A disclosed debt-service reserve account or capitalized-interest schedule** would replace `kappa` and the pre-COD service treatment with observed values.
3. **A disclosed spend curve** would replace the linear deployment assumption, which currently has no effect under full capitalisation but would matter at `kappa` < 1.
4. **Evidence that debt service attaches to energized rather than deployed capacity** — a covenant written on energized GW, or a construction-period service holiday longer than the ramp — would remove the attachment mismatch and with it the ramp penalty.
5. **A multi-tenant or non-debt capital structure shown to carry the ramp** would make the single-tenant reading inapplicable rather than wrong.

## 9. Boundary rules

- **No new anchors.** Every input is read from `15` and `17`; the script asserts the recomputed required-cash values against `15`'s published table so the layers cannot drift.
- **Necessary, not sufficient.** Opex, tax, equity return, refresh capex, and residual value are all outside.
- **No facility inference.** Nothing here is a claim about any named project's capital structure, actual debt service, or realized cash position.
- **No composite index.** The curve and its three scalars must never be netted into a score, and must never be multiplied into a multi-family signal.
- **Non-covering cumulative figures are mechanical.** They state the annual rate and read as "does not close".
- **No scenario switch, no S4, no evidence grade.** This cannot change `10`'s scenario relevance, cannot confirm S4, and cannot enter the frozen chapters.
- **D-grade labeling is mandatory** wherever an output is reused, under `04-research-transparency`'s hierarchy.

## 10. Integration

1. Re-run `scripts/recompute_ramp_gap.py` when `15`'s anchors or `17`'s dials move; the script asserts against `15`'s published required-cash table and fails loudly if the two layers diverge.
2. **Proposed extension (not yet applied):** `15` §9 could carry the §6 peak as a second output once a facility discloses realized capital per GW and energized capacity together. Until then `15` is unchanged and this file holds the composition.
3. `01A` adjudication: a closed multi-year funding gap at a contract-backed facility would be a financing-family observation, recorded as one family among several — never as a standalone S4 confirmation, and never netted with `14` or `16`.
