AI Buyers Are Repricing the Stack

A 2026–2036 framework for who gets advanced logic, HBM, packaging, power — and what is left for everyone else.

The core question is no longer which accelerator is fastest. It is which system stacks can secure scarce capacity, turn it into production workloads, and keep the economics working as supply expands, algorithms improve, and alternative stacks mature.

01

System stack, not a chip war

The competitive unit is advanced logic + HBM + packaging + interconnect + runtime + rack + power + capital + workload — not the accelerator alone.

02

More supply can still mean less for everyone else

Non-AI Residual Capacity asks what phones, PCs, vehicles, and industrial systems can actually obtain after priority demand is allocated.

03

Bottlenecks move — and can reverse

HBM, packaging, networking, power, and capital can become binding in sequence; a shortage at one layer can later flip into headroom or oversupply.

04

The thesis has to survive substitution

Hyperscaler custom stacks, China’s parallel stack, supply expansion, algorithmic efficiency, and capital clearing are explicit paths that can weaken the baseline.
